Learning-based operational deflection shape extraction and magnification via latent-space spectral processing

Authors: Mario Visconte, Francesco Cosco, Deborah Danieli, Davide Mastrodicasa, Emilio Di Lorenzo, Domenico Mundo

Institution: University of Calabria

Published: 2026-11-04

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Machine Learning

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Phase-based Video Motion Processing, a video motion magnification (VMM) method, is an image-based technique that amplifies small motion. This approach has been integrated into Operational Deflection Shapes (ODS) analyses of vibrating structures, producing accurate visualisations that are robust to noise. However, despite advances in computing power, in some cases, this method still suffers from artefacts related to amplification and long processing times when dealing with numerous and high-resolution frames. In this paper, we propose a novel approach that adapts an existing Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), originally trained for RGB-video motion magnification, to the ODS characterisation workflow, in order to inherit its advantages of faster computation, lower noise sensitivity, and reduced artefacts. While the original network architecture captures and directly amplifies motion between every frame and a fixed reference, producing a new video where the objects move or deform within a prescribed, amplified dynamic bandwidth, our technique directly extracts the animations of the ODS at the frequencies of interest. We validated our approach in a series of case studies, performing both qualitative and quantitative analyses to evaluate the clarity of the results, the presence of artefacts, the physical accuracy, and other characteristics, and conducting a parametric study on how varying the magnification factor influences image quality and the production of artefacts. Finally, we compared processing times with those of conventional pipelines, demonstrating that our learning-based method achieves a significant advantage.

Distributed fiber-optic temperature measurement for monitoring microwave drying processes

Authors: Martin Ganß, Ralf Wagner, Vetrivel Govindarasu, Alexander Ulanov, Albrecht Schmidt, Anne Tretau

Published: 2026-11-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

High resolution, spatially distributed temperature measurements are essential for understanding and controlling thermally driven industrial processes characterized by strong gradients and transient conditions. This study presents an in situ temperature measurement approach based on distributed fiber optic sensing for monitoring microwave assisted drying processes. The measurement system was applied to the drying of paving clinker clay samples, serving as a representative case study for heterogeneous porous materials exposed to microwave heating.Distributed fiber optic sensors with high spatial resolution were embedded to continuously capture temperature profiles throughout the samples during intermittent microwave power input. The measurements enabled detailed analysis of temperature distribution, drying kinetics, and their correlation with shrinkage behavior and crack formation. Experiments were conducted at different mean microwave power levels ranging from 11 W to 61 W.The results demonstrate that increasing power input accelerates moisture removal but leads to pronounced temperature gradients, which are directly associated with shrinkage induced cracking and reduced final density. The distributed temperature measurements allowed identification of critical power regimes and revealed that mean power levels between 34 W and 40 W provide a balanced thermal profile, enabling rapid and crack free drying for the investigated samples.The study highlights the capability of distributed fiber optic temperature sensing to resolve spatial and temporal thermal phenomena in microwave assisted processes. By providing real time, high resolution temperature data, the proposed measurement approach supports process optimization and defect prevention, demonstrating its potential for application in advanced process monitoring and control of thermally sensitive materials.

Evaluation of dwell time influence in Brinell, Vickers and Knoop hardness measurements of metallic materials

Authors: Andrea Prato, Cihan Kuzu, Febo Menelao, Claudio Origlia, Alessandro Germak

Institution: Istituto Nazionale di Ricerca Metrologica, Türkiye Bilimsel ve Teknolojik Araştırma Kurumu, Tübitak National Metrology Institute, Physikalisch-Technische Bundesanstalt

Published: 2026-11-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This work investigates the influence of test force duration, or dwell time, on Brinell, Vickers and Knoop hardness measurements of metallic materials. The experimental campaign includes 50 hardness configurations, combining different hardness scales, force levels, indentation geometries, materials and nominal hardness levels. Measurements are mainly performed at INRiM, with additional independent supporting datasets provided by PTB and TUBITAK UME. For each configuration, hardness values are evaluated at six dwell times, from 5 s to 50 s, giving a total of 300 indentations. The dependence of hardness on dwell time is modelled as a linear function of ln( t ). Sensitivity coefficients are estimated by weighted least-squares regression, using hardness uncertainties that include indentation measurement uncertainty and material inhomogeneity. The statistical significance level of each coefficient is assessed by a one-sided t -test. Results show that dwell time is a measurable influence factor in indentation hardness metrology, but its observability depends on the balance between the physical dwell-time effect and the dispersion caused by specimen inhomogeneity. Therefore, a higher p -value does not necessarily imply that dwell time is negligible. In several cases, physically consistent negative coefficients are obtained but with large uncertainties. The clearest trends are observed for macro-Vickers HV 30 and selected Brinell configurations, whereas low-force Vickers and Knoop measurements are more strongly affected by local heterogeneity and optical measurement uncertainty. The proposed approach supports the use of scale-specific sensitivity coefficients together with their uncertainties, rather than relying only on statistical significance, for dwell-time correction and uncertainty propagation in Brinell, Vickers and Knoop hardness metrology.

Experimental investigation on cyclic lateral response of masonry-infilled reinforced concrete frames strengthened with cold-formed lightweight external steel exoskeleton

Authors: Simone Galano, Andrea Rossi, Enrico Tubaldi

Institution: University of Modena and Reggio Emilia, University of Strathclyde, Azienda Unita' Sanitaria Locale Di Modena

Published: 2026-11-04

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

The seismic performance of masonry-infilled reinforced concrete (RC) frames is strongly governed by infill–frame interaction, which enhances stiffness and strength but often limits deformation capacity and promotes brittle failure. This study investigates the effectiveness of an innovative steel exoskeleton system for improving the lateral response of existing non-ductile RC frames. An experimental campaign was conducted on five full-scale, single-story, one-bay RC frames representative of gravity-load-designed buildings. The test matrix included a bare frame, two unstrengthened infilled frames with slender and thick masonry panels, and their corresponding strengthened configurations. All specimens were subjected to constant axial load and quasi-static cyclic lateral loading up to ultimate conditions. The experimental program enabled detailed assessment of both global and local behavior, including the isolated lateral contribution of the infill and strengthening system, beam–column joint deformation, infill–frame interaction, and deformation mechanisms of critical exoskeleton components. Results show that masonry infill markedly increases initial stiffness and peak strength, with thicker panels providing higher capacity but exhibiting more brittle post-peak behavior. The strengthening system substantially improved both strength and deformation capacity, increasing peak lateral resistance by up to 90% for slender infills and 40% for thick infills, while displacement capacity reached levels comparable to the bare frame. Energy dissipation was significantly enhanced, with equivalent viscous damping ratios exceeding 10% at large drifts, and damage in both infill and RC elements was delayed and reduced. The exoskeleton response was governed by cyclic buckling and yielding of the diagonal braces, whereas primary steel members remained below yielding, confirming the system’s robustness and reparability. Leveraging the experimental evidence, a practical design strategy was developed to estimate the strength and stiffness contribution of the exoskeleton based on its configuration. By demonstrating improved load-carrying capacity, enhanced energy dissipation, and effective damage control, while also providing a basis for design, this study establishes a solid foundation for the adoption of the proposed strengthening system in the seismic retrofitting of deficient masonry-infilled RC frames.

Rocks at High Pressure and Temperature: Bridging Index and Engineering Properties Using Physics-Guided Machine Learning

Authors: Gyeol Han, H. Huang, J. C. Santamarina

Institution: Georgia Institute of Technology, China University of Petroleum, Beijing

Published: 2026-11-04

Journal: Rock Mechanics and Rock Engineering

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Abstract Heat recovery from superhot rock is a transformative, high-density energy source with strong baseload potential. However, in situ geomechanical characterization remains impractical, while conventional sampling and laboratory testing are intrusive, costly, and alter material properties. We compile a curated database of 1711 specimens spanning eight lithologies tested under high-pressure, high-temperature conditions to examine the use of index properties recoverable from drill cuttings—rock type, porosity, and mineralogy—to predict stiffness, strength, and brittle–ductile response at in situ stress and temperature. Physics-guided machine learning (PGML) embeds semi-empirical baseline equations as physical constraints while extracting high-dimensional patterns to derive explicit multiplicative formulations. This structure preserves phenomenological consistency beyond the calibration range and enables systematic refinement as new data emerge. The model predicts stiffness and strength with a normalized mean absolute error NMAE~0.3, and the brittle-to-ductile transition confining stress with 85% accuracy, while limiting the critical misclassification of ductile failure as brittle behavior to ~3%. Stiffness reflects formation history, with lithology, mineralogy, and porosity as key descriptors; in contrast, strength depends strongly on the current stress state. Some lithologies require additional microstructural descriptors beyond mineralogy, yet PGML predictions capture the governing microphysical processes encoded in the inputs. These results enable real-time drilling optimization and inform thermo-mechanical stimulation design.
