A fully multivariate multifractal detrended fluctuation analysis method for fault diagnosis

Authors: Khuram Naveed, Naveed ur Rehman

Published: 2026-09-04

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Analytical / Theoretical

Citations: 0

Abstract:

We propose a fully multivariate generalization of multifractal detrended fluctuation analysis (MFDFA) and leverage it to develop a fault diagnosis framework for multichannel machine vibration data. We introduce a novel covariance-weighted $L_{pq}$ matrix norm based on Mahalanobis distance to define a fully multivariate fluctuation function that uniquely captures cross-channel dependencies and variance biases in multichannel vibration data. This formulation, termed FM-MFDFA, allows for a more accurate characterization of the multiscale structure of multivariate signals. To enhance feature relevance, the proposed framework integrates multivariate variational mode decomposition (MVMD) to isolate fault-relevant components before applying FM-MFDFA. Results on wind turbine gearbox data demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms conventional MFDFA approaches by effectively distinguishing between healthy and faulty machine states, even under noisy conditions.

Mechanics-informed unsupervised bondline damage detection in CFRP–steel joints using FBG-based spatial strain gradients

Authors: Sina Mansourdehghan, Farhad Aslani, Farid Fazel Mojtahedi, Sriram Ravisankar Padma, Du Huynh, Peter Butt

Institution: The University of Western Australia, Government of Western Australia Department of Health

Published: 2026-09-02

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

• Unsupervised FBG-based bondline damage detection in CFRP–steel joints. • Spatial Gradient Strain improves defect sensitivity vs absolute strain fields. • VAE and GANomaly achieve ∼ 0.99 AUC using healthy-state training only. • Feature representation more critical than deep model complexity for SHM. • C-GANomaly enables near real-time, high-accuracy structural monitoring. Bondline integrity between carbon fibre-reinforced polymer (CFRP) laminates and steel substrates is critical to the performance of composite-strengthened infrastructure, yet early interfacial defects such as kissing bonds remain difficult to detect because they produce only subtle strain perturbations under service-level loading. This study presents an unsupervised, data-driven framework for bondline damage detection using two parallel embedded Fibre Bragg Grating (FBG) arrays with eight sensing locations, requiring only healthy-state data for model development. Two CNN-based deep generative models, namely a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) and GANomaly, were trained using healthy measurements from an instrumented CFRP-strengthened steel coupon under multiple service-level loading conditions and evaluated through four-point bending tests on healthy and defective specimens. Manufacturing consistency was independently verified using three additional healthy specimens. A novel Spatial Gradient Strain (SGS) representation is proposed and benchmarked against the conventional Absolute Strain Field (ASF). By emphasising local strain discontinuities while suppressing global load-scaling effects, SGS provides a mechanics-informed, load-invariant representation of bondline behaviour. Both deep generative models achieved AUC-ROC values of approximately 0.99 using SGS, compared with only 0.70–0.81 using ASF, demonstrating that feature representation has a greater influence on detection performance than network architecture. The C-GANomaly (SGS) model achieved the best performance, with an F1-score and recall of 0.99 while maintaining near-real-time inference capability. Benchmarking against conventional one-class learning and multivariate statistical process control methods confirmed the superiority of the proposed framework, with PCA representing the strongest conventional baseline (F1-score = 0.89). Deep ensemble uncertainty analysis further showed well-calibrated confidence estimates (ECE = 0.011), supporting confidence-aware bondline monitoring. The proposed framework demonstrates proof-of-concept feasibility for asset-specific, physically interpretable, uncertainty-aware bondline monitoring under controlled laboratory conditions.

New insights into the use of particle-size sensors for asphalt mixture gyratory and roller compaction

Authors: Omar Guazzaroni, Lorenzo Paolo Ingrassia, Amedeo Virgili, Andrea Graziani, Francesco Canestrari

Institution: Marche Polytechnic University

Published: 2026-09-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Compaction is a critical phase in pavement construction, directly influencing early performance and long‑term durability. While field procedures aim to achieve target density levels, laboratory methods still struggle to fully replicate field compaction mechanisms. Although particle‑size sensors have recently been standardized for gyratory compaction through ASTM D8541 , the reliability of the associated workability parameters remains uncertain due to machine‑specific mold kinematics. The first objective of this study is therefore to assess the consistency and interpretability of ASTM D8541 workability parameters. A second objective is to explore the feasibility of applying particle‑size sensors in roller compaction, where no established framework currently exists. To address these aims, two gyratory compactors, one roller compactor, three asphalt mixtures, an unbound granular material, and two sensor geometries were investigated to characterize particle motion under laboratory compaction. Results show that ASTM D8541 parameters are affected by rigid mold movement, leading to inconsistent outcomes across gyratory machines and indicating the need for compactor‑specific calibration. A new parameter, M‑S RRC, is introduced to quantify the relative motion between mold and sensor, providing an improved and locally measured indicator of compaction effort; in gyratory compaction, it exhibits a favourable indicative trend with the Compaction Energy Index (CEI). Finally, roller compaction tests demonstrate that spherical sensors are more sensitive than cubic ones to variations in mixture workability, supporting their preferential use for detecting subtle differences in compaction behavior.

Flexural behaviour of glulam panels under sequential fire and blast loading

Authors: Abla Krouma, Ghasan Doudak

Institution: University of Ottawa

Published: 2026-09-03

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This study presents an experimental investigation of glued-laminated timber (GLT) panels subjected to a real fire followed by far-field blast-type loading. Seven GLT panels extracted from the Mass Timber Demonstration Fire Test Program were exposed to one-sided fire until self-extinguishment and then tested under four-point bending with simply supported boundary conditions, statically as well as dynamically using the University of Ottawa Shock Tube testing facility. Failure modes in charred panels were characterized by flexural failure, consistent with uncharred panels' failure modes, with some charred panels exhibiting visible glue line separation. Static and dynamic resistance curves were characterized as linear elastic up to maximum resistance in panels. The average strength increase factor (SIF) obtained for the charred panels was 1.8. No statistically significant dynamic strength increase in flexural strength or stiffness was observed. The findings demonstrated that a single-degree-of-freedom model, used together with the Reduced Cross-Section Method, can be a reliable predictive tool with proper inputs to simulate the dynamic response of structural elements for fire-followed-by-blast loading scenario; a methodology which can be extended to other timber structural elements.
