A phenomenological model for the nonlinear vibration behavior of PBF-LB/M structures with unfused particles

Authors: Sebastian Tatzko, Mirco Jonkeren, Marcus Oel

Published: 2026-11-25

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Influencing vibrations by unfused powder in additive manufacturing has gained interest due to a high potential for vibration reduction. This form of particle damping can be realized by PBF-LB/M (Powder Bed Fusion - Laser Beam/Metallic) within the manufacturing process. However, there are still open questions related to optimal cavity placement inside a structure as well as to modeling and simulation. The numerous particles of the fine manufacturing powder prohibit discrete element approaches with a resolution corresponding to the particle size. In this work, the effects of particle damping are approximated by means of a nonlinear oscillator with phenomenological modeling of the fundamental nonlinear effects. First of all, six beam test specimens with different cavities are designed and manufactured. The beams are then fixed to a shaker table and exposed to base excitation. From sweep measurements, the main characteristics are investigated and suitable nonlinear force models are derived. The aim is to ensure that the forces are physically meaningful and therefore interpretable. This way, the involved nonlinear mechanisms can be explained from identified parameter values. The beam dynamics are approximated by a linear two DOF system which is reduced to the second mode for each beam. The modal oscillator is expressed in the physical coordinate of the attached sensor for direct comparison of measurements and simulations. Nonlinear vibration response of the model is obtained by applying the Harmonic Balance Method (HBM). In an iterative process, the model parameters are identified for each specimen and the frequency response functions are investigated. The phenomenological approach is able to capture the different characteristics and helps to explain how friction and inertia mechanisms interact in particle dampers. The approach proposed in this work provides a preliminary step toward system identification and may serve as preparation for model development. It further helps to gain insight in the nonlinear effects involved in particle damping and how they depend on cavity size and location. The model is not intended as a design tool with transferable parameters but as an interpretation tool that helps to attribute measured effects to their physical causes in closed powder cavities, which cannot be inspected during operation.

Integrated Hydrogeological Assessment of Underground Constructions in Swelling-Prone Keuper Lithologies: Insights from the Markusberg Tunnel (Luxembourg)

Authors: Jonas Dickmann, Romain Meyer, Thomas R. Rüde, Florian Amann

Institution: RWTH Aachen University, Greenland Government, Fraunhofer Research Institution for Energy Infrastructures and Geotechnologies IEG

Published: 2026-11-24

Journal: Rock Mechanics and Rock Engineering

Method: Review / Survey

Citations: 0

Abstract:

The construction and long-term stability of underground structures in swelling-prone lithologies pose geotechnical challenges, particularly in clay–sulfate Keuper formations. This study presents an integrated, multi-scale hydrogeological assessment of the Markusberg Tunnel (Luxembourg), a twin-tube highway tunnel affected by severe syn- and post-construction deformation and heave in clay–sulfate-rich Keuper lithology. The investigation combines hydrogeological, geological, construction-related, and geomorphological analyses to identify the mechanisms controlling swelling and associated heave. Hydrochemical analyses, drainage discharge monitoring, and mixing calculations reveal the intrusion of multiple groundwater types, including meteoric and highly saline artesian waters, into the tunnel near-field and associated excavation damage zone (EDZ), potentially contributing to ongoing heave through clay swelling, sulfate dissolution, and gypsum precipitation. Structural-geological mapping, LiDAR-based geomorphological analyses, and field surveys identified landslides near the tunnel alignment that may act as preferential infiltration pathways into low-permeability Keuper formations. The results suggest that tunnel excavation may have established a hydraulic short circuit between weathered and unweathered sulfate rocks, promoting sustained expansive reactions, local karstification, and long-term deformation. Persistent swelling points to an open hydrogeological system that enables continuous groundwater circulation and water–rock interaction in the tunnel surroundings. These findings underline the importance of multidisciplinary, multi-scale site investigations to resolve system behavior and develop effective monitoring and mitigation strategies for underground construction in swelling-prone formations.
