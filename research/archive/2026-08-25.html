Washer-shaped piezoresistive nanocomposite sensor for detecting tightness loss in bolted joints

Authors: Eduardo Preto, Jose Malmonge, Gaël Chevallier, Samuel da Silva

Published: 2026-08-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

A novel method for detecting bolt tightness loss is introduced, utilizing real-time measurements from piezoresistive nanocomposite sensors. Piezoresistive sensors alter their electrical resistance in response to applied mechanical force. The sensor consists of natural rubber embedded with carbon nanowires and carbon black, which together facilitate electrical conductivity within the rubber matrix. When subjected to compressive or tensile loading, the composite exhibits a piezoresistive response, as the distribution of conductive nanomaterials shifts. Electrical characterization, including current and voltage measurements, was conducted to confirm and quantify this behavior. A washer-shaped sensor was fabricated and implemented to monitor bolted joints under two configurations: bolts arranged in a single direction and in two directions. Compressive deformation at the bolt interface resulted in measurable changes in the sensor’s electrical response as the applied torque varied. In both configurations, the sensor consistently detected torque variations, including in bolts that were not directly loosened, indicating its ability to monitor entire joints without instrumenting every bolt. Dynamic testing, involving variable loads and vibrations, demonstrated that the sensor did not significantly influence the structure’s dynamic properties, except when bolts were extremely loose. This approach reduces the cost and complexity of sensing, data acquisition, processing, and diagnosis, while offering greater sensitivity to torque variations than conventional techniques.
