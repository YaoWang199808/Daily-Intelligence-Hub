Data-driven modeling of synthetic wind speed signals with adaptive temporal resolution

Authors: Parth Tambat, Marius Bittner, Marco Behrendt, Michael Beer

Institution: Leibniz University Hannover, Rice University, Tongji University, University of Liverpool

Published: 2026-07-25

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

As wind energy systems continue to expand in size and complexity, ensuring their structural reliability under stochastic wind loading has become increasingly critical. Accurate wind speed modeling across multiple temporal scales is essential for reliability assessment, extreme load estimation, and risk-based design. However, a central challenge in data-driven wind speed and storm hazard analysis is the limited temporal resolution of available wind measurements. In practice, wind speed data are often provided as 10 min or hourly averages. While such coarse discretizations are sufficient for estimating aggregate power production, they fail to capture the high-frequency variability that governs extreme loading, structural response, and short-term system dynamics. This paper introduces a novel signal-processing based framework for synthesizing high-resolution wind speed time series from coarse measurements while maintaining statistical and spectral consistency. The methodology operates in the frequency domain, where spectral components are extrapolated and modeled to reflect physically plausible high-frequency behavior while also enforcing a strict energy conservation and preserving extreme-value characteristics. The resulting synthetic wind speed exhibits essential statistical characteristics, including mean values, variance, maximum amplitudes, and distributional properties, this enables high-fidelity stochastic simulations at resolutions suitable for advanced engineering and environmental analyses. The results indicate that the proposed methodology is capable of generating high-resolution, data-informed wind speed realizations with consistent statistical and spectral behavior. The framework provides a robust and computationally efficient tool for wind modeling.

Reliability-based seismic performance assessment of single-pier hybrid coupled walls with hinged base and corner components

Authors: Fabrizio Scozzese, Alessandro Zona, Graziano Leoni, Andrea Dall’Asta

Institution: Università di Camerino, Istituto Nazionale di Geofisica e Vulcanologia

Published: 2026-07-24

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Numerical Simulation

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This study concerns single-pier hybrid coupled walls (SP-HCWs), a recently proposed alternative to conventional hybrid coupled walls (HCWs), the latter consisting of two reinforced concrete (RC) walls coupled by steel links. In SP-HCWs, a single RC wall is coupled to two steel side columns through steel links. This configuration simplifies the RC wall response under horizontal actions and reduces the amount of concrete in favour of steel, enabling faster erection, easier post-earthquake repair, and improved disassembly at the end of service life. To further enhance seismic performance, an alternative base detail for the RC wall was introduced, i.e., a hinged wall–foundation connection combined with vertical steel corner components. Previous numerical investigations based on nonlinear static (pushover) analyses highlighted the potential of SP-HCWs proportioned according to a specifically developed design approach, for both cases of fixed and hinged bases. The present study advances the assessment of SP-HCWs and their design method by moving from static to nonlinear time-history analyses, explicitly accounting for uncertainties in the seismic input. The resulting response dataset is exploited to perform seismic reliability analyses to evaluate the ability of the proposed design method to maintain the probability of failure under satisfactory levels as well as to derive behaviour (q) factors. The outcomes provide a deeper understanding of the seismic performance of SP-HCWs, fostering possible future real-world applications.
