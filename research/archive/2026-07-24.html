Time-optimal motion trajectory generation incorporating an input shaper for overhead cranes operated by industrial servo systems

Authors: Minetaka Maeda, Y. Matsuyama, Yasushi MURAGISHI, Naoki Uchiyama

Institution: Toyohashi University of Technology, Shinko Electric Industries (Japan)

Published: 2026-07-22

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This paper proposes a motion trajectory generation method that achieves time-optimality under input shaping constraints for overhead cranes operated by industrial servo systems. The proposed approach formulates a constrained trajectory optimization problem that minimizes motion time while suppressing residual vibration by embedding a zero-vibration (ZV) or zero-vibration-and-derivative (ZVD) input shaper into the trajectory generation process. By using a simplified model that requires only the natural frequency and damping ratio to characterize vibration, this approach enables practical implementation even when full system identification is difficult. The effectiveness and robustness of the proposed method are demonstrated through simulations and experiments using an overhead crane apparatus. The results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves shorter motion times with effective vibration suppression compared to conventional input shaping and maintains robustness against modeling errors in natural frequency and damping ratio.

Hybrid approach for metamaterial behavior prediction for non-uniform resonator grids

Authors: Sara Tincani, Nimish Pandiya, Christian Dindorf, Claus Claeys, Elke Deckers

Institution: Robert Bosch (Taiwan), KU Leuven

Published: 2026-07-21

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

A novel methodology to predict the behavior of metamaterial solutions is presented, with application to a simplified academic structure and an automotive electrical component. In scenarios where a non-uniform grid of resonators is employed, it is essential to consider an alternative to the unit cell modeling strategy, which is typically applied, to ensure robust predictions of metamaterial behavior. In this work, a simulation model of the resonators is combined with experimental transfer functions of the host structure through frequency-based substructuring to obtain these predictions. By employing the recently introduced longest vector approach, the necessity for a substantial number of driving points corresponding to the locations of the resonators, is avoided. Additionally, a metric is proposed to compare various configurations, demonstrating the potential of the presented technique for precise optimizations. The predictions obtained from experimental measurements are compared with those from unit cell modeling. The substructuring methodology offers a robust framework for predicting metamaterial performance in cases where a uniform grid of resonators cannot be placed, or when accurate simulation models of the complex host structure are not readily available.

Geometry-driven sensitivity mapping of viscoelastic tactile sensors using a two-stage design of experiments framework

Authors: Shahba Tasmiya Mouna, Md Jarir Hossain, Chaehyeon Kim, Richard Einsporn, In-Hwan Lee, Jae‐Won Choi

Institution: University of Akron, Chungbuk National University

Published: 2026-07-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Viscoelastic tactile sensors based on ionic-liquid (IL) and polymer composites are increasingly used in different applications. Although versatile, their strain sensitivity strongly depends on geometric parameters, making application-specific tuning challenging. . Existing studies on IL-based solid-state sensors often rely on empirical or one-factor-at-a-time approaches, which fail to capture interaction effects or nonlinear behavior inherent to such systems. To address this limitation, this study implements a two-stage Design of Experiments (DOE) framework to investigate geometry-driven sensitivity in viscoelastic solid-state IL-based stretchable sensors. In Stage-1, screening was conducted using five geometric variables to evaluate their individual contributions to sensitivity for both normal-force and shear-force sensors. The analysis established an order of dominance among the factors and revealed that interaction effects contribute to sensitivity beyond individual factor effects. Motivated by these findings, Stage-2 employed Response Surface Methodology (RSM), using the same five geometric factors to extend the first-order screening model and evaluate dominant curvature and interaction trends. Results show that IL layer thickness and electrode width are the primary sensitivity-control parameter for both normal and shear sensors. Electrode length has minimal influence, whereas electrode thickness and support-layer thickness have secondary effects and should be optimized after the dominant factors are fixed. Notably, the two sensing modes differ in interaction structure: for normal sensors, a single dominant nonlinear parameter (IL-layer thickness) governs the response, whereas for shear sensors two-factor geometric interactions led by electrode width, persist after quadratic modeling, identifying geometric coupling as a distinguishing feature of shear sensing. Together, the sequential DOE approach provides a structured framework for characterizing sensitivity behavior in sensors, enabling geometry-guided design decisions. This methodology supports the development of application-specific tactile sensors by revealing complex multivariable relationships that influence sensor performance.

Capacitance-based detection of tissue freezing for real-time cryotherapy monitoring

Authors: Noelia Vaquero-Gallardo, Nicolau Solà-Coves, Oliver Millán-Blasco, Herminio Martínez García

Institution: Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya

Published: 2026-07-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Cryotherapy is a non-invasive technique used to reduce subcutaneous tissue with controlled cooling. However, unintended freezing can cause cell damage due to intracellular ice formation. Conventional monitoring methods, based primarily on temperature detection, identify freezing indirectly through exothermic changes, but they have limited responsiveness and spatial resolution. Previous studies have explored temperature and bioimpedance sensing; however, both approaches have inherent limitations: temperature measurements are highly localized and slow to respond, while bioimpedance typically reflects structural changes only after ice formation has progressed. In contrast, this work introduces capacitive sensing as a novel and potentially more efficient approach for detecting freezing events, as it captures the early dielectric shifts associated with the onset of freezing, enabling earlier detection. The proposed method demonstrated high performance: 93.0% accuracy, 97.0% precision, and 93.0% recall, with narrow confidence intervals and a statistically significant p-value (< 0.0001). These results outperform temperature detection and show comparable performance to bioimpedance detection, with a slight numerical improvement under the tested experimental conditions. These results suggest that capacitance sensing could complement temperature and bioimpedance monitoring in future multimodal cryotherapy systems. However, formal sensor-fusion strategies combining capacitance and bioimpedance, including machine-learning classifiers or combined decision rules, were not evaluated in the present study and remain a subject for future research. Capacitance contributes earlier and less spatially localized detection due to its sensitivity to initial dielectric shifts, bioimpedance adds information about subsequent structural changes, and temperature provides confirmation of thermal dynamics. A multimodal sensing scheme demonstrably enhances both temporal and spatial resolution, facilitating prompt and reliable tissue-freezing detection and reducing the risk of delayed damage recognition. Overall, the proposed strategy provides a robust and responsive tool for monitoring freezing during cryotherapy procedures, with potential benefits for improving treatment safety and therapeutic outcomes.

Experimental and numerical full-scale investigation of a large triangular laminated insulating glass unit in bending under quasi-static loading

Authors: Janne Heiskari, Federica Mancini, Pauli Lehto, Aleksi Laakso, Jani Romanoff, Ari Niemelä

Institution: Meyer Turku (Finland), Lappeenranta-Lahti University of Technology, Aalto University

Published: 2026-07-22

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Large laminated insulating glass units (IGUs) with thin glass panes are increasingly used in lightweight structures, while experimental data under high uniform loads remain limited. This paper presents a case study on full-scale tests and nonlinear Finite Element (FE) simulations of large triangular IGUs subjected to quasi-static hydrostatic pressure. Three IGUs with an area of 4.26 m 2 and defined boundary conditions were tested to failure. Each unit consisted of two laminated panes of 6-mm-thick fully tempered glass with 1.52-mm-thick PVB interlayers and a 15-mm-thick cavity Water pressure, cavity pressure, pane deflections, and surface strains were measured. A nonlinear FE model including geometric nonlinearity, shear transfer, and load sharing between the panes was constructed and experimentally validated. The results show that nonlinear effects occur at relatively low load levels and become dominant with increasing deflection. At the maximum load, the average ultimate pressure was 74.5 kPa and the maximum deflection was 81 mm, corresponding to a deflection-to-thickness ratio of ∼ 6.8 based on the 12-mm total glass thickness of one laminated pane. At maximum load, the cavity pressure reached up to 45% of the applied load. The FE model predicted center deflections within ∼ 5% of the measured values and reached good overall stress agreement. The results indicate that linear approaches significantly underestimate the response of such large IGUs under increasing loads, highlighting the need for nonlinear FE analysis in similar configurations.

LES simulations of downburst-ABL wind interactions and their impact on building aerodynamic loads

Authors: Mohammad Hadavi, Djordje Romanić, Alessio Ricci

Institution: McGill University, Istituto Universitario di Studi Superiori di Pavia

Published: 2026-07-22

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Numerical Simulation

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Downbursts generate localized and non-stationary outflows that differ fundamentally from the quasi-stationary atmospheric boundary layer (ABL) winds used in design standards. However, the combined effects of downburst outflows and background ABL winds on building aerodynamics and loads remain insufficiently characterized. This study employs large-eddy simulation (LES) to investigate the interaction between a downburst-like impinging jet (DB), an ABL wind, and a mid-rise benchmark building. A two-stage validation of the ABL wind-building and isolated DB cases confirmed adequate resolution of turbulent kinetic energy and correct inertial subrange behavior, with good agreement against wind-tunnel data. Combined ABL-DB simulations revealed two dominant flow features: a near-surface accelerating wall jet and an overlying velocity-deficit layer. Above the wall jet, the strong downdraft blocks the background ABL flow from penetrating the high-momentum jet core, producing a marked velocity deficit aloft. The ABL wind breaks the symmetry of the outflow, deflecting the wall jet downstream, shifting stagnation, intensifying near-ground velocities, and elongating the wake. At lower elevations, the peak ABL-DB velocity is approximately 33% higher than the mean ABL-DB value (plateau), highlighting the severe impact of downburst pulse on near-ground wind loading and wind–structure interactions. The building further modifies this compound flow through aerodynamic effects such as edge separation, roof-corner vortices, and downstream recirculation. An energy-based metric, the available dynamic pressure ratio (ADPR), showed strong near-ground energy concentration, with values substantially higher than ABL wind baseline. Surface pressure and integrated force analyses demonstrated that peak lateral loads under ABL-DB wind can exceed windward streamwise loads by up to 1.5 times. The results show that transient ABL-DB interactions fundamentally alter the building loading hierarchy, with lateral facades often governing, and must be explicitly considered in performance-based wind design.
