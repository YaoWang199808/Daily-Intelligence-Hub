Simulating human-building interactions in adaptive environments: Narrative-based coordination of human and spatial agents

Authors: Davide Schaumann

Institution: Technion – Israel Institute of Technology

Published: 2026-11-07

Journal: Automation in Construction

Method: Review / Survey

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Multi-agent simulation supports occupant behavior analyses in static buildings, but rarely extends to dynamically reconfigurable environments, preventing pre-deployment evaluation of human-building interactions. This paper introduces a narrative-based framework modeling occupants and adaptive architectural systems as agents, positioning buildings as active participants rather than passive containers. A narrative formalism coordinates human and spatial agents, a visual scripting interface enables non-experts to author interaction scenarios, and modular components aim to foster usability and transfer across spatial contexts and interaction types. An exploratory, early-stage evaluation with novice architects in an academic setting modeled eight scenarios spanning multiple spatial scales and four adaptive component types using a shared component library, indicating the framework supports simulations across spatial contexts and adaptation types. Survey responses ( n = 25) suggest participants perceived it as valuable, transferable, and supportive of complex interaction scenarios. An exploratory between-cohort comparison links reduced visual scripting node counts to improved perceived template support.

Estimating diffusive body-wave properties in bounded concrete members using an energy-contribution-based image-source method

Authors: Hao Cheng, Katrin Löer, Max A.N. Hendriks, Yuguang Yang

Institution: Delft University of Technology, Norwegian University of Science and Technology

Published: 2026-11-07

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Reliable characterization of diffusive body-wave properties in concrete is essential for damage assessment and diffuse-wave-based imaging. However, existing approaches either neglect boundary reflections, thereby biasing the fitted properties, or rely on full analytical solutions for bounded domains, which greatly increase the complexity of curve fitting. In this study, an energy-contribution-based image-source method is proposed for concrete members with external planar concrete-air boundaries. The method retains only the image sources whose energy contributions are sufficiently large, thereby reducing model complexity while maintaining agreement with the bounded-domain solution. The method is evaluated using four concrete members with multiple pairs of ultrasonic sensors embedded. The experimental comparisons show that neglecting reflected energy underestimates both diffusivity and dissipation, with the strongest influence occurring at low frequencies. The comparisons across the four members show that retaining the dominant reflection terms achieves close agreement with the cuboid-domain benchmark, with only marginal improvement from additional reflections. These comparisons, together with the estimated reflection contributions, suggest approximately 30% as an empirical reference level for reflection screening in similar configurations. For the retained reflection configurations, the mean relative differences of diffusivity and dissipation remain below 2% for all four members when compared with the cuboid-medium reference solution, while the computational cost is much lower than that of the full analytical formulation. Across all members, diffusivity decreases with increasing frequency, and dissipation increases with increasing frequency. These results indicate that the proposed method provides a benchmark-consistent and computationally efficient approach to estimate frequency-dependent diffusive properties in bounded concrete members with approximately planar free concrete-air boundaries in the monitored region.

A review of magnetic sensors for nondestructive testing

Authors: Xun Zou, Yu Li, Wuliang Yin

Institution: University of Manchester

Published: 2026-11-05

Journal: Measurement

Method: Review / Survey

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Magnetic sensors are essential components for electromagnetic nondestructive testing (NDT). In practical applications, the diverse operating principles and performance characteristics make magnetic sensor selection challenging. This review analyzes mainstream magnetic sensors used in NDT, including induction coils, Hall effect sensors, magnetoresistive sensors, giant magneto-impedance sensors, magnetoelectric composite sensors, fluxgate magnetometers, superconducting quantum interference devices, optically pumped magnetometers, and nitrogen-vacancy center sensors. Their operating principles, applications in major NDT techniques and performance characteristics are summarized, with magnetic noise discussed in relation to measurement conditions and practical defect detectability. In addition, comparisons are presented in terms of representative magnetic noise, output sensitivity, advantages, and limitations. Based on the comparisons, key considerations for sensor selection and future research directions are also discussed.
