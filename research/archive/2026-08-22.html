Dimension-reduced probability density evolution equation for spatial three-dimensional vehicle-rail-bridge partitioned system

Authors: Peng Yuan, Yi Luo

Institution: Southeast University, Leibniz University Hannover

Published: 2026-08-19

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Stochastic dynamic response analysis of three-dimensional (3D) vehicle-rail-bridge (VRB) systems is challenging due to time-varying coupling, high-dimensional uncertainties, and strongly nonstationary and non-Gaussian response characteristics. This study develops an efficient stochastic dynamic analysis framework by introducing the dimension-reduced probability density evolution equation (DR-PDEE) into a non-iterative partitioned VRB model. The vehicle, rail, and bridge subsystems are formulated as time-invariant stochastic subsystems connected through time-dependent wheel-rail and time-independent rail-bridge interfaces, thereby avoiding repeated assembly and factorization of global time-varying system matrices. Based on this deterministic backbone, the DR-PDEE is used to characterize the time-varying probability density evolution of selected displacement responses under random system parameters and stochastic track irregularities. To improve numerical robustness for highly nonstationary and non-Gaussian VRB responses, a dimension-reduced Monte Carlo solution strategy is adopted for solving the DR-PDEE and compared with the classical path-integration scheme. Numerical results are validated against Monte Carlo simulations with 10,000 realizations. The proposed framework accurately captures nonstationary and non-Gaussian response distributions using a limited number of samples, demonstrating its efficiency and potential for stochastic analysis of complex coupled railway systems.

Ensemble machine learning for multiclass welding quality classification using sensor-derived signal features

Authors: Solomon Habtamu Tessema, Dariusz Bismor

Institution: Silesian University of Technology

Published: 2026-08-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This study proposes an ensemble machine-learning framework for multiclass MAG welding joint quality classification to reduce reliance on destructive inspection. Acoustic, electrical, and vibration signals were acquired using electric microphones, ADXL386 accelerometers, and current sensors. The experimental dataset, consisting of 105 original samples, was divided at the experiment level into training (80%) and testing (20%) sets to minimize data leakage and overfitting. The signals were processed using a genetic algorithm-optimized band-pass filter, denoised, and segmented into one-second windows, resulting in 1800 data segments. Features were then extracted from the time, frequency, and time-frequency domains and refined using delta-threshold mRMR feature selection. Class imbalance was addressed using oversampling, SMOTE, SMOTE-ENN, ENN, and undersampling. A stacked ensemble with SVM and Random Forest (RF) base learners and a multinomial logistic regression meta-learner captures both local and global decision structures. With Optuna-based hyperparameter tuning and stratified cross-validation, the proposed model achieves 94.4% accuracy, a 94.4% weighted F1-score, an 87.3% macro F1-score, an 87.1% balanced accuracy, and a 98.6% weighted ROC-AUC, outperforming standalone SVM, RF, SVM+XGBoost, and XGBoost classifiers. Confusion matrices and ROC-AUC curves further demonstrate the robustness and generalization capability of the proposed approach for imbalanced multiclass welding data.

Cross-section behaviour and CSM based design of stainless steel I-sections in fire

Authors: Congding Zhang, Merih Kucukler, Zhe Xing

Institution: University of Warwick, Tianjin University

Published: 2026-08-20

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Numerical Simulation

Citations: 0

Abstract:

In this paper, a comprehensive investigation into the cross-section behaviour and design of stainless steel I-sections in fire is carried out. Shell finite element (FE) models able to mimic the cross-section response of stainless steel I-sections at elevated temperatures are developed and validated. An extensive numerical parametric study is then conducted using the validated shell FE models to comprehensively investigate the behaviour of stainless steel I-sections in fire, taking into account 12825 stainless steel I-sections and covering a very broad range of cross-section slendernesses, cross-section geometries, elevated temperature levels and stainless steel grades. Based on the results from the parametric studies, a new version of the deformation based cross-section design approach Continuous Strength Method (CSM) is proposed for the fire design of stainless steel cross-sections. The proposed new version of the CSM is assessed against (i) the stainless steel cross-section design provisions of the European structural steel fire design standard EN 1993-1-2 and (ii) the existing versions of the CSM. The results demonstrate that the proposed version of the CSM offers a very reliable approach to predict the ultimate resistances of stainless steel I-sections at elevated temperatures, leading to more consistent ultimate resistance predictions relative to EN 1993-1-2 and the existing versions of the CSM.

Development of an analytical framework for thermal design of energy walls

Authors: Marco Gerola, Aakash Gupta, Ida Shafagh, Francesco Cecinato, Fleur Loveridge

Institution: University of Milan, University of Leeds

Published: 2026-08-19

Journal: Tunnelling and Underground Space Technology

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Energy walls are conventional retaining structures integrated with heat exchangers to harness near-surface geothermal energy for space heating and cooling. Despite their growing application, limited research has focused on developing analytical formulations that streamline the thermal design of energy walls. This study presents an analytical framework that combines the infinite plane source and shape factor methods, designed to accurately predict the temperature changes within energy walls. Three novel method modifications are introduced: an equivalent length for the infinite plane source method, a new shape factor formulation, and optimising the reference surface selection. The proposed formulations are validated through two-dimensional finite element numerical models and subsequently tested using monitored data from a full-scale Cutter Soil Mix (CSM) energy wall test. The results show the predicted outlet temperature from the heat exchanger pipes differs by only 5.7% from the measured data under near steady-state conditions. These findings demonstrate the reliability and accuracy of the new analytical formulations, providing an improved fast run-time tool for advancing the thermal design of energy walls.

A Physics-Informed Neural Network for Pressurized Cavities of Arbitrary Shape in Heterogeneous Rock

Authors: Yulong Liu, Chloé Arson

Institution: Cornell University

Published: 2026-08-19

Journal: Rock Mechanics and Rock Engineering

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Pressurized cavity problems underlie a wide range of rock engineering applications, including tunneling, drilling, and in situ testing, but accurate field prediction remains challenging in heterogeneous rock masses where discontinuities are pervasive. This work proposes a Physics-Informed Neural Network to solve forward problems of pressurized cavities with arbitrary smooth shapes embedded in heterogeneous elastic media. The novelty of the approach is that a single shared network is augmented with signed-distance and level-set embeddings, and domain-conditioned activation functions allow the representation of piecewise-smooth fields across discontinuities. The physics-informed loss enforces equilibrium, constitutive relationships, traction boundary conditions, interface continuity, and sparse observation data. Ground-truth data are generated using Finite-Element simulations. The model achieves displacement mean absolute errors of $$\mathcal {O}(10^{-5})\,\textrm{m}$$ in both homogeneous and heterogeneous rock masses. In homogeneous benchmarks, the relative $$L_2$$ -norm errors for the normal stress components remain below $$3.1\times 10^{-3}$$ on average. In heterogeneous cases with intersecting discontinuities, the mean relative $$L_2$$ -norm errors for $$\sigma _{xx}$$ and $$\sigma _{yy}$$ remain below $$6.6\times 10^{-3}$$ , with discrepancies localized near interfaces. Compared with XPINN, the proposed PINN framework delivers comparable stress accuracy with smaller displacement errors near discontinuities while keeping the number of parameters nearly constant across subdomains, yielding a $$\sim 5\times$$ training speedup. Furthermore, the number of training epochs for related heterogeneous cases can be reduced by half through transfer learning.
