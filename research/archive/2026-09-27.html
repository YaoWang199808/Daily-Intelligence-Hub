Toward intelligent safety assurance in construction human-robot collaboration

Authors: Mingzhu Wang, Shijing Xu

Institution: City University of Hong Kong

Published: 2026-09-26

Journal: Automation in Construction

Method: Review / Survey

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Construction human-robot collaboration (HRC) introduces physical, ergonomic, and cognitive risks that conventional isolated-based safeguards cannot adequately manage in dynamic, unstructured worksites and requires more adaptive forms of safety assurance. Although diverse robotics-related approaches have been developed, how they function as interconnected, active safety-assurance mechanisms for construction HRC remains insufficiently synthesised. This paper systematically reviews 215 studies published from 2011 to 2025 through PRISMA-guided screening, bibliometric analysis and thematic synthesis. Five connected technological directions are identified: robot design optimisation, context-aware perception, human-centric understanding and forecasting, autonomous robotic adaptation, and safety management. The synthesis shows a transition from passive risk avoidance toward perception-driven, prediction, and adaptive safety assurance, but 54% of studies remain limited to controlled laboratory validation, and few integrate uncertainty across sensing, prediction, control and supervision. Accordingly, a closed-loop framework is proposed to guide the integration and construction-realistic validation of verifiable, uncertainty-aware HRC safety systems.

Seismic derailment-induced train–wall impact on high-speed railway bridges: A two-stage numerical and data-driven assessment framework

Authors: Jun Chen, Biao Wei, Chenxi ZHAO, Minghui Wang

Institution: Central South University

Published: 2026-09-26

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Numerical Simulation

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Earthquake-induced derailment can lead to train impacts against bridge protective walls, but the collision state is rarely derived from the preceding derailment motion. This study links a train–track–bridge seismic model to a train–reinforced concrete wall impact model to assess the resulting spatial intrusion. The framework transfers pre-contact attitude, velocity, and position and measures the maximum leading-car upper-body volume fraction beyond the wall plane within 1.0 s. For the investigated CRH2 train, China Railway Track System type III (CRTS III) slab track, and six-span bridge, 182 of 225 seismic cases enter the right-wall terminal class. A balanced database of 400 impact simulations supports comparison of six surrogate models and evaluation of prediction uncertainty. Gaussian process regression achieves a root mean square error of 0.0384 and a coefficient of determination of 0.927 on 80 independent holdout cases. Its 95% conformal prediction interval provides 97.5% empirical marginal coverage. Motion variables account for 93.6% of normalized positive permutation importance, while increasing stirrup diameter reduces the predicted exceedance ratio by about 0.069 at a fixed high-demand state. These results show that collision demand depends strongly on the inherited motion state, with wall reinforcement providing additional restraint. The framework supports preliminary comparison of protective-wall options and prioritization of detailed analyses; applications to other railway systems require independent validation and calibration.

Cyclic behavior and flexural-shear deformation mechanism of sea-sand concrete shear walls reinforced with steel-GFRP composite bars

Authors: Minsheng Guan, Jiayu Wu, Meng Sha, Wenting Liu, Jiajun Gu, Xiaohong Liu

Institution: Shenzhen University, Shenzhen Stock Exchange, Shenzhen Third People’s Hospital

Published: 2026-09-26

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Sea-sand concrete is attractive for coastal and marine engineering, but chloride salts in sea sand may induce corrosion of conventional steel bars, compromising the durability and safety of shear wall structures. Although FRP bars provide excellent corrosion resistance, their limited ductility and energy dissipation capacity restrict their seismic application. To address this issue, steel-GFRP composite bars (SGFCBs) were used as longitudinal bars in sea-sand concrete shear walls. Four SGFCB-reinforced sea-sand concrete shear wall (SFSW) specimens and one conventional steel-reinforced concrete shear wall (SSW) specimen were tested under quasi-static cyclic loading. The investigated parameters were axial compression ratio, steel–fiber ratio, and GFRP stirrup ratio in the boundary confinement region. Compared with SSW, the SFSW specimens exhibited similar flexural-shear failure, slightly lower lateral load-carrying capacity, and larger ultimate drift ratios. Cyclic response analysis showed that the SFSW specimens had higher ductility and more stable stiffness and strength degradation, but slightly lower energy dissipation capacity. Lateral deformation decomposition showed that flexural deformation contributed 72–84% of the ultimate lateral displacement when the reference shape factor 0.8 was adopted and remained dominant in the sensitivity analysis, which indicates that SGFCB bars did not change the flexure-dominated deformation mechanism. Parameter analysis showed that increasing the steel-fiber ratio improved overall cyclic performance, increasing the axial compression ratio enhanced load-carrying capacity but reduced deformation capacity, and increasing the GFRP stirrup ratio in the boundary confinement region mainly improved deformation capacity. Overall, the proposed SFSW system exhibited satisfactory cyclic behavior, providing experimental evidence for durable and ductile shear wall structures in coastal and marine environments.

A time-domain semi-analytical framework for constructing input energy spectra from design spectra and its application to the Chinese seismic code

Authors: Zhenyu Li, Xiaowei Cheng, T.Y. Yang, Hailin Sun, Yi Li

Institution: Beijing University of Technology, University of British Columbia, China Architecture Design & Research Group (China)

Published: 2026-09-26

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Constructing design spectra that accurately quantify seismic input energy is a fundamental task in energy-based seismic design. Existing time-domain empirical methods often rely on extensive or region-specific seismic data regressions, deep learning-based approaches lack physical interpretability, and frequency-domain theoretical methods face challenges in explicitly incorporating the cumulative effects of strong-motion duration ( t D ). To address these limitations, this study proposes a code-compatible, time-domain semi-analytical framework that derives elastic and plastic input energy equivalent velocity spectra ( V EI,E -spectra and V EI,P -spectra) directly from the design acceleration response spectra ( S a ) and t D. This study originally formulates harmonic time-domain steady-state solutions that decompose input energy into peak-response and time-accumulative components. Serving as the foundation, these solutions are extended to seismic scenarios via an inter-spectral mapping coefficient, C E ( t D , T g , ζ ), which links S a to input energy. Introducing a medium-to-long period attenuation index γ 1 ( t D , T g , ζ ) and categorizing t D into three types enable the proposed method to successfully establish V EI,E -spectra explicitly tailored for the Chinese Code for Seismic Design of Buildings. The V EI,E -spectra are further converted into V EI,P -spectra via regression-based conversion equations. Ground motion effects are already captured, which is why this conversion is governed solely by structural parameters—specifically the strength reduction factor ( R ), post-yield stiffness ratio ( λ ), and damping ratio ( ζ )—ultimately establishing V EI,P -spectra compatible with the Chinese code. Finally, the practicality and accuracy of the proposed V EI,E -spectra and V EI,P -spectra for the Chinese code are validated through four sets of case studies. This methodology provides a practical tool for rapidly estimating structural input energy directly from seismic codes.

From microseismic monitoring to targeted reinforcement: a data-driven simulation approach for deep tunnel stability

Authors: Tongyan Fan, Qinghe Zhang, Wenxuan Dong, Xiaorui Wang, Changrui Duan, Jingguo Li

Institution: Anhui University of Science and Technology, Huainan Mining Industry Group (China), China Railway Construction Corporation (China), China Railway Group (China)

Published: 2026-09-26

Journal: Tunnelling and Underground Space Technology

Method: Numerical Simulation

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Deep hard-rock tunnels are highly susceptible to localized rock mass damage under the coupling effects of high in-situ stress and blasting disturbances during construction. The complex spatio-temporal evolution of such damage makes real-time identification and mitigation challenging. Taking a specific deep-seated hard-rock tunnel as the engineering background, this study develops a method for characterizing energy-induced damage and analyzing dynamic support under localized damage conditions, primarily utilizing three-dimensional numerical simulations integrated with microseismic monitoring and acoustic testing data. By establishing a quantitative relationship between energy dissipation and the degradation of rock mass mechanical parameters, a dynamic update of the damage evolution process within the numerical model is achieved. Simultaneously, acoustic testing is employed to characterize the damage zoning and spatial distribution, providing a benchmark for the numerical results. Furthermore, the spatial distribution of damage, energy response patterns, and their sensitivity to variations in support parameters are analyzed. The results indicate that: (1) Rock mass damage exhibits pronounced asymmetry, with high-damage zones concentrated at the right shoulder, which plays a dominant role in energy accumulation and crack propagation. The overall damage primarily develops within a range of 5 ∼ 16 m from the tunnel wall. (2) The damage field derived from microseismic analysis agrees well with the acoustic testing results, revealing a highly damaged excavation zone extending approximately 1.2 m; (3) A rapid increase in cumulative energy and apparent volume, accompanied by a decrease in the energy index, can serve as effective precursors of accelerated damage evolution; and (4) The combined support scheme outperforms single-parameter reinforcement measures, reducing the peak damage variable from 1.0 to 0.45 and significantly shrinking the extent of the damage-affected zone.

Rockburst evolution modeling with fracture-ejection behavior by a quasi-state-based peridynamics with cluster motion algorithm and energy-based criterion

Authors: Feng Tian, Zaobao Liu, Yubo Wang, 전석원

Institution: Seoul National University, Northeastern University

Published: 2026-09-26

Journal: Tunnelling and Underground Space Technology

Method: Numerical Simulation

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Rockburst is a dynamic hazard with sudden occurrence and rapid energy release involving fracture localization and violent ejection of fragmented rock blocks during tunneling under high-stress environments. However, numerical simulations of rockbursts still lack a cluster-scale description of post-detachment rock block dynamics and an objective criterion for identifying whether the simulated motion constitutes a rockburst event. To address these gaps, a unified numerical framework integrating quasi-state-based peridynamics (QSBPD) with a motion-collision-fragment algorithm is developed for rockburst numerical modeling. This algorithm allows the post-failure rock block response, including separation, translation, rotation, collision, and secondary fragmentation, to be tracked in a unified manner, rather than being treated implicitly or omitted. Furthermore, an energy-based dual-index criterion is introduced to reduce ambiguity in event identification by distinguishing strain energy-driven ejection from gravity-dominated rock block motion and quantifying the strain-to-kinetic energy conversion. The proposed framework is evaluated using the Jinping 11·28 rockburst case. The simulation result reproduces the four-stage evolution, localized V-shaped failure zone, cluster ejection and interaction, and fragmentation observed in the documented event. The cluster velocities exhibit a clear size dependence, with smaller clusters moving faster than larger ones. Energy analysis identifies a pronounced conversion during the burst episode, with a global strain-to-kinetic energy conversion ratio of 44%. This energy signature provides a quantitative basis for identifying the simulated event as a rockburst and supports the applicability of the proposed energy-based criterion. The proposed framework provides a physically interpretable tool for process-consistent rockburst simulation and quantitative energy assessment.
