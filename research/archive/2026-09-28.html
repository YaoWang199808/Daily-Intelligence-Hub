Performance comparison of distance measurement methods with eXtended Reality head-mounted displays

Authors: Leopoldo Angrisani, Egidio De Benedetto, Luigi Duraccio, Michele Sansone, Annarita Tedesco

Institution: University of Naples Federico II, University of Bari Aldo Moro

Published: 2026-09-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This paper presents a comparative analysis of hands-based and hands-free distance measurement methods implemented through Extended Reality Head-Mounted Displays (XR HMDs). Since XR-based measurements are inherently human-centered, the operator cannot be considered merely a passive user of the measuring system, but rather an active element of the measurement process whose contribution should be explicitly accounted for. Accordingly, a methodology compliant with the Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement (GUM) is adopted to express the measurement result in terms of best estimate and measurement uncertainty, including operator-related contributions associated with intra- and inter-operator variability. The analysis is based on repeated acquisitions performed by a heterogeneous group of participants, with the aim of comparing the measurement results associated with the two considered methods under the same experimental framework. Experimental results from 30 participants using a Meta Quest 3S, with four measurands and two measurement depths, show broadly comparable best estimates between the two methods but different uncertainty budgets: the hands-based method is mainly affected by inter-operator variability, whereas the hands-free method exhibits a more pronounced intra-operator contribution. Although the experimental findings are specific to the Meta Quest 3S and to the considered measurement setup, they show that the choice of interaction modality can affect not only usability and operational constraints, but also the structure of the associated uncertainty budget. As a matter of fact, identifying the dominant uncertainty contribution can provide practical indications on where performance-improvement actions should be directed, such as operator training and procedural standardization or improvements in acquisition and tracking stability. Overall, this work contributes to the metrological assessment of XR-based distance measurement methods and provides an uncertainty-aware basis for selecting and improving alternative interaction modalities according to the target measurement uncertainty and the operational requirements of the intended application context.

Intelligent prediction under rockburst class imbalance: A coupled model fusing KMeans-SMOTE and CatBoost

Authors: Jin Qiao, Yan Zhang, Tianbin Li, ShaoJun Li, Peng Zeng

Institution: Chengdu University of Technology, State Key Laboratory of Geohazard Prevention and Geoenvironment Protection, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Institute of Rock and Soil Mechanics, State Key Laboratory of Geomechanics and Geotechnical Engineering

Published: 2026-09-27

Journal: Tunnelling and Underground Space Technology

Method: Machine Learning

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Solving the problem of data class balance is the key premise to ensure the performance of rockburst intelligent prediction model. This paper adopted an oversampling method (KMeans‑SMOTE) based on K‑means clustering and Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique (SMOTE). Firstly, this study collected 337 sets of original rockburst samples containing six key features, and obtained 300 sets of valid data after data cleaning. Then, KMeans‑SMOTE oversampling technology was applied to oversample and expand the training data evenly. Subsequently, five ensemble learning algorithms (CatBoost, XGBoost, RF, AdaBoost, LightGBM) were used to train and predict the balanced rockburst dataset. Finally, the model prediction was analyzed using multiple classification evaluation metrics, and the importance of model features was further assessed by combining the SHapley Additive exPlanations interpretability method. The results show that the combined framework of KMeans‑SMOTE and CatBoost achieves good prediction performance, with an average prediction accuracy of 82.67% and a Macro F1 score of 82.51%, outperforming the other four ensemble learning models. In the engineering application at Jiangbian Hydropower Station, the model achieves a prediction accuracy of 95% on 20 test samples, with only one misclassification. This study adopts a prediction strategy for intelligent prediction of rockburst with balanced data.

Integrated Transformer approach for immersed-tunnel settlement prediction considering spatial heterogeneity and temporal degradation of sand filling compactness

Authors: Ziyao Xu, Zhengzhong Qiu, Ailan Che, Hanxu Zhou, Ganglie Yuan

Institution: Shenzhen University, Shanghai Jiao Tong University

Published: 2026-09-27

Journal: Tunnelling and Underground Space Technology

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Settlement stability of immersed tunnels is strongly affected by the spatial heterogeneity and long-term degradation of sand filling compactness. Existing settlement prediction methods mainly rely on historical monitoring data while overlooking the time-dependent evolution of foundation compactness, resulting in insufficient prediction accuracy under heterogeneous geological and operational conditions. To overcome these limitations, this study develops an integrated Transformer-based approach that combines elastic wave detection, field monitoring data, and compactness degradation modeling for long-term settlement prediction of immersed tunnels. Elastic wave testing is first employed to characterize the two-dimensional distribution of sand filling compactness, and an empirical evolution model is established to capture the temporal degradation characteristics under different filling conditions. Subsequently, an improved IASB-Transformer model is developed to integrate settlement time-series information with time-varying compactness features, enabling effective learning of the coupled spatial–temporal deformation patterns of tunnel foundations. The results demonstrate that the proposed IASB-Transformer achieved an average coefficient of determination (R 2 ) of 0.951 across the evaluated monitoring points, outperforming the vanilla Transformer, LSTM, CNN, and RF models. At the representative monitoring point A35, incorporating the time-varying compactness sequence increased R 2 from 0.87 to 0.94, corresponding to an absolute increase of 0.07 and an approximately 8 % relative improvement in R 2 . The proposed model also exhibited lower prediction errors and more stable performance under heterogeneous filling conditions. The developed framework provides a physically interpretable approach for long-term settlement prediction and differential-settlement assessment of immersed tunnels considering the evolving condition of foundation filling materials.
