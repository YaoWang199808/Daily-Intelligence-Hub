Enabling infrastructure subsystems integration via agentic ontology engineering

Authors: Mengtian Yin, Sin Ming Wong, Tao Huang, Pieter Pauwels, Liming Xu, Jinying Xu, Ioannis Brilakis

Institution: University of Nottingham Ningbo China, University of Cambridge, Eindhoven University of Technology, National University of Singapore

Published: 2026-10-29

Journal: Automation in Construction

Method: Numerical Simulation

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Semantic harmonisation of heterogeneous infrastructure information systems is fundamental to data and system integration for infrastructure asset management (IAM). However, manual knowledge engineering over complex organisational databases is labour-intensive. This paper presents an agentic ontology engineering method that uses large language models (LLMs) within a multi-agent framework to automatically extract knowledge and construct ontologies from heterogeneous IAM databases. The system progressively generates classes, hierarchies, relations and logical restrictions, while agents align cross-system concepts and detect contradictions to ensure consistency. Evaluated on four real-world IAM schema sets, the method achieves 92.1% entity recall against a ground truth ontology, 90.91% entity classification accuracy, and 83.18% relation classification accuracy. Compared with a single-LLM baseline, the multi-agent approach yields higher quality ontologies with fewer constraint violations. The resulting IAM ontology supports interoperability across subsystems, demonstrating the potential of an agentic, LLM-driven approach to reduce modelling effort while improving ontology fidelity for infrastructure information management.

Operational joint identification using transfer path analysis and frequency-based substructuring

Authors: Michael Kreutz, Daniel Jean Rixen

Institution: Technical University of Munich

Published: 2026-10-29

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Large machines like engines or turbines are subject to operational forces that may be hard to model or measure directly. These machines are often connected to the environment via joints that can degrade over time, necessitating condition monitoring to detect and quantify changes in their dynamics. This paper presents a novel approach combining transfer path analysis (TPA) and frequency-based substructuring (FBS) to identify joint dynamics during operation. Unlike conventional TPA applications, we use the characterization of operational forces together with measured operational responses to estimate frequency response functions (FRFs) of the system with dynamic substructures. The estimated FRFs are then used in an FBS procedure to isolate the joint dynamics. The methodology is demonstrated on a magnetic bearing test rig, where both synthetic operational forces and run-up tests are conducted. Results show good agreement for synthetic force cases, while highlighting challenges with correlated excitation in run-up scenarios. The proposed method enables condition monitoring of joints in operational conditions.

Evaluation method for measuring mechanical impedance of the human hand-arm system under dynamic interaction forces

Authors: Saurbier Simon, Wolter Kai, Spengler Carina, Lukas Bunk, Jan Heinrich Robens, Krause Dieter, Matthiesen Sven

Institution: Karlsruhe Institute of Technology, Universität Hamburg, Hamburg University of Technology

Published: 2026-10-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This paper introduces a time-resolved evaluation method for determining the mechanical impedance of the human hand-arm system under time-varying force conditions within a single continuous test. The method is intended to support the investigation of dynamic hand-arm interaction conditions, while the present proof-of-concept focuses primarily on continuously varying applied grip-force profiles. Conventional impedance measurements, in contrast, are typically conducted at discrete constant force levels and require multiple separate tests at different force conditions. The proposed approach enables the measurement of mechanical impedance for continuously varying force profiles, resulting in increased test efficiency. Experiments were conducted using a test rig compliant with DIN EN ISO 10819:2022. Test subjects were exposed to multi-sine translational vibration in z-direction at frequencies between 10 and 500 Hz, in accordance with DIN EN ISO 5349-1, while performing time-varying applied grip-force profiles. First, mechanical impedance was evaluated using an established frequency-dependent impedance evaluation method for constant force conditions as a reference. In a second step, the proposed time-resolved evaluation method was applied to determine mechanical impedance under non-stationary force conditions. The results showed strong agreement between the dynamic method and the conventional approach, with a coefficient of determination of R 2 = 0.9925 over the investigated frequency range for static grip-force conditions. These findings indicate that mechanical impedance can be reliably calculated under time-varying force conditions, eliminating the need for separate measurements at each force level. This method reduces experimental effort without compromising accuracy and enables future investigations of force‑dependent, time‑varying impedance behaviour of the human hand‑arm system. Furthermore, it enables the representation of mechanical impedance as a time‑resolved curve for different frequencies and varying interaction forces.

Unsupervised Latent-Space analysis of X-ray computed tomography images for physically Informed interpretation and quality control of gas hydrate depressurization experiments

Authors: Sungil Kim, Taewoong Ahn, Minhui Lee, Jaehyoung Lee

Institution: Korea Institute of Geoscience and Mineral Resources

Published: 2026-10-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

X-ray computed tomography (CT) provides rich measurement data for gas hydrate (GH) depressurization (DP) experiments, but interpretation is hindered when reliable phase labels are unavailable during transient multiphase evolution. This study develops a measurement-informatics framework for interpreting, comparing, and quality-checking unlabeled CT image datasets from GH core DP experiments. A convolutional autoencoder was used as a self-supervised feature extractor, and the resulting latent descriptors were analyzed using t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding visualization, Mahalanobis-distance-based transition analysis, sliced Wasserstein distribution comparison, and train-referenced z mahal screening. The focus is not direct phase visualization, but extraction of image-derived measurement indicators from measured CT intensity and texture patterns. The latent descriptors organized the CT data into pressure-dependent trajectories, and tipping analysis identified a dissociation-related transition range of 16.0–16.3 MPa, consistent with theoretical equilibrium conditions and measured pressure–temperature histories. Exploratory paired-core comparisons suggested that differences in experimental conditions may be reflected in centroid-level and distribution-level latent variability; however, the limited number of paired experiments precludes establishing general relationships with DP rate or initial GH saturation. The train-referenced z mahal criterion flagged atypical slices for expert inspection, including an inconsistent slice embedded in an otherwise coherent scan sequence. These results demonstrate that unlabeled CT measurement data can be reduced, contextualized, and quality-checked using image-derived descriptors, supporting more reliable interpretation of GH DP experiments.
