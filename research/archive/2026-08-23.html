Deep learning-based structural element extraction and knowledge-graph-based validation of 2D bridge drawings

Authors: Hakan Bayer, Phillip Schönfelder, Markus König

Institution: Ruhr University Bochum

Published: 2026-08-22

Journal: Automation in Construction

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

The maintenance and management of existing bridge infrastructure rely on conventional two-dimensional construction drawings, which remain largely unstructured and difficult to integrate into digital workflows, including Building Information Modeling (BIM). In prestressed concrete bridges, tendons and anchorage systems are documented across multiple drawings and sectional views, making manual interpretation time-consuming and error-prone. This paper presents an automated pipeline for detecting and semantically structuring structural elements in 2D bridge drawings. The approach combines deep learning-based object detection, classical computer vision for identifier-frame detection, optical character recognition, and spatial matching to identify elements, recognize identifiers, and link elements with their labels. The extracted information is integrated into an ontology-based knowledge graph, enabling SPARQL-based validation of missing identifiers, cross-view relationships, and section-level quantities. On 29 bridge drawings, detection achieved mAP@50 values of up to 97.4%, and text recognition reached 93.0% exact-match accuracy with a 2.2% character error rate.

Magnetic-gear-enabled counter-rotating electromagnetic energy harvester for capturing low-speed wind energy

Authors: Yihao Li, Hao Zhang, Guoqing Cang, Lihua Tang, Kean C. Aw

Institution: University of Auckland, Harbin Engineering University

Published: 2026-08-22

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Wind energy is an abundant ambient power source for self-powered sensing, but conventional electromagnetic wind harvesters remain constrained under weak wind due to low rotor speed and limited magnetic flux variation. To address this issue, this paper presents a magnetic-gear-enabled Savonius-driven counter-rotating electromagnetic energy harvester (MG-SCR-EMEH) that integrates a Savonius turbine, an axial magnetic gear, and upper and lower electromagnetic power-generation modules. A non-contact magnetic speed amplification mechanism and a coaxial counter-rotating configuration triple the effective magnet-coil relative speed over direct-drive, thereby enhancing the magnetic flux variation and induction output while avoiding the wear and dissipation of contact-based frequency up-conversion. A theoretical model that combines aerodynamic drag and induced-voltage analyses, supported by finite element simulations, elucidates the mechanisms of magnetic-field modulation, torque transmission, and voltage generation. Experimental results show that the proposed MG-SCR-EMEH starts up at 3.5 m/s and, at 4.5 m/s, delivers 5.552 mW system-level equivalent charging power (27.76 mJ over 5 s into a 1 mF capacitor, 1.903 W/m 3 power density). At the same input speed of 50 RPM, increasing the magnet-coil relative speed from 50 RPM in the direct-drive configuration to 150 RPM in the magnetic-gear-coupled configuration raises the peak power from 22.78 to 166.78 mW. Furthermore, integrated with an energy-management unit, layered millimeter-wave radar nodes, and a BLE module, the MG-SCR-EMEH serves as the power source for a falling-object warning system, highlighting strong potential for low-speed wind energy harvesting and structural safety monitoring.

Uncertainty-aware metrics for evaluating machine learning regression models in materials testing

Authors: Mohammad Abbas, Mārtiņš Zaumanis

Institution: Swiss Federal Laboratories for Materials Science and Technology

Published: 2026-08-20

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

The advancement of machine learning across various fields has made model evaluation more critical. In materials science and engineering, classical metrics such as the mean absolute error MAE , the root mean square error RMSE , and coefficient of determination R 2 are widely used to report and compare model performance. However, since these are point accuracy metrics that consider deterministic targets, they often overlook uncertainty of the physical measurements. We propose to incorporate measurement uncertainty to evaluate model performance. We demonstrate that in synthetic datasets with varying homoscedastic and heteroscedastic uncertainty levels, these uncertainty-aware metrics provide estimates closer to the ground truth, especially the uncertainty-aware RMSE and R 2 . Additionally, the proposed uncertainty-aware estimates demonstrate an ability to preserve model ranking in asymmetric tests, by increasing the uncertainty threshold at which a lower-performing model is incorrectly ranked first by at least two times compared to classical metrics. When applied to real data, the uncertainty-aware metrics preserve differences between models similar to the classical metrics, but unlike the classical metrics, they also indicate when models reach the learning limit due to the uncertainty of the physical test. Overall, the proposed simple, statistically consistent metrics allow a more realistic comparison of machine-learning models to the hidden ground truth that any materials test possesses, thus improving training and evaluation of machine learning models.

Heat flux measurement system for assessing peripheral vascular function during cold provocation

Authors: Carlotta Massotti, Saeed Hadizadeh Aghueia, Massimo Bovenzi, Marcella Mauro, Angelo Tirabasso, Marco Tarabini

Institution: Politecnico di Milano, University of Trieste, Istituto Nazionale per l'Assicurazione Contro gli Infortuni sul Lavoro

Published: 2026-08-19

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Peripheral vascular function is important for the early detection and monitoring of vibration-related disorders, such as vibration-induced white finger, yet standardized cold-provocation assessments often rely on bulky or operator-dependent methods. We present a compact heat-flux measurement system that quantifies conductive heat exchange between the fingertip and a temperature-controlled surface while monitoring contact force, a key confounder of fingertip heat transfer. A thermoelectric cooler imposes a controlled boundary temperature, and an integrated force sensor supports repeatable contact conditions. Metrological characterization under controlled conditions shows measurement variability consistent with the heat flux sensor specification. Recordings in healthy volunteers exhibit a reproducible transient well captured by a double-exponential model and reach steady state after approximately 70 s. In a comparative validation study conducted in healthy subjects, local cooling to 10 °C induced a significant reduction in finger blood flow measured by strain-gauge venous-occlusion plethysmography. Consistently, mixed-effects modeling showed that, in the total sample (n = 20, obs = 98), each 1 ml/100 ml/s increase in finger blood flow was associated with a 39.6 mW increase in mean thermal power (robust standard error 7.52, p < 0.0001 ) after adjustment for covariates. The R-squared measures showed that the proportion of the total thermal power variance explained by all predictors via fixed slopes and random slope variation was 0.57, and the whole model explained about 68% of the variability in the measured thermal power. Overall, these findings support the feasibility of heat flux-based descriptors for tracking perfusion-related changes during cold provocation in healthy individuals. The present study does not establish diagnostic utility or discrimination between healthy and pathological subjects, and further evaluation in larger cohorts, including individuals with vibration-induced white finger or Raynaud-type disorders, is required.

Damage evaluation of surrounding rocks subjected to dynamic internal unloading and external loading

Authors: Ming Tao, Gongliang Xiang, Rui Zhao, Jinjie Liu

Institution: Central South University, Hong Kong Polytechnic University

Published: 2026-08-21

Journal: Tunnelling and Underground Space Technology

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Dynamic disasters in deep underground engineering are frequently triggered by the interaction between the pre-existing excavation damaged zone (EDZ) and external disturbances such as blasting or earthquakes. Current studies on the dynamic response of tunnels have mainly focused on wave scattering around openings without considering EDZ, whereas the cumulative effects of excavation-induced damage and subsequent dynamic loading remain insufficiently investigated. Therefore, this study formulated a theoretical and numerical framework to investigate the dynamic response of tunnels considering the cumulative damage induced by transient unloading and subsequent external dynamic loading. An analytical solution for the dynamic response considering the EDZ was derived using integral transformation and the wave function expansion method. Subsequently, a numerical simulation scheme utilizing the restart technique and a specific elasto-plastic damage model was implemented in LS-DYNA to reproduce the progressive failure process. The results indicated that the depth of the EDZ increased with the initial stress σ 0 thereby significantly affecting the stress distribution around the tunnel and the evolution of the dynamic damage zone (DDZ). Under dynamic disturbances, the near-field EDZ undergoes a stress drop due to softening, forcing the high-stress concentration to migrate to the deeper intact rock mass. This mechanism of unloading and stress transfer reveals the dual role of the EDZ. While it acts as a flexible layer that dissipates wave energy via scattering and damping, its degradation simultaneously drives peak stress migration into the deeper rock mass, thereby expanding the damage zone and increasing the risk of rockbursts.
