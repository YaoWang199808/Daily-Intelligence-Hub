Physics-guided data fusion for settlement prediction of high-fill railway embankments

Authors: Zongyu Zhang, Zhichao Huang, Qian Su, Shijie Liu, Zhuobin Wang, Mengfan Chen, Sakdirat; id_orcid 0000-0003-2153-3538 Kaewunruen

Published: 2026-10-10

Journal: Automation in Construction

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Settlement prediction for high-fill railway embankments is challenged by monitoring noise and stage-dependent deformation across construction and operation. This paper presents a physics-guided framework integrating multivariate construction information with settlement monitoring data. Monitoring sequences are denoised by a hybrid autoencoder with a signal-to-noise-ratio-based cleaning mechanism. A temporal graph convolutional neural network embeds a modified layer-wise summation formulation to guide variable interactions and settlement prediction. On the analysed dataset, the model achieved a coefficient of determination of 0.99 with the selected parameters. Compared with Informer, MTGNN, and TQNet, it achieved the lowest test-set mean squared error, averaged over ten random seeds, under both working conditions. Ablation experiments supported the effectiveness of the hybrid architecture. Component analyses indicated that the physics-guided module estimated settlement trends, while the data-driven module supplied local residual corrections. It provides a feasible framework for handling complex settlement-pattern transitions that are difficult to capture using conventional approaches.

Measurement-informed finite element damage identification under modal uncertainty using hybrid QPSO–SA

Authors: M.Q. Tran, Thuc Ngo Van, Hélder Silva Sousa, Jose Campos Matos, Huan Xuan Nguyen, Seyedmilad Komarizadehasl

Institution: University of Minho, Trường Đại học Xây dựng Miền Tây, VNU International School, Middlesex University London, Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya

Published: 2026-10-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Vibration-based damage estimates depend not only on the inverse algorithm but also on the quality, completeness, and modal reliability of the measured data. This study presents a measurement-informed finite element model updating framework that combines quantum-behaved particle swarm optimization with conditionally activated simulated annealing. Field acceleration measurements from a steel truss span of the Chuong Duong Bridge in Hanoi were processed by covariance-driven stochastic subspace identification and used to calibrate the healthy-state model. Damage-identification performance was then assessed using field-calibrated numerical scenarios with 1 to 4 stiffness-reduced members. Controlled tests represented degraded modal information through nominal 0–10% perturbations, mode-confidence weighting, and sensor layouts reduced from 34 to 6 locations. The calibrated model achieved a maximum frequency discrepancy of 1.789% and a minimum modal assurance criterion of 0.992. In the four-damage case, the mean cumulative normalized severity error of QPSO–SA increased from 76.1% at 0% noise to 93.9% at 5% noise and 133.0% at 10% noise. Its error remained lower than that of every benchmark algorithm at each investigated noise level. Reducing the sensor layout to six locations increased its mean error by 6.6 percentage points, compared with 10.7–17.6 percentage points for the alternatives. The additional simulated-annealing search increases computation time, but the results demonstrate a favorable stability–cost trade-off for periodic, measurement-informed condition assessment. The study is a field-calibrated numerical validation, measured damaged-state responses and environmental variability remain outside its scope.

Mechanisms of Hydraulic Fracture Offset Propagation in Layered Rocks: Insights from a Coupled Damage-hydro-mechanical Phase-Field Model

Authors: Lingfu Liu, Zahera Jabeen, Chloé F. Arson

Institution: Cornell University, ExxonMobil (United States)

Published: 2026-10-07

Journal: Rock Mechanics and Rock Engineering

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

It has been observed that hydraulic fractures that interact with weak interlayers sometimes deflect and propagate laterally before re-initiating into adjacent layers. Despite being commonly noted in layered formations, this phenomenon, known as offset propagation, remains poorly understood. To bridge this gap, a novel fully coupled damage-hydro-mechanical Phase-Field Model is formulated. The model captures mixed-mode fracture propagation, damage-dependent poroelastic degradation, and anisotropic permeability evolution induced by fracturing. The model is implemented in the open-source finite element framework MOOSE, designed for large-scale multiphysics simulations. To enhance numerical stability and convergence, a multi-app strategy is employed: the hydraulic and mechanical fields are solved in the main application, while the damage field is updated in a sub-application. Both resolution schemes are staggered within each time step, until global convergence is reached. Benchmark tests confirm the model’s ability to reproduce analytical solutions of pressure- and flow-controlled fracture dynamics problems. Numerical simulations reveal that fracture propagation within weak interlayers is governed by shear-dominated failure, while re-initiation into the overlying layer may result from a local competition between fluid pressure evolution and in situ stress contrast near the fracture tip. A comprehensive parametric study identifies the key mechanical and hydraulic factors that control the magnitude of the offset distance and the occurrence of fracture re-initiation. Results provide new insights into the interactions between hydraulic fractures and weak or soft interlayers, and highlight the mechanical and hydraulic conditions that govern offset propagation in layered formations.
