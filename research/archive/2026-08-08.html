High-fidelity random micro-vibration reproduction via a stinger-free magneto-pneumatic platform and multi-resolution adaptive FD-ILC

Authors: Chenglong Yu, Tianyi Li, Xuyang Yu, Chunguang Fan, Bo Zhao, Jiubin Tan

Institution: Harbin Institute of Technology, Ministry of Industry and Information Technology

Published: 2026-08-06

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

High-fidelity broadband random micro-vibration reproduction is a fundamental prerequisite for vibration-isolation performance evaluation and metrological calibration of ultra-precision equipment. Unfortunately, conventional ground excitation systems generally rely on mechanical stingers for force transmission, which tend to introduce parasitic stiffness and nonlinear friction. Moreover, under random vibration conditions, standard control algorithms inherently suffer from noise amplification and steady-state overlearning, severely constraining the signal-to-noise ratio and metrological reliability of micro-vibration testing. To address the above issues, this paper proposes a non-contact, direct-drive ground excitation platform based on a stinger-free magneto-pneumatic hybrid suspension, combining contactless actuation via a voice-coil-motor array with air-spring pre-isolation and low-stiffness load-bearing support to establish a low-background dynamic environment with reduced parasitic stiffness and mechanical coupling. A multi-resolution adaptive frequency-domain iterative learning control (MRA-FD-ILC) algorithm is further developed. This algorithm integrates one-third-octave-band error evaluation with narrowband high-resolution spectral updating to effectively suppress stochastic variance disturbances and overlearning, thereby enabling stable convergence in random vibration spectral-shaping control. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed system reproduces an adjustable random vibration spectrum from VC-C to VC-A over the 0.5–100 Hz frequency band. Across the VC-A–VC-C tests, the mean maximum one-third-octave band-wise absolute error did not exceed 0.78 dB, while the corresponding mean overall RMSE did not exceed 0.68 dB. The system also maintains stable spectral-shaping capability in the ultra-low-frequency range. The proposed method can provide important technical support for the performance evaluation of ultra-precision vibration-isolation systems. • A stinger-free direct-drive topology enables low-coupling random micro-vibration. • An MRA-FD-ILC strategy is developed with 1/3-octave spectral assessment. • Noise-robust gain scheduling and freezing improve reproduction stability. • VC-C to VC-A vibration is reproduced over 0.5-100 Hz with ≤ 0.78 dB error.

Bypass ultrasonic flow sensor for respiratory monitoring: flow-division-based structural adaptation and optimization

Authors: Jiayi Shi, Hui Hu, Shuai Ren, Tao Wang, Bingbing Ma

Institution: Beijing Institute of Technology

Published: 2026-08-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Accurate airflow measurement remains challenging in respiratory monitoring because the required operating range is wide and the aerodynamic conditions are complex. This study proposes a bypass ultrasonic airflow sensor and investigates the effects of the throttling structure and the bypass inlet diameter on flow-division characteristics. Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations and bench experiments were conducted for orifice-plate and vane-type restrictors with bypass inlet diameters of 2–6 mm. Based on the numerical and experimental results, three optimized configurations were selected for representative respiratory-monitoring scenarios, and piecewise-polynomial calibration models were established. After calibration, the three selected configurations achieved accuracies of ± 1.25%, ±1.86%, ±2.17% F.S., with the best repeatability of ± 0.13% F.S. and a response time below 20 ms. Robustness assessments demonstrate the sensor’s resistance to moisture interference and clarify the changes in split ratio and system applicability caused by inlet contamination. This study provides a practical framework for the structural design, selection, calibration, and evaluation of clinical-scenario adaptability of bypass ultrasonic airflow sensors for respiratory monitoring.

TPA-TCT analysis of the RD50-MPW4 monolithic pixel particle detector

Authors: F.R. Palomo, Jorge Jiménez‐Sánchez, Moritz Wiehe, Jory Sonneveld, B. Pilsl, F. Muñoz, Raimon Casanova, C. Irmler, P. Sieberer, C. Zhang

Institution: Universidad de Sevilla, European Organization for Nuclear Research, National Institute for Subatomic Physics, Austrian Academy of Sciences, Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona, University of Bonn, University of Liverpool

Published: 2026-08-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

The RD50-MPW4, a Depleted Monolithic Active Pixel Sensor (DMAPS) was analyzed using a Two Photon Absorption Transient Current Technique (TPA-TCT). This technique provides sensitivity maps with micrometer-scale spatial resolution, enabling the resolution of the boundaries of the detector’s sensitive volume, even for small-area pixels (62 × 62 μ m 2 in this study). With a 3D resolution, the depletion depth, the boundaries of the detector electric field, the 3D hit detection efficiency and the charge sharing between neighboring pixels were measured. The RD50-MPW4, a multi-project wafer chip developed by the HV-CMOS working group within the CERN RD50 collaboration, features a 64 × 64 DMAPS pixel matrix. Illuminating the chip from the backside, the TPA-TCT technique can characterize any pixel element in the matrix because silicon is transparent for near infrared laser light (1550 nm). Electron–hole pairs are generated only around the light focal point, deep in the silicon, so that any charge collected is precisely only from the focal point. With the TPA-TCT technique, the RD50-MPW4 was found to be have a 100% hit detection efficiency under specified conditions and an effective depletion depth of 226 μ m . It was also found that part of the charge in the periphery of the pixel was collected in the neighboring pixel. A 3D map of the sensor clearly shows the in-pixel electronics and the limits of the depletion region.

Bio-inspired 3D printed SHCC (3DP-SHCC) porous lattice walls for enhanced compressive performance

Authors: Guoqiang Du, Yan Sun, Ye Qian

Institution: University of Hong Kong

Published: 2026-08-06

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Drawing inspiration from the slender stem of Elytrigia repens , 3D printed strain-hardening cementitious composite (3DP-SHCC) porous lattice walls were designed and fabricated using 3D concrete printing. In this study, we developed 3DP-SHCC porous lattice walls incorporating five distinct porous lattice infill patterns. Both experimental tests and numerical simulations were conducted to examine the compressive performance of these walls. Results show that all specimens exhibit ductile failure, maintaining structural integrity even with axial compressive strains of up to 10%, which is difficult to achieve in traditional concrete structures. Compared to traditional 3D printed concrete walls, the bio-inspired walls exhibited 1.93- and 6.03-times improvements in compressive strength and ductility factor, respectively, while achieving lower structural density. In addition, a predictive method for the axial compressive ultimate load-bearing capacity was proposed, achieving high accuracy with errors between 0.02% and 11.2%, although independent validation is still required. This study provides new insights into the design of material-efficient and high-performance 3D printed walls.

Experimental characterization of the confinement-like behaviour in radial joints of steel fibre reinforced concrete segmental linings

Authors: Angel Denia, Andrea Monserrat‐López, Xavier Torelló, Albert de la Fuente

Institution: TYPSA Group (Spain), Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya

Published: 2026-08-06

Journal: Tunnelling and Underground Space Technology

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This paper presents an experimental investigation into the confinement-like behaviour in radial joints of steel fibre reinforced concrete (SFRC) precast segmental tunnel linings under concentrated loading. A total of 58 moulded and cored cylinders were tested under uniaxial compression to quantify the influence of fibre dosage, while qualitatively assessing the influence of fibre distribution and orientation conditions on the post-peak residual response. Additionally, 18 SFRC prismatic specimens were tested under concentric and eccentric bearing loads. Digital image correlation (DIC) was used to measure local strains, crack initiation, and propagation within the confined region. The results reveal two complementary confinement-like mechanisms: material confinement and geometric confinement. Material confinement is associated with fibre bridging in bursting and splitting cracks, which enhances the residual compressive strength and ductility with higher fibre dosage. Geometric confinement is associated with the biaxial stress field beneath the loaded area. In the prismatic specimens, geometric confinement within the upper 20% of the specimen thickness increased lateral restraint, delaying splitting and raising the effective compressive strength. Under eccentric loading, this confinement-like effect was reduced, although a more stable response was observed due to crack localisation. Comparison with an existing analytical splitting model showed that experimental contact stresses were 10–15% lower than predicted, indicating generally consistent agreement within the investigated configurations. The results provide insight into localised in-plane response of SFRC radial joints under concentrated loading and establish a basis for future investigations considering out-of-plane stress conditions.

Managing squeezing rock mass with TBM data analysis: rail link Rishikesh – Karnaprayag (India)

Authors: Georg H. Erharter, Sumit Jain, Øyvind Dammyr, Sjur Beyer, Rajinder Bhasin

Published: 2026-08-05

Journal: Tunnelling and Underground Space Technology

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

The 125.2 km rail link Rishikesh–Karnaprayag in the Lesser Himalayas of India represents a benchmark in mechanized tunnelling through complex geology. This paper focuses on Tunnel 8, a 14.58 km section excavated primarily using two single-shield hard rock tunnel boring machines (TBM) under challenging conditions characterized by tectonically deformed, partly water-bearing phyllites and high in-situ stresses, thus squeezing rock mass conditions were at hand. Both TBMs were equipped with advanced instrumentation, including a Void Measuring System (VMS) for real-time monitoring of shield gap size and tunnel wall deformation rates - an unprecedented application in single-shield TBM tunnelling. An integrated, near–real-time data analysis framework was developed to continuously assess TBM operational parameters, enabling proactive control and optimization of excavation performance. Within that framework, the VMS data was used to run a novel analytical model-based squeezing risk monitoring system which integrates gross TBM advance rate, tunnel wall deformation rate, shield length, and shield gap size. Additionally, tunnel seismic prediction was employed to characterize the geology ahead of the face, and supervised machine learning algorithms were implemented for rapid interpretation and visualization, facilitating informed decision-making by TBM operators. The project demonstrates how advanced monitoring systems and data-driven tunnelling can significantly enhance TBM performance and risk management in geotechnically adverse conditions. Key operational insights and recommendations are provided to guide future large-scale TBM projects in similar geological environments.
