3D-Defect identification method for bent pipe based on multimodal characteristics of ultrasonic guided wave

Authors: Denglong Ma, Yuxiang Zhou, Yuan Liu, Jianmin Gao, Yunxiang Tian

Institution: Xi'an Jiaotong University, Shaanxi Coal Chemical Industry Technology Research Institute

Published: 2026-09-29

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

The ultrasonic guided wave (UGW) method is widely used to detect corrosion defects in straight pipes, but its application to bent pipes remains limited. This study presents a 3D defect identification method for bent pipes based on multimodal UGW features, covering axial localization, circumferential localization, and radial penetration-risk assessment. Guided-wave propagation was first examined by numerical simulations and experiments. Results show that UGW energy is relatively uniform far from the bend and concentrates on the outside near the bending region. The radial depth of a defect has a stronger effect on echo amplitude than its circumferential length. A multimodal identification framework was then developed. Combining Gabor atomic matching pursuit with the Hilbert envelope method, axial positions were located with maximum errors of 1.84 % for straight pipes and 3.01 % for bent pipes. Circumferential position can be evaluated from the radar map of the reflection coefficient: for defects symmetric about the elbow geometry or located in straight-pipe sections, the symmetry axis of the radar map coincides with that of the defect cross-section. Finally, the peak amplitude ratio of the L(0,1) and L(0,2) modes is used to assess radial penetration risk, completing the three-dimensional characterization of bent-pipe defects. The proposed method shows potential for monitoring corrosion and crack damage in bent pipes.

Effective sampling-rate characterization of digital MEMS vibration sensing systems and implications for calibration

Authors: Andrea Prato, Alessio Facello, Giulio D’Emilia, Luciano Chiominto, Emanuela Natale, Alessandro Schiavi

Institution: Istituto Nazionale di Ricerca Metrologica, University of L'Aquila

Published: 2026-09-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

The calibration of digital MEMS sensing systems for vibration measurements (here intended as a single measuring chain, including the digital accelerometer sensor and the supporting microcontroller) presents unique challenges, particularly due to the variability in effective sampling rate introduced by the internal clocks of MEMS. This variability, in terms of mean sampling rate different from the nominal one and its standard deviation or short-term variability (hereinafter denoted in terms of jitter ), introduces systematic effects in frequency and amplitude determination, respectively, that are usually neglected during standard calibration procedures. The aim of this work is to provide simple, practical methods for estimating the effective sampling rate and its intrinsic short-term variability in MEMS sensing systems using time- or frequency-domain analysis, offering a balance between accuracy and usability. An analytical expression is also derived to evaluate the impact of jitter on sensitivity determination. These methods are experimentally applied to 25 nominally identical digital MEMS accelerometer systems, characterizing both their mean sampling rates and the jitter. Results show that the actual sampling rates differ notably from the nominal value up to 5.2 % while exhibiting negligible short-term variability, under the investigated conditions. These findings provide a foundation for improving calibration protocols of digital MEMS sensors in dynamic applications.
