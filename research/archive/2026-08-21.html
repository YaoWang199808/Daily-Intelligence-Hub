Fatigue performance of electrical insulator bolted connection with an unintended gap

Authors: Shiroshi Jayathilake, Pathmanathan Rajeev, Saman Fernando, Emad Gad

Institution: Swinburne University of Technology

Published: 2026-08-17

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

The bolted connections are important components in overhead power distribution network systems, fixing the insulator to the cross-arm. This connection is subjected to various loads, such as the self-weight of the conductor, wind, and thermal, which induce cyclic stresses that can lead to fatigue failure. The presence of separation gaps, resulting from non-compliances with dimensional tolerances in insulator connections, increases stress concentrations at the thread roots and promotes the initiation of cracks. Therefore, the role of gap size in the fatigue performance of the insulator connection is investigated in this study. The fatigue performance of a commonly used bolted connection (i.e., M24x3.0p stud) in a distribution power network having controlled gap sizes was experimentally tested under reverse cyclic flexural load using a test rig specifically developed for this purpose. The experimental results were used to develop a probabilistic fatigue life model for bolted connections with gaps. Furthermore, the probability of failure was estimated considering the effect of both gap size and load level of the connection. Also, the Miner's linear cumulative damage theory was employed, together with Monte Carlo simulation, to enable the inclusion of wind load variation to describe variable in-service loading and used for life expectancy estimation of the in-service connection. It was observed that fatigue life is reduced by approximately 80% as the gap distance increases from 1 mm to 3 mm. Finally, probabilistic life expectancy curves were developed to provide a quantitative assessment tool to assist the asset owners, in performing risk-based asset management.

Operational Modal Analysis from multi-setup measurements with roving references and Stochastic Subspace Identification

Authors: Samuel González Jiménez, Luis A. Padrón, Guillermo M. Álamo, Jacob D.R. Bordón

Institution: Universidad de Las Palmas de Gran Canaria

Published: 2026-08-17

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Accurate characterization of the modal parameters of existing structures from ambient vibration data within the field of operational modal analysis requires, in general, the collection of synchronous measurements at multiple points of the structure. However, when an insufficient number of sensors is available, the experimental campaign must be organized by dividing the measurements into subsets. In these situations, approaches like the reference-based Stochastic Subspace Identification method allows for full structural output-only identification with fixed reference sensors. Nevertheless, having common reference sensors for the whole campaign is not always feasible. Thus, this paper proposes a method to merge measurements from different ambient vibration data collection times and locations without a common reference sensor across all sets. To do so, the approach relies on roving references that are common only between successive groups of simultaneously measured positions. The algorithm’s operation is tested and analysed for two different case studies: a first one that uses synthetic data from a numerical model for the dynamic response of a simply supported beam subjected to ambient vibrations, and a second case that employs experimental measurements at an elevated pedestrian crossing. The results show that, in general, the proposed method is able to identify natural frequencies and mode shapes from ambient vibration measurements even with significant noise levels. Thus, this method can serve as a viable and straightforward alternative in situations where maintaining common reference sensor locations through the whole measurement campaign is not possible.
