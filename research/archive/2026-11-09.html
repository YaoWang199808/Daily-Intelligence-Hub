Metrological framework for Ground-Based measurement of gaseous emissions from turbine aircraft engines

Authors: Remigiusz Jasiński, Jakub Niklas

Institution: Poznań University of Technology

Published: 2026-11-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This article focuses on discussing and comparing the most critical aspects of metrology in testing gaseous emissions from aircraft engines during ground tests, such as choice of measuring equipment, its layout on site and its technical parameters. By referencing actual measurement campaigns and research projects, the study characterizes the objects of investigation – turbojet (specifically turbofan) engines used in commercial aviation – as well as global policies regarding emission analysis, the objectives and scope of engine trials, and the environmental conditions accompanying these tests. Furthermore, the article provides a detailed description of the measurement systems and the technical parameters of the instrumentation used to determine the concentrations of the gaseous compounds under study, primarily the carbon dioxide (CO 2 ), carbon monoxide (CO), unburned hydrocarbons (UHC), nitrogen oxides (NO x ) and sulfur oxides (SO x ). The second part presents a comparative analysis of major measurement campaigns along four dimensions: (1) sampling system design (probe type and position, line length and heating, dilution), (2) analytical techniques used for the same compounds, (3) engine operating conditions and fuels, and (4) ground-based versus in-flight measurement conditions. On this basis, the most vital stages of the research process are identified from a methodological perspective. A decision matrix for selecting measurement techniques, an uncertainty chain including wet/dry basis conversion, and a minimum set of reporting parameters are proposed.Finally, the study highlights the need for harmonization in gaseous emission research to facilitate the implementation of improvements in modern propulsion systems with reduced environmental impact, thereby addressing the challenges facing the dynamically evolving passenger aviation sector.
