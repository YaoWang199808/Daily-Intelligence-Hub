Recovering ontology-normalized layer classes from vector-native PDF drawings via graph-based learning

Authors: Andrea Carrara, Mahito Sugiyama, Stavros Nousias, André Borrmann

Institution: Technical University of Munich, National Institute of Informatics

Published: 2026-10-19

Journal: Automation in Construction

Method: Other

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Vector-native PDFs preserve geometry but often lose authoring-time CAD-layer organization. This paper presents a document-preserving vector-to-graph pipeline that reconstructs drawing elements and predicts ontology-normalized layer classes. Raw and canonicalized source-layer strings construct supervision but are excluded from inputs. The benchmark contains 381 drawings and 12.1 million vector elements under a three-level hierarchy of 307 fine-grained classes, 36 intermediate groups, and 5 coarse domains. Local geometric context lifts fine accuracy from 0.342 to 0.438, whereas geometry plus text reaches 0.344. Extended single-run training reaches 0.612 fine and 0.777 coarse accuracy. In matched three-seed runs, graph models reach 0.462–0.487 fine accuracy versus 0.416 for an equally informed MLP, while source-group holdouts are weaker. The evidence supports assisted recovery of ontology-normalized authoring-layer organization, not unrestricted engineering-object recognition or unattended automation.

Physics-informed DeepONet with modal coordinate encoding for predicting dynamic responses of unseen motor housings

Authors: Dayeon Jeong, Seho Son, YeonJong Kim, Ki‐Yong Oh

Institution: Anyang University, Hyundai Motor Group (South Korea), Hyundai Motors (South Korea)

Published: 2026-10-19

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This study proposes a unified physics-informed machine-learning (PIML) framework for predicting the dynamic responses of traction motor housings under unseen geometric conditions. The framework incorporates three key forms of physical priors into a neural network. First, an observation bias is introduced through modal coordinate encoding, which embeds both geometric characteristics and excitation-dependent modal contributions into the neural network. Second, an inductive bias is incorporated by deploying a separable deep operator network architecture, which effectively learns nonlinear operator mappings and generalizes them to unseen housing geometries. Third, a learning bias is enforced by embedding the governing equations of structural dynamics into the training process to ensure physically consistent predictions. Novel training strategies are also employed to enhance convergence and numerical stability during physics-informed training. Quantitative evaluations and ablation studies demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework, achieving a mean MAPE of 3.32 % across the full test set and outperforming conventional neural networks. A design-optimization application further confirms that the proposed framework improves computational efficiency compared with conventional finite element analysis. Overall, this study establishes a robust framework for accelerating AI-driven design optimization and advancing intelligent structural engineering methodologies.

Sensitivity-driven model updating for bridge structures using operational modal analysis and enhanced batched Thompson sampling-based Bayesian optimization

Authors: Thu Huynh Van, Duc C. Nguyen, Quang-Viet Vu, Wei Gao, Sawekchai Tangaramvong

Institution: Chulalongkorn University, Trường Đại học Xây dựng Miền Trung, VNU Vietnam Japan University, UNSW Sydney

Published: 2026-10-19

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This paper proposes a robust and effective finite element model updating (FEMU) framework for inverse parameter identification of bridge structures by integrating operational modal analysis (OMA) and variance-based global sensitivity analysis (GSA) with an enhanced batched Thompson sampling-based Bayesian optimization (ETS-BO) method. Specifically, a Gaussian process (GP) model is constructed to approximate the nonlinear relationship between uncertain structural parameters and the corresponding modal responses. Leveraging the trained GP model, the variance-based GSA identifies the most influential parameters governing the dynamic behavior of the bridge, thereby significantly reducing the dimensionality of the FEMU problem and improving computational efficiency. Building upon the reduced parameter space, the ETS-BO method is developed by integrating an adaptive trust-region selection strategy with an enhanced batched Thompson sampling (ETS) method to efficiently solve the FEMU problem. The adaptive trust-region selection evaluates the improvement potential at the regional level, guiding the search toward promising regions of the parameter space while avoiding over-exploration of less promising ones. The trust region method is then applied to perform local optimization, whereby each trust region is adaptively expanded or contracted based on its observed improvement history, and local optimization runs exhibiting greater potential for further improvement are selectively continued. Within the selected trust regions, the ETS method is employed to determine the optimal updating parameters by iteratively selecting batches of candidate solutions sampled from the posterior distributions of the corresponding independent GP models. The effectiveness of the proposed ETS-BO is validated through a real-world case study of a bridge structure equipped with wireless accelerometers for vibration monitoring. Ambient vibration responses recorded under normal traffic conditions are analyzed using OMA to identify modal parameters, which subsequently serve as targets for updating the finite element (FE) model developed to represent the structural behavior of the bridge. The results demonstrate that the proposed ETS-BO requires substantially fewer FE evaluations than conventional optimization methods while achieving rapid convergence toward the optimal solution. Furthermore, the updated FE model closely reproduces the experimentally identified modal parameters, including natural frequencies and mode shape correlations, thereby providing a reliable numerical representation of the in-service dynamic behavior of the bridge and demonstrating the potential of the proposed framework to support long-term structural health monitoring of large-scale civil infrastructure systems.

Energy transfer and modal coupling in coated composite spatial shells under thermal loading

Authors: Jichuan Cao, Hui Li, Guangyu Du, Xinyu Cui, 闫海乐, Bo Yang, Jian Xiong, Kaixiang Li, Zhongwei Guan

Institution: Northeastern University, Aircraft Strength Research Institute (China), Chinese Academy of Sciences, Institute of Metal Research, Harbin Institute of Technology, Technology Innovation Institute

Published: 2026-10-19

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

To address the vibration prediction and suppression requirements of fiber-reinforced composite spatial shells with protective coating (PC-FRCSSs) in thermal environments, this study develops an integrated framework combining dynamic modelling, coating preparation, thermal protection testing, and experimental validation. A theoretical model of PC-FRCSSs assembled from cylindrical and toroidal substructures is established by introducing virtual springs, spatial corner coupling, temperature-dependent material properties, and a higher-order shear deformation displacement field. The modal strain energy ratios and a degeneracy-sensitive coupling index are further proposed to quantify energy transfer and modal coupling. Then, the convergence analysis, literature verification, and finite element comparisons demonstrate the accuracy of the proposed model for coated and uncoated spatial shells. The coating preparation process is presented, and the thermal protection performance of the protective coating is evaluated under continuous heating at 473.15 K. Experimental results show that the maximum frequency errors of the uncoated and coated specimens are 3.96 % and 4.03 %, respectively, and the response prediction error remains within 10.91 %. The displacement responses of the coated specimens decrease by 23.5 % and 29.3 % at 299.15 K and 373.15 K, respectively. Finally, parametric results further reveal the effects of laying angle, spatial corner, and thickness-radius ratio on mode degeneracy and high-response regions.

Transformer-based video motion magnification for vision-driven structural health monitoring: Experimental validation on lab and full-scale structures

Authors: Marco A. Pérez, Ricard Lado-Roigé

Institution: Universitat Ramon Llull

Published: 2026-10-19

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This paper investigates the potential of deep learning-based video motion magnification (VMM) to enhance vision-based structural health monitoring (SHM) under real-world conditions. The proposed approach integrates a Swin Transformer architecture to amplify subtle structural vibrations captured in video recordings, aiming to improve the stability and reliability of non-contact dynamic response measurements. Building upon prior research on learning-based VMM, this paper evaluates whether incorporating attention-based representations can mitigate typical image artifacts and noise amplification seen in traditional approaches and enable the extraction of quantitative vibration-related indicators relevant to SHM. Experimental validation on both laboratory-scale and full-scale structures demonstrates that STB-VMM provides sharper magnified motion and more stable frequency responses than a previous convolutional model, particularly when video quality or spatial resolution is limited. The results suggest that transformer-based magnification represents a promising approach for enhancing vision-based vibration measurements, contributing to the advancement of practical, scalable, and cost-efficient SHM using standard video recordings.

Formulating pinched hysteresis models of wood shear walls using time integrals of restoring force and displacement

Authors: Jin‐Song Pei, Xuanda Shao, Joseph P. Wright, Shiling Pei, Yue Li, Joseph Havlicek, Peng F. Tang, Royce W. Floyd, Aikaterini P. Kyprioti

Institution: Colorado School of Mines, Western Michigan University, Weidlinger Associates (United States), Case Western Reserve University, University of Oklahoma, Meta (United States)

Published: 2026-10-19

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Modeling hysteresis is highly relevant for many nonlinear vibrating systems, calling for physically insightful and computational efficient solutions. In earthquake and wind engineering, wood shear walls are subjected to lateral loads rendering highly hysteretic load–displacement responses featuring pinching, force-intercept, and strength and stiffness degradation. Existing phenomenological models directly work on the evolution of the load and displacement, often leading to complicated models with a large number of empirical parameters. Facilitated with three real-world experimental datasets for wood shear walls from an existing publication, this study showcases an alternative hysteresis modeling approach. The time integrals of load and displacement are utilized instead; modeling pinching is made front and center. This is mem-modeling transferred from electrical engineering, where “mem” is for memory, i.e., hysteresis. Grounded in bond graph theory, formulated under nonlinear state-space representation, and enriched with hybrid dynamical system concepts, the mem-modeling framework is substantiated with critical details such as choices of state variables, formulation and visualization of the governing secant modulus functions, conceptually ready for identification and simulation using real-world restoring force–displacement responses.
