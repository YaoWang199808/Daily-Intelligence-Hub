Ontology-based spatial interlinking of heterogeneous asset data for bridge documentation

Authors: Anne Göbels, Oliver Schulz, Mengyan Peng, Morris Florek, Jakob Beetz, Steffen Marx, Chongjie Kang

Institution: Climate-Neutral and Resource-Efficient Construction, Technische Universität Dresden, RWTH Aachen University

Published: 2026-10-23

Journal: Automation in Construction

Method: Review / Survey

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Informed decision-making for built assets requires a comprehensive, spatially integrated overview of available documentation. This documentation, however, is typically heterogeneous, siloed, and loosely connected. The spatial relationship of each data source to the structure thereby remains implicit and accessible only to individual experts. To interlink these sources and query them automatically, their spatial relations to the asset must be made machine-readable, yet no vocabulary exists to express them. To address this, the Space Types Ontology (SPOT) is developed to explicitly express these relationships in a format-agnostic way, including the qualitative references that prevail in legacy documentation. The applicability of SPOT is demonstrated by semi-automatically interlinking the documentation of the Nibelungen Bridge in Worms, Germany, into a spatial knowledge graph. This approach makes heterogeneous asset data queryable across data types, reducing dependence on individual expert knowledge and providing a format-independent foundation for informed, data-driven asset management.

Fault identification for intelligent real-time monitoring via mobile accessible networks: a critical review of MEMS microphone-based sensing for bearing diagnostics

Authors: Firmansyah, Muhammad Khan, Isidro Durazo Cardenas

Institution: Cranfield University

Published: 2026-10-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Review / Survey

Citations: 0

Abstract:

The main objective of Condition Monitoring (CM) is to prevent unexpected downtime by monitoring machine conditions and reducing maintenance costs. To prevent failure, particularly in rotating machinery, CM is performed utilizing sensing devices, which have been the focus of extensive research in recent decades. This paper presents a critical review and provides comprehensive coverage of recent advances in CM for rotating machinery that utilize MEMS microphone-based sensing, with particular emphasis on bearing fault diagnosis relevant to the Fault Identification for Intelligent Real-time Monitoring via Mobile Accessible Network research programme. It synthesises recent journal articles focusing on MEMS (Micro-Electro-Mechanical Systems) microphone technologies, signal processing and machine learning techniques, methodological study design and experimental framework, and reported comparative analysis, advantages, limitations, and future research directions. Furthermore, the study reveals a dominant trend toward the use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) techniques, resulting in significant advances in CM and an emerging on non-contact-based sensing via mobile accessible system and self-powered sensing. Key gaps identified include the under-representation of MEMS microphone-based sensing, noise robustness and high sensitivity sensors, insufficient edge-deployment validation, the barriers of large datasets processing to achieve effectiveness, and the absence of multi-domain adaptive diagnostic frameworks. As a final point, options for scalable, low-cost, acoustic-based sensing and edge-intelligent computing are proposed, to deliver real-time monitoring across multiple domains.

Creep and post-creep performance of CFRP-confined steel tube columns filled with alkali-activated slag recycled concrete

Authors: Yunhe Li, Pang Chen, Jianxin Zhang, Shaojun Cao, Wenzhong Zheng

Institution: Hebei University of Technology, Harbin Institute of Technology

Published: 2026-10-23

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Carbon fiber reinforced polymer (CFRP) fabric–confined circular steel tube columns filled with alkali-activated slag recycled aggregate concrete (AAS-RAC), hereafter referred to as CFRP-ARCFST columns, employ multiple external confinement mechanisms to mitigate the high creep potential of AAS-RAC, exhibiting strong prospects for engineering applications. To quantify their long-term service performance, this study systematically investigates the creep behavior and post-creep mechanical response of CFRP-ARCFST columns. The results show that stress redistribution between the steel tube and the CFRP confinement effectively counteracts the creep increase induced by high recycled aggregate replacement ratios. Steel tube stiffness plays a dominant role in controlling creep, increasing the steel ratio from 8% to 12% reduces the creep coefficient by 41.4%. Increasing the number of CFRP layers provides an additional, though secondary, restraint effect, with a maximum reduction of 6.5%. A numerical creep analysis model that incorporates stress redistribution is developed. Furthermore, sustained loading does not alter the failure mode and results in a 1.7%–6.7% increase in ultimate load capacity. A predictive model for peak compressive strain is proposed by coupling time-dependent damage evolution with confinement enhancement. These findings provide a solid theoretical basis for the design of high-performance and sustainable composite columns.

A review of carbon fibre reinforced polymer–steel hybrid structures for dynamic mechanical systems

Authors: Aleksandr Pochezhertsev, Aki Mikkola, R. Scott Semken, José Humberto S. Almeida

Institution: Lappeenranta-Lahti University of Technology

Published: 2026-10-23

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Review / Survey

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This paper reviews the suitability of carbon fibre reinforced polymer (CFRP)–steel hybrid structures as load-bearing components of dynamic mechanical systems. The literature is evaluated against structural requirements, including load-carrying capacity, fatigue resistance and durability, and motion-related requirements, including inertial properties, vibration and damping. Static and fatigue findings are distinguished from direct measurements of dynamic response and from qualitative implications for multibody applications. The synthesis identifies configuration-dependent benefits in structural performance and selected vibration, damping and impact applications, while examining the limitations of transferring component-level findings to operating machinery. Design parameters are assessed in relation to the response quantities actually investigated; their effects on coupled system dynamics are not inferred from strengthening results alone. The available evidence supports preliminary design and feasibility assessment, but further validation is required for repeated acceleration and deceleration, damage-dependent dynamic properties and coupled multibody operation. The review provides an evidence-based framework for identifying applicable findings, design constraints and priorities for further investigation.

Tunnelling and Underground Space Technology

Mechanism of gas migration and release in magmatic high-pressure gas reservoirs induced by deep-buried tunnel excavation

Authors: Jilu Zhang, Xinrong Liu, Xiaohan Zhou, Yuanming Lai, Lei Fang

Institution: Chongqing Jiaotong University, Chongqing University

Published: 2026-10-23

Journal: Tunnelling and Underground Space Technology

Method: Analytical / Theoretical

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Deep-stratum non-coal gas reservoirs are characterized by low permeability, high in-situ stress, and limited gas reserves. The gas migration and release laws of surrounding rocks in such strata under excavation disturbance have not been systematically investigated. Based on field cases of gas emission in magmatic rock strata, this study explores the formation and occurrence mechanisms of high-pressure gas chambers. According to the unique characteristics of high-pressure gas reserves in magmatic rocks, a physical model test system for gas migration and release during tunnel excavation is developed. The stress response laws of surrounding rocks under the coupling effect of gas pressure and excavation unloading are revealed, and four typical evolutionary stages of gas emission from high-pressure gas chambers in magmatic rocks are proposed. Considering the significant fluctuation characteristics of pressure boundaries in small-scale gas reserves, a dynamic gas pressure boundary calculation method is established. A steady-state damage and transient seepage iterative coupling approach is adopted to realize the full-process evolutionary calculation of surrounding rock damage, deformation, and gas seepage. The dominant factors governing gas emission at different excavation stages are clarified, and a prediction model for gas emission volume of high-pressure gas chambers is established with comprehensive consideration of fracture density and in-situ stress. The research findings provide theoretical support for the prediction and control of gas outburst disasters in tunnels excavated in non-coal strata.
