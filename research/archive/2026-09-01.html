Uncertainty-aware close-range photogrammetry design: Circular marker 2D measurement accuracy characterisation and precision modelling for subpixel methods

Authors: Oier Saez de Egilaz, Iñigo Auzmendi Iriarte, Pedro Gonzalez de Alaiza Martinez, Ibai Leizea, Stuart Robson, Rafael Barcena

Institution: University College London

Published: 2026-08-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Incorporating propagation of quantified uncertainties into the design process of close-range optical-marker-based photogrammetry solutions would help in developing more accurate and precise systems able to keep up with competing metrological solutions in emerging Intelligent-Manufacturing environments. This article presents a novel analytical model for image-coordinate measurement precision of circular markers as a function of sensor noise and detected ellipse shape and grey-value intensity. The model can predict the image coordinate measurement standard deviations required for uncertainty propagation of estimated 3D coordinates more reliably than published techniques. Synthetic images with known lens distortion, sensor noise and focusing blur are used to characterise a selection of subpixel circular marker measurement methods with particular emphasis on uncertainty propagation during eccentricity error compensation. For the first time for the existing literature, the eccentricity-error correction disambiguation uncertainty is analysed, i.e., which of the two existing candidates for projected centre must be taken. At the same time, the range of ellipse shapes where eccentricity-error correction becomes ineffective in presence of other uncertainty sources is characterised.

Hoop strain-based leakage detection in field pipeline using pump induced hydraulic transients

Authors: Yuto Takahashi, Taiki Hagiwara, Tetsuya Suzuki

Institution: Niigata University, Yamaguchi University

Published: 2026-08-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Transient test-based techniques (TTBTs) detect anomalies in pressurized pipelines from pressure responses. Among TTBTs, the damping-based method estimates leakage location from the leakage-induced damping of water hammer pressure. This formulation assumes a uniform pipeline with a single wave speed. Many in-service pipelines composed of multiple pipe materials result in generating pressure waves with different wave speeds. Field validation under such conditions remains limited. This study extends the uniform-pipe formulation to a piecewise-uniform eigenfunction formulation. The extension accounts for wave-speed variation between segments. The formulation is applied to an in-service 5.3 km pumped irrigation pipeline of FRPM, steel, and PVC-U sections. Water hammer transients are generated by gradual pump shutdown. Leakage locations are estimated from ratios of harmonic damping rates of internal pressure and surface hoop strain. Strain gauges are used for the hoop strain measurement. A preliminary feasibility test of non-contact strain measurement is additionally conducted, which introduces digital image correlation (DIC). Under the piecewise-uniform formulation, pressure-based localization gives smallest candidate errors of 13% or less in all six cases. Strain-gauge data localizes leakage in four of six cases. The smallest candidate errors are 2.4%, 4.3%, and 4.7% in three of these. The DIC test gives the smallest candidate error of 4.3% in one of three tested cases. The piecewise-uniform formulation reduces the smallest candidate error compared with the uniform assumption in most cases with candidate positions. DIC indicates feasibility as a non-contact measurement option for further investigation. These extensions broaden the practical reach of damping-based TTBTs in pipeline diagnostics.

Surrogate assisted optimization of S-type shape memory polymer lattice metastructures for enhanced energy absorption and recovery

Authors: Shahram Hosseini, Alireza Pirhaji, Mohadeseh Hosseinzadeh, Amin Farrokhabadi

Institution: Tarbiat Modares University, University of Nottingham Ningbo China

Published: 2026-08-31

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Shape memory polymers have attracted growing attention in recent years due to their unique capability of combining high energy absorption with reversible deformation. These characteristics make them promising candidates for lightweight, multifunctional structures in aerospace, automotive, and biomedical applications. In this study, a lattice structure with S-type unit cells (a geometry known for its curved, arc-shaped configuration) is fabricated from polylactic acid (PLA) shape-memory polymer. The thermo-mechanical cycle of deformation, cooling, unloading, and reheating was simulated and experimentally validated to characterize the time-dependent shape-memory behavior. Data extracted from finite element simulations were employed to train an artificial neural network (ANN) surrogate model, which provided accurate predictions of mechanical performance. Subsequently, a genetic algorithm (GA) was applied to optimize lattice geometry with the dual objectives of maximizing energy absorption and enhancing recovery efficiency. Results demonstrated approximately 80% shape recovery upon reheating above T g . The proposed integrated numerical-machine learning-optimization framework highlights the potential of combining high-fidelity physics-based modeling with data-driven surrogate models for the efficient design of advanced energy-absorbing metastructures with excellent recoverability. The novelty of this study does not lie in the geometric shape of the structure itself, but rather in the first-time combination of: (i) experimental and numerical characterization of its full thermo-visco- hyper-elastic shape memory behavior, (ii) development of an ANN surrogate trained on high-fidelity finite element data, and (iii) multi-objective GA optimization specifically targeting the concurrent maximization of energy absorption and shape recovery.

Experimental dynamic progressive collapse assessment of post-and-beam mass timber substructures with two-bay cross-laminated timber floors under interior column removal

Authors: Nikshan Amatya, Benoit P. Gilbert, Hong Guan, Chuen Yie Lo, Minghao Li, Frank Lam

Institution: Griffith University, University of British Columbia

Published: 2026-08-31

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Mass timber buildings are emerging construction systems that present distinct design challenges, one of which is progressive collapse. Current design guidelines are largely based on studies conducted on steel and concrete structures, whereas experimental research on the progressive collapse behaviour of mass timber buildings has been predominantly limited to quasi-static testing. However, progressive collapse is a dynamic and nonlinear phenomenon that can lead to severe social and economic repercussions. This paper provides new insights into the progressive collapse behaviour of three-dimensional post-and-beam mass timber buildings with cross-laminated timber (CLT) panels spanning two-bays. Two scaled substructures were subjected to both quasi-static and dynamic testing under an interior column removal scenario. The load was incrementally increased until collapse. The structural responses, load redistribution mechanisms, dynamic increase factors (DIFs), failure modes, and associated collapse mechanisms are analysed and discussed. The findings highlighted that the substructures exhibited fewer alternative load paths and a less uniform load‑redistribution pattern compared with conventional concrete systems. The CLT panels spanning two-bays provided the primary alternate load path, only compressive arch action was observed, not catenary action. It is critical that the elements along the primary alternate load path are designed to sustain most of the redistributed load. The observed average DIF value was 1.69 and was 15.5% lower than the theoretical DIF value of 2.0 proposed in the DoD and Eurocode. The observed collapse mechanisms were characterised by the successive shear failure of the beam-to-column connectors, with those along the primary alternate load path failing first.

An Overview of Planetary Regolith Simulant Development and Testing for Geomechanics and ISRU Research

Authors: Jared Long-Fox, Brandon Dotson, D. T. Britt

Institution: University of Central Florida

Published: 2026-08-31

Journal: Rock Mechanics and Rock Engineering

Method: Review / Survey

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Sustained human and robotic exploration of the Moon, Mars, and beyond requires a thorough understanding of planetary regolith, the granular surface material covering rocky bodies. Limited availability of returned extraterrestrial samples makes terrestrially manufactured regolith simulants essential for technology development in mobility, excavation, in situ resource utilization, and infrastructure. Designing and using simulants involves trade-offs between compositional fidelity, physical property fidelity, safety, and material availability. This paper provides an overview of best practices and considerations in simulant development, informed by work at the University of Central Florida’s Center for Lunar and Asteroid Surface Science (CLASS). Key factors in simulant design, including mechanical, thermal, and electromagnetic properties, are discussed alongside standard characterization methods. The influence of environmental conditions and preparation protocols on experimental outcomes is also examined. Many of these lessons are not widely published, so this work aims to provide researchers, engineers, and technologists with guidance for creating high-fidelity, well-characterized simulants and experimental protocols. Recommendations include advancing standardized testing procedures, developing open-access simulant property databases, and building large-scale test facilities to improve reproducibility and relevance. A systems-level, interdisciplinary approach to simulant design and testing is critical for ensuring that terrestrial experiments accurately represent extraterrestrial surface conditions, supporting safe and effective planetary exploration and resource utilization.
