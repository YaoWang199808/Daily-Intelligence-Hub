Impact of ab initio line-intensity ratios on primary spectroscopic thermometry

Authors: Daniel Lisak, Szymon Wójtewicz, Agata Cygan, Piotr Wcisło, R. Ciuryło, Oleg L. Polyansky A B, Nikolai F. Zobov, Jonathan Tennyson, Katarzyna Bielska

Institution: Nicolaus Copernicus University, University College London

Published: 2026-10-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Review / Survey

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Line-intensity-ratio thermometry (LRT) is a spectroscopic primary thermometry method whose accuracy depends critically on the quality of the reference molecular line intensities. We investigate the impact of reference spectroscopic data on LRT by reanalyzing previously measured cavity mode-dispersion spectroscopy spectra of ten CO (3–0) band transitions at 296 K using newly available ab initio line-intensity ratios [Science Advances 11, eadz6560 (2025)]. Keeping the experimental data and analysis procedure unchanged allows the contribution of the reference data to be isolated from other uncertainty sources. The updated line-intensity ratios improve the consistency of temperatures retrieved from different line pairs by an order of magnitude compared to the result reported in [Physical Review Letters 135, 103201 (2025)]; for seven out of nine line pairs, the statistical standard error of the mean (SEM) of retrieved temperatures is 1.3 mK. The weighted mean spectroscopic temperature agrees with the independently measured gas-cell temperature within a few mK. The combined standard uncertainty of the mean spectroscopic temperature is 19 mK (62 ppm at 296 K), with the dominant contribution arising from spectral line-shape modeling rather than reference line intensities.

Measurement-grounded harmonic compliance of a modular 180 kW ultra-fast DC charger

Authors: Ali Algaddafi, Siham Hasan, E. Diryak, Fathi Eltayeeb Abukhres

Institution: University of Birmingham, Sirte University

Published: 2026-10-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This paper establishes a measurement-grounded framework for assessing harmonic compliance of a modular 180 kW ultra-fast DC charging system formed from six parallel 30 kW power supply modules (PSMs). The central scientific problem is determining how to make grid-compliance decisions for a scalable, multi-module charger when harmonic emissions vary with operating power and module count. Harmonic currents for 120 kW and 180 kW configurations are acquired using an IEC 61000–4-30 Class A analyser, independently cross-validated by an FFT measurement chain to the 50th order, and extended to the 100th order (5 kHz). An explicit uncertainty budget and repeated-session statistics establish the reliability of the compliance margin. Converter parameterisation and droop current-sharing analysis provide the physical and control context for the measured spectra; a cluster assessment propagates the measured per-charger spectrum to IEEE 519–2022 limits; and a compact regression model provides an operational screening estimate rather than replacing Class A measurement. THDi was measured as 8.8% at 120 kW and 8.2% at 180 kW, with the difference comparable to the reported session variability (±0.3% and ±0.4%, respectively), while the individual harmonic currents remain within IEC 61000–3-12 limits at Rsce = 33. The resulting framework connects charger operation, traceable harmonic measurement, uncertainty, and deployment-scale compliance in one experimentally anchored workflow.
