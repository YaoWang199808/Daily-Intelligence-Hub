Complex-amplitude design of dynamic acoustic holography via physics-assisted neural network

Authors: Jian Chen, Dan Xu, Youlong Hua, Lixiaoxue CHEN, Zheng Fan, Mengru Zhang

Institution: State Key Laboratory Fluid Power and Mechatronic Systems, Zhejiang University, Nanyang Technological University, State Key Laboratory of Chemical Engineering

Published: 2026-10-21

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Acoustic holography promises diverse applications, including high-resolution imaging, water–air communication, and in-vivo tissue/organ printing. While complex-amplitude design of acoustic holography yields superior performance in resolution and information content for sophisticated acoustic fields, but most holograms are spatially and temporally static once fabricated. This dilemma may be addressed by introducing the incident wavefront modulation as an additional degree of freedom, and dynamic holography would be realized by tuning the incident wavefronts via electrically switching a transducer array. Here, we propose a physics-assisted neural network optimization framework for the complex-amplitude design of dynamic acoustic holography, which enables joint optimization of multiple incident amplitude and phase profile. The framework embeds an angular-spectrum propagation model into neural network and introduces amplitude-binarization regularization to enable dynamic control through an electrically switched partitioned transducer. Furthermore, the proposed method exhibits remarkable versatility, supporting the phase modulations in either continuous or binary manner and even cascaded configuration. As a proof-of-concept demonstration, it successfully achieves the dynamic display of three-frame animations and 26 alphabetic letters, creating compelling “acoustic movies”. Quantitatively, the proposed method outperforms traditional designs both numerically and experimentally, which reduces mean squared error by 24.1% and 32.1%, respectively. We believe this work would greatly advance acoustic holography and imply unprecedented opportunity for broad acoustics.

New integrated autonomous solution for underground monitoring and mapping

Authors: Valerio Brunacci, Domenico Capriglione, Chiara Carissimo, Damiano Crescini, Filippo Milano, Antonio Moschitta, Giordano Piubeni, Giorgia Polidori, Cecilia Provenzale, Francesco Santoni

Institution: University of Perugia, Czech Technical University in Prague, Università degli studi di Cassino e del Lazio Meridionale, University of Molise, University of Brescia

Published: 2026-10-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Soil contamination monitoring and precision nutrient assessment require measurement systems capable of operating directly in subsurface environments while ensuring reliable spatial referencing of collected data. This paper presents a novel integrated autonomous solution for underground monitoring and mapping, combining in-line chemical sensing with magnetic-field-based localization in a unified architecture designed for deployment on drilling or burrowing robotic platforms. The proposed system integrates: (i) a near-infrared (NIR) spectroscopy module coupled with a convolutional neural network (CNN) for the estimation of urea-nitrogen content; (ii) a solid-state ion-selective electrochemical sensing unit for selective potassium detection in soil-derived aqueous extracts; and (iii) an AC magnetic localization subsystem based on surface transmitting anchors and a triaxial underground receiver. The architecture is designed to ensure spatio-temporal integration between measurement and positioning, enabling spatially consistent subsurface chemical mapping in GNSS-denied environments. The NIR–CNN approach demonstrated reliable urea-N estimation (R 2 = 0.79 , RMSE = 3.57%), while the electrochemical subsystem provided selective and repeatable potassium measurements with a more repeatable potentiometric response in the additively manufactured sensors. The magnetic localization algorithm achieved sub-decimeter accuracy in simulation (RMSE ≈ 8.8 cm when the anchors’ position is known with negligible accuracy) and sub-meter performance (RMSE ≈ 40 cm, in presence of anchors’ positioning errors) in outdoor experimental validation, confirming the feasibility of real-time underground positioning within operational constraints of drilling platforms. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed integrated sensing and localization framework for subsurface environmental monitoring, supporting future applications in precision agriculture, soil contamination assessment, and autonomous underground exploration.

Fire performance and post-fire axial capacity of composite columns composed of parallel strand bamboo and laminated bamboo lumber

Authors: Lu Wang, Shihao Chen, Xinjie Zhu, Jincheng Zou, Jiajia Ou, Keqian Ji, Yanyan Sun, X. Zhang, Lu Wang

Institution: Southeast University, Nanjing Tech University, Sichuan Provincial Architectural Design and Research Institute (China), Suqian University

Published: 2026-10-21

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Parallel strand bamboo (PSB)-laminated bamboo lumber (LBL) composite columns were tested under ISO 834 standard fire exposure and post-fire axial compression to investigate their charring behavior and residual axial capacity. Results showed that charring depth increased bilinearly with exposure time, with nominal rates of 0.42 mm/min for the outer PSB layer and 0.86 mm/min for the inner LBL core. An area-equivalent charring-depth model was developed to account for corner rounding and nonuniform cross-sectional loss, yielding depths approximately 1.04 times the measured values. As fire exposure increased, failure shifted from local crushing under the combined action of the outer PSB layer and LBL core to coupled crushing–lateral buckling dominated by the residual LBL cross-section. Over 0–105 min, the post-fire slenderness ratio increased from 20.78 to 59.95, residual axial capacity decreased by 93.6% from 2620.12 to 166.71 kN, and ultimate axial displacement decreased from 25.60 to 9.05 mm. A post-fire residual axial capacity assessment method was also developed by combining the effective residual cross-section method with stability coefficients from timber design codes. Zonal design recommendations based on the post-fire slenderness ratio were proposed: GB 50005–2017 is recommended when λ ≤ 38, whereas EN 1995–1–1 is recommended when λ > 38. The findings provide guidance for the fire resistance design and post-fire safety assessment of PSB–LBL composite columns.

A semi-supervised approach for structural damage state diagnosis under severely imbalanced class distributions

Authors: Dengke Jiu, Bin Wang, Yan-Gang Zhao

Institution: Beijing University of Technology, Yanshan University

Published: 2026-10-21

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Rapid and accurate assessment of structural damage after earthquakes is crucial for improving emergency response efficiency and optimizing decision-making. Traditional nonlinear time-history analysis (NLTHA) is computationally expensive and relies on precise structural parameters, limiting its applicability for large-scale or rapid evaluations. While data-driven methods generally require a large amount of labeled data and are susceptible to class imbalance, resulting in insufficient recognition of minority classes, especially severe damage. To address the challenges of scarce labeled data and highly imbalanced classes in practical scenarios, this paper proposes a semi-supervised structural damage diagnosis model that integrates deformable convolutional residual networks (DCRN) with an imbalance-aware mechanism. This method uses continuous wavelet transform (CWT) to construct a time-frequency representation of structural responses and employs the DCRN to capture local non-uniform deformations and key feature regions adaptively. A Teacher-Student consistency-based semi-supervised learning mechanism is then applied to effectively exploit latent information from unlabeled data under limited labeled conditions. Additionally, the imbalance-aware loss function combining focal loss, logit adjustment, and distribution alignment is designed to suppress majority-class bias and enhance the stability of minority-class recognition. Finally, numerical simulations and ablation experiments on two datasets demonstrate that, even with only approximately 10% labeled samples and highly imbalanced class distributions, the proposed method achieves accurate recognition of mild, moderate, and severe damage states, with particularly superior performance in identifying severely damaged samples compared to traditional fully supervised models and semi-supervised methods.

Lateral impact response of circular RC columns at low temperatures: Experimental and numerical modelling

Authors: Rong Xi, Xie Jian, Jia‐Bao Yan, Ou Zhao, Hao Wu

Institution: Tianjin University, Nanyang Technological University

Published: 2026-10-21

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Reinforced concrete (RC) infrastructure in polar and cold regions is exposed to the extreme low temperatures and subjected to accidental impact loadings. This study experimentally and numerically investigates the lateral impact behavior of circular RC columns over a temperature range from −80 °C to ambient temperature (20 °C). Two axial compression tests were firstly conducted to obtain the baseline load-carrying capacities, while five drop-hammer impact tests were then performed to explore the dynamic responses and damage evolution. The test results showed that the decrease in temperature significantly enhanced the axial compression capacity. As the temperature decreased from 20 °C to −80 °C, the average impact force increased by 9.6%, while the ultimate and residual displacements decreased by 27.9% and 48.1%, respectively. Moreover, finite elements were developed to reproduce the impact responses and damage evolution, demonstrating good agreement with the experimental observations. Parametric analyses further revealed that increasing impact mass and velocity markedly amplified structural deformation and damage, while increasing reinforcement ratios effectively suppressed both deformation and residual damage. These findings provide new insights into the low-temperature dynamic impact performance of RC structures and establish a robust analytical basis for the optimized design and assessment of infrastructure in cold regions.

Unveiling the sources of discrepancies in fiber-model predictions of residual displacement for RC columns: Insights from pseudo-dynamic tests

Authors: Wei Liu, Shaowei Wu, Mingke Li, Run Tian, Zhongguo Guan

Institution: Tongji University

Published: 2026-10-21

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Residual displacement is a critical indicator for evaluating the post-earthquake functionality and repairability of reinforced concrete (RC) structures. Although fiber beam models have been widely used to simulate the seismic responses of RC structures, both existing studies and the findings of this study have shown varying discrepancies in their predictions of residual displacement: conventional fiber models generally underestimate residual displacement, whereas models incorporating the SITC (Stuff in the cracks) material tend to overestimate it. However, the sources of these discrepancies remain unclear, mainly because shake-table tests usually provide limited access to real-time hysteretic responses, while quasi-static tests cannot fully characterize the evolution of dynamic residual displacement under seismic excitation. To address this issue, pseudo-dynamic tests were conducted on two 1/5-scale RC columns, producing different levels of residual displacement. The tests aimed to compare the discrepancies between numerical simulations and experimental results in terms of hysteretic behavior and residual displacement, and to identify their underlying causes. The comparison results indicate that the numerical hysteretic curves obtained from conventional fiber models exhibit an abrupt turn from a low-stiffness state to a high-stiffness state during reverse loading, whereas the measured hysteretic responses show a smooth transition at this stage. The presence of the low-stiffness branch makes the numerical response more likely to unload toward the vicinity of zero displacement after the peak displacement. In addition, the SITC material generates additional strain during unloading, which introduces extra plastic deformation after the peak displacement in the numerical model and consequently leads to an overestimation of residual displacement. Based on the material-level mechanism analysis, a modified uniaxial concrete constitutive model is proposed and implemented in OpenSees. Comparisons with the experimental results demonstrate that the proposed model outperforms widely used fiber models in predicting dynamic residual displacement.

Seismic fragility analysis considering multidimensional performance limit states and vector-valued seismic intensity measures

Authors: Jiting Qu, Junwen Lin, Linsheng Huo

Institution: Dalian University of Technology

Published: 2026-10-21

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Analytical / Theoretical

Citations: 0

Abstract:

To comprehensive quantify seismic intensity, mitigate the dispersion of structural dynamic responses under seismic excitations, and more precisely evaluate the structural seismic fragility, this study proposes a seismic fragility analysis method that concurrently accounts for multidimensional performance limit states and a vector-valued seismic intensity measure (IM). In this study, an optimal selection method for vector-valued IMs is first developed by evaluating 36 individual scalar IMs and conducting correlation analysis via maximal information coefficient (MIC) and multicollinearity diagnosis on the superior ones. Subsequently, marginal distributions of two distinct engineering demand parameters (EDPs) are analyzed, leading to a method for determining the optimal copula function that can account for capture the complex dependence among varying EDPs. Based on the determined optimal vector-valued IM and copula function, the formulation of seismic fragility functions incorporating multidimensional performance limit states and vector-valued IM is established. A five-story steel frame is employed as the case study to validate the proposed methodology. Comparisons with traditional method based on a single EDP demonstrate that the proposed approach provides a more comprehensive evaluation of structural seismic performance.

Foam concrete-filled S600E stainless CFS built-up special shape columns: Tests, numerical and machine-learning investigations

Authors: Yi Hu, Yuntian Su, Shizhong Zhou, Liqiang Jiang, Jin Xu

Institution: Central South University of Forestry and Technology, Central South University

Published: 2026-10-21

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Concrete-filled cold-formed steel (CFS) columns may brittlely fail if local buckling occurred of CFS components, which result in lateral confinement losses for the infilled concrete. Thus how to enhance the performance of CFS components is important. In this paper, a novel type of S600E stainless steel (SSS) with built-up closed special shape sections was proposed for enhancing the loading capacity instead of the normal cold-formed steel. Experimental and numerical simulation analyses were conducted for understanding the failure mechanism, confinement effects and ultimate capacity of foam concrete-infilled cold-formed S600E sorbite stainless steel composite columns. Finally, a data-driven machine-learning method is used for quick predicting the ultimate capacity of the columns, and the results are compared to the existing codes. The results of eight composite specimens indicate that the failure mode of composite specimens was still characterized by local buckling of the outer stainless steel and crushing of the core foam concrete, however, they exhibit a smooth transition in load-displacement curves before and after the peak load, indicating the favorable ductility. The results calculated from the Eurocode 4 were significantly overestimated, even though it was demonstrated good accuracy for foam concrete-filled normal CFS built-up special shaped sections by the authors’ previous work, and the DBJ/T 13–51 in China exhibited more accurate. An ultimate capacity prediction formula is proposed taking into account the yield strength of sorbite stainless steel and the confinement effect coefficient, and the errors were less than 15% compared to tests, and the results of different sections are compared with the ML-based results to verify its reliability and applicability.
