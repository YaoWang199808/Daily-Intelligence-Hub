LLM-guided adaptive segmentation of tunnel linings from point clouds

Authors: Xinghui Tao, Zehao Ye, Guangming Wang, Jelena Ninic, Brian Sheil

Published: 2026-11-11

Journal: Automation in Construction

Method: Other

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Automated inspection of segmental tunnel linings requires adaptive segmentation from 3D point clouds, yet expert-tuned pipelines often degrade when tunnel conditions vary. This paper presents R4Tun, a large language model (LLM)-driven adaptation framework that extends an expert-designed pipeline (SAM4Tun) with bounded parameter tuning informed by structured context: memory ($m$), state ($s$), and knowledge ($k$). Evaluated on 30 selected Seg2Tunnel subsets (13 regular, 17 complex) across three LLMs, the full $m+s+k$ design raised mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) from 0.18 to 0.43--0.48 and overall accuracy (OA) from 0.42 to 0.59--0.65 relative to the static SAM4Tun baseline, with the near-reference regular (staggered) subsets reaching mIoU 0.784--0.796 across LLMs. Across 270 (30 tunnels $\times$ 3 different LLMs $\times$ 3 context settings) runs, the LLMs showed similar parameter-adjustment trends (with overlapping 95\% CIs on mean gains) and consistently adjusted a shared set of critical parameters. These results support R4Tun as a controlled, label-free, cross-LLM adaptation mechanism in the tested SAM4Tun--Seg2Tunnel setting, demonstrating consistent accuracy gains; we position R4Tun as a mechanism contribution rather than a deployable final-inspection system, in which each bounded parameter change is auditable via logged rationales.

Dynamic compensation for sharper cutting force signals: A case study on the veneer peeling process

Authors: Hugo Curial, Bertrand Marcon, Stéphane Girardon, Joffrey Viguier, Louis Etienne Denaud

Institution: Arts et Métiers, Laboratoire Bourguignon des Matériaux et Procédés

Published: 2026-11-11

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

The accurate measurement of cutting forces is essential for understanding and controlling the wood peeling process, in particular when force measurements are coupled with high-speed visual observation of the cutting zone. However, the dynamic behavior of the embedded dynamometers in the studied micro-peeling machine severely limits the usable measurement bandwidth. This introduces amplitude distortions and phase delays at frequencies associated with critical cutting phenomena such as lathe-check formation. This study presents a complete dynamic characterization of a micro-peeling lathe equipped with two 2-axis piezoelectric dynamometers and applies. A direct inverse filtering approach is applied to compensate for these structural dynamic effects. The frequency response functions of the measurement system are identified through modal analysis using broadband excitation and Welch-averaged spectral estimators. A frequency-domain compensation is designed to extend the effective bandwidth while preserving phase accuracy. The configuration studied combines two tools acting simultaneously in the same cutting zone with a continuous cutting process. No force model of sufficient fidelity is available to serve as a validation reference for this configuration. The compensation is first validated on known inputs, under both impulse-hammer and sinusoidal excitation, showing substantial reductions in amplitude and phase errors across the main resonances. It is then assessed under real peeling conditions at near-industrial cutting speeds. In the absence of a reference model, lathe-check formation is used as a physical landmark, identifiable in both the force signal and the synchronized high-speed images. The compensated signals restore the temporal correlation between force variations and the observed lathe-check events, an alignment that conventional low-pass filtering cannot preserve.

A density–uniformity-based method for extracting building boundaries and corners from point-cloud data under non-uniform measurement conditions

Authors: Hanbin Park, Donggun Lee, kyuman cho, Taehoon Kim

Institution: Chosun University

Published: 2026-11-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Review / Survey

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Point-Cloud Data (PCD) are increasingly used to derive geometric information for existing buildings; however, extracting reliable boundary lines and corner points remains difficult under non-uniform point distributions, noise, and incomplete observations. Conventional approaches such as RANSAC and convex hull are often sensitive to such conditions, which limits the accuracy and consistency of geometric measurement from PCD. This study proposes a density–uniformity-based method for extracting building boundaries and corners from PCD under non-uniform measurement conditions. The proposed workflow consists of axis alignment, initial boundary candidate estimation, density-based candidate generation, and weighted evaluation of density and spatial uniformity for final boundary and corner extraction. To validate the proposed method, Aerial LiDAR Data (ALD) and terrestrial LiDAR Data (TLD) datasets were acquired for two buildings with different geometric characteristics, and boundary lines and corner points were extracted at multiple elevations. The extracted results were quantitatively compared with Robotic Total Station (RTS) measurements and with the results obtained using RANSAC, Convex Hull, and Alpha-shape. For the RTS-surveyed front-façade corners and corresponding boundary lengths, the proposed method achieved an average boundary-length error of 0.132% and a corner Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of 0.1358 m. In addition, sensitivity analyses demonstrated stable behavior of the proposed method within the investigated RANSAC-threshold, bin-resolution, and weighting ranges, while the Alpha-shape analysis confirmed its dependence on the α parameter and point-spacing structure. These results indicate that the proposed method can improve the consistency and objectivity of geometric measurement from point-cloud data and can support existing-building documentation and digital geometric information generation under irregular measurement conditions.

Engineering Structures

Data-driven multifidelity multiscale topology optimization via phasor-based evolutionary de-homogenization

Authors: Shuzhi Xu, Yifan Guo, Hiroki Kawabe, Kentaro Yaji

Institution: The University of Osaka, University of Alberta

Published: 2026-11-11

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Numerical Simulation

Citations: 1

Abstract:

Multiscale topology optimization is crucial for designing porous infill structures with high stiffness-to-weight ratios and excellent energy absorption. Although gradient-based methods provide a rigorous framework, they are computationally expensive and struggle to capture cross-scale sensitivities in nonlinear settings. Moreover, the resulting hierarchical geometries are often overly complex and lack macroscopically meaningful features. To overcome these issues, we propose an evolutionary de-homogenization framework that couples MultiFidelity Topology Design with a phasor-based de-homogenization technique. The framework translates low-dimensional geometric descriptors into explicit, CAD-compatible high-resolution structures through a hybrid evolutionary algorithm integrating NSGA-II selection, PCA-VAE-enabled latent-space crossover, and a novel image-deformation-based mutation operator. This gradient-free approach supports multi-objective refinement through non-differentiable geometry processing and body-fitted high-fidelity analysis. The numerical studies show design trade-offs for stiffness, stress, and buckling objectives; the intended scope is design-set exploration rather than universal computational superiority over full-scale gradient-based optimization.

Rock Mechanics and Rock Engineering

Yield Strength Determination Based on Dissipative Energetic Transition: Theory and Validation on Rock Analogue

Authors: Martin Lesueur, Xinrui Zhang, Hadrien Rattez, Tomasz Hueckel

Institution: Delft University of Technology, UCLouvain, Duke University

Published: 2026-11-11

Journal: Rock Mechanics and Rock Engineering

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Mechanical yield strength is one of the most crucial rock properties to consider to remain operating underground in safe elastic regimes. Despite its importance, it remains ambiguous to measure as different disciplines acknowledge different definitions. Most common examples include the deviation from linear elasticity or the peak stress. This difference has significant consequences for the modelling of non-linear deformations since the yield point is the foundation of most plasticity models. We seek to find a criterion that is not empirical in comparison to most but based instead on energetic considerations. Indeed, the transition from elastic to plastic regime corresponds to a transition of energy dissipation. The energy potential we introduce for our new definition of the yield is based on equilibrium theory which states that instability happens when the second derivative of potential energy goes to zero. Practically, we measure this point in time where the maximum of the derivative of the mechanical work is found. The method is examined for numerical simulations and experiments on 3D-printed idealised rock samples. It shows similarities to the traditional offset method in uniaxial compression but is more accurate and differs at high confinements in the compaction cap. It exhibits consistency with the other energetic yield criterion of Gurson. In the future, we aim to make it a new experimental standard for mechanical testing on rocks.
