Automated compliance checking in AEC in the era of AI and LLMs: A review (2022–2025)

Authors: Youssef Senousy, Franco Cheung, Thomas Beach, Ogerta Elezaj, Edlira Vakaj

Institution: Birmingham City University, Cardiff University

Published: 2026-08-04

Journal: Automation in Construction

Method: Review / Survey

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Since 2022, advances in Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Large Language Models (LLMs) have reshaped Automated Compliance Checking (ACC) in AEC. This review applies an AI-driven PRISMA workflow combining LLM-assisted discovery and screening with transparent provenance and audit trails. Studies are mapped to five ACC pipeline stages: rule interpretation, model preparation, rule execution, reporting, and decision support. Iterative coding identifies ten cross-cutting themes used as analytical lenses: Multimodal Information Extraction, Formalisation of Regulatory Text, Semantic Alignment with BIM/IFC, Integration of Ontologies and Knowledge Graphs, Rule Representation and Reasoning, Model-Driven Compliance Intelligence, Tool Development and Real-World Application, Explainability and Trust in AI Systems, Human-in-the-Loop Approaches, Evaluation and Benchmarking. The analysis examines their presence across stages, highlights the rise of LLM-assisted rule discovery, and identifies assurance practices. The review presents a stage-based gap analysis, an evidence-based agenda for interpretable, auditable, multimodal ACC, and a reproducible method for maintaining a living review over time.

Experimental study and numerical simulation on seismic performance of a self-centering deformation-amplified torsional steel tube damper

Authors: De-bin Wang, De-bin Wang, Yuan-Ke Zhu, Wenming Wang

Institution: Dalian Jiaotong University, Shandong Jianzhu University

Published: 2026-08-04

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

To address the limitations of conventional metallic dampers, which exhibit difficulty in yielding under small deformations and significant residual deformation under large deformations, this paper proposes a novel self-centering deformation-amplified torsional steel tube damper (SC-DATSTD). The structural configuration, working mechanism, and assembly process of the proposed damper are described in detail. Quasi-static cyclic loading tests were conducted to investigate its hysteretic behavior, with a focus on key performance indicators, including failure mode, load-bearing characteristics, energy dissipation efficiency, and self-centering capability. Furthermore, a refined finite element model was developed. Experimental results indicate that the hysteresis curves exhibit a distinct asymmetric flag-shaped pattern. Reducing the initial amplification angle effectively enhances both the deformation and load-bearing amplification factors of the energy-dissipation system, significantly improves its energy-dissipation performance under low displacement levels, and increases the equivalent damping ratio by up to 43.3%, while maintaining stable self-centering capacity. Numerical simulation results show good agreement with experimental data, with maximum load errors across all loading stages remaining within 10% and nearly identical tension-compression load ratios. The reduction in the initial amplification angle leads to markedly increased plastic deformation in the energy-dissipation steel tubes, consistent with experimentally observed energy dissipation trends, thereby confirming that the finite element model accurately captures the working mechanism and hysteretic behavior of the damper.
