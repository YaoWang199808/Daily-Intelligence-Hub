Investigation of high-temperature tribological behaviour of Al-7075/TiO2 composites

Authors: Shadab Ahmad, Fengjun Chen, J. Ding, Yebing Tian, Shanay Rab

Institution: Wenzhou University, Shandong University of Technology, University of Brighton

Published: 2026-08-23

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Aluminium-based metal matrix composites (MMCs) are widely used in high-temperature engineering applications, yet their tribological performance under varying thermal and mechanical conditions remains inadequately understood. This study investigates the high-temperature dry sliding behaviour of stir-cast Al-7075/TiO 2 composites and integrates artificial neural network (ANN) modelling with improved grey wolf optimiser (I-GWO)-based multi-objective optimisation. Tribological experiments were conducted over a temperature range of 50–250°C, sliding speeds of 0.94–2.20 m s⁻ 1 , normal loads of 20–60 N, and TiO 2 reinforcement contents of 2–10 wt% to evaluate the specific wear rate (SWR) and coefficient of friction (COF). The experimental results revealed distinct temperature-dependent wear transitions. Mild abrasive wear dominated at temperatures below 100°C for composites containing 2–4 wt% TiO 2 , whereas mixed abrasive–adhesive wear became predominant between 150 and 250°C. Severe adhesive–abrasive wear accompanied by matrix softening and particle pull-out was observed at 250°C under higher loads and TiO 2 contents ≥ 8 wt%. ANN models with selected architectures of 4–9-6–1 and 4–10-6–1 were employed as surrogate interpolation models to represent the nonlinear relationships between the process parameters and tribological responses within the investigated experimental domain. The ANN-assisted I-GWO analysis computationally identified candidate operating conditions within the investigated design space, with controlled temperatures and moderate TiO 2 contents showing potentially favourable model-estimated trade-offs between wear and friction. The Pareto-optimal solutions illustrate the trade-off between SWR and COF and provide computational guidance for selecting candidate operating conditions for subsequent experimental assessment. The combined experimental, ANN-based surrogate modelling, and I-GWO analysis approach provides a framework for interpreting the tribological behaviour of Al-7075/TiO 2 composites and identifying candidate conditions for subsequent experimental assessment.

Portable Low-Impact Surface Hardness Testing of Mineral Materials: An Integrative Review of Applications, Interpretation, and Methodological Considerations

Authors: Katrin Wilhelm, Davide Gulotta, Beril Biçer-Simşir, A. G. Corkum, Julie Désarnaud, Heather Viles

Institution: University of Oxford, Getty Conservation Institute, Dalhousie University, Royal Institute for Cultural Heritage

Published: 2026-08-24

Journal: Rock Mechanics and Rock Engineering

Method: Review / Survey

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Abstract This integrative review critically examines portable dynamic low-impact surface hardness (LISH) testing, also known as Leeb hardness. Since its adaptation for mineral materials in 1993, LISH has been increasingly adopted across geoscience, engineering, and heritage conservation as a portable, minimally invasive alternative or complementary method to traditional methods, such as the Schmidt Hammer. Despite this increased use, methodological knowledge remains fragmented across disciplines, with operational limitations and data interpretation challenges frequently under-reported. To address this, the review combined a semi-systematic literature review ( n = 181 publications) with a cross-and interdisciplinary user survey ( n = 50) to identify key strengths, including utility, versatility, and suitability for weathering and conservation assessments, while critically addressing challenges related to surface heterogeneity and porosity, as well as calibration and standardisation , thereby providing a novel integrative framework that captures both published evidence and practitioner-derived insights . Furthermore, this review identifies opportunities to extend the use of LISH to heterogeneous and layered materials, surface–biological interactions, and more systematic integration within existing monitoring frameworks. The review encourages transparent reporting of protocols, integration within multi-analytical frameworks, and the development of standardised procedures to enhance cross-study comparability and support informed adoption across research and practice.

Analytical Assessment of Far-Field Thermo-Poroelastic Response and Thermally Induced Failure Potential in the Host Rock Around Underground Radioactive Waste Repositories

Authors: Maria Anthi, Georg Anagnostou

Institution: Union Bank of Switzerland, ETH Zurich

Published: 2026-08-24

Journal: Rock Mechanics and Rock Engineering

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Abstract The decay heat generated by high-level radioactive waste increases the temperature and leads to pore pressure buildup in the surrounding host rock. This so-called thermal pressurization may extend well beyond the excavation-damaged zone (EDZ) in the low-permeability formations typically considered for underground disposal and may lead to tensile or shear failure, thereby compromising the long-term integrity of the geological barrier. This study presents closed-form solutions for assessing the thermo-hydro-mechanical response of the host rock beyond the EDZ, far from the individual emplacement drifts (“far field”), based on a simplified one-dimensional model assuming linear material behavior. The applicability of the derived analytical solutions for practical design purposes is evaluated through comparative two-dimensional numerical analyses covering three representative European repository cases and avoiding several of the simplifying assumptions of the analytical model. The results show good agreement between analytical and numerical predictions of temperature, pore pressure, and stress evolution, particularly in the far-field region of interest. Using a 10% deviation criterion for assessing one-dimensionality, the thermal response in the three representative cases becomes practically one-dimensional no later than 75 years after waste emplacement and at distances above emplacement level not exceeding approximately one-fourth of the drift spacing. The excess pore pressure field becomes one-dimensional earlier. The potential for tensile and shear failure is assessed a posteriori by applying the respective failure criteria to the elastically computed trial stress state; post-failure behavior is outside the scope of the formulation. The main deviations between the analytical and numerical predictions occur in the near field, where multidimensional effects and excavation-induced plastic yielding are relevant and lie outside the intended scope of the formulation. The proposed solutions therefore provide a physically transparent and computationally efficient tool for assessing thermally induced hydro-mechanical effects under low-permeability, conduction-dominated far-field conditions and for supporting design assessments, sensitivity analyses, and the verification of numerical models in underground waste disposal applications.
