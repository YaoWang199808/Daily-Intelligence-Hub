Fire tests on RC columns jacketed by FRP with a silicon-based matrix

Authors: Mohamed Elshorbagi, R. S. Krishna, Zhong Tao, Cheng Jiang

Institution: Western Sydney University

Published: 2026-08-08

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This paper presents an experimental study conducted to investigate the feasibility of implementing an inorganic silicon-based matrix (SBM) as a matrix replacement for fibre-reinforced polymer (FRP) rehabilitation, aiming to address the dilemma of poor thermal performance associated with conventional FRP rehabilitation schemes. The presented work utilises typical carbon fibre sheets to demonstrate the feasibility of its implementation without the need for fibre mesh, unlike conventional approaches used with inorganic matrices (i.e., due to lower fibre-matrix compatibility) when replacing polymeric systems to enhance thermal response. Nine full-scale RC columns were tested under different conditions to validate the performance of SBM FRP and assess its practicality in fulfilling structural requirements at both ambient and fire conditions. The results revealed that the column with the SBM FRP scheme can match up to 84% of the capacity of a conventional epoxy-based FRP jacketed column at ambient conditions due to relatively lower matrix-to-fibre compatibility. The superior performance of the columns jacketed with SBM FRP, along with a relatively thin layer of insulation, was confirmed after surviving a 7-hour fire exposure at various load levels. The presented results are unprecedented, as no FRP system in the literature has been reported to withstand such exposure durations and load levels. Conversely, conventional FRP schemes typically fail prematurely due to their low glass transition temperature ( T g ) and the fact that they ignite in the presence of oxygen. The enhancement in the mechanical performance of the SBM FRP scheme for post-heating and fire conditions is primarily attributed to its high thermal stability and fibre-matrix compatibility. The results confirmed the promising potential of the proposed novel SBM FRP scheme as a viable alternative to traditional polymeric FRP schemes for practical use under ambient and fire conditions.
