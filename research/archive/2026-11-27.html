Vision-guided robotic disassembly of reusable steel structures with bolted connections

Authors: Carlos Santos, Ana Claudia Carvalho, Ana Claudia Carvalho, Ricardo Carvalho, Ricardo Correia, Sree Sabari Shunmugam, Carlos Alberto da Silva Rebelo

Institution: University of Coimbra, Instituto de Telecomunicações, Universidade Politécnica de Viseu, University of Aveiro

Published: 2026-11-26

Journal: Automation in Construction

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Robotic automation can improve safety and efficiency in steel-structure deconstruction by reducing human involvement in repetitive and unsafe disassembly operations. However, robotic technologies for automating the disassembly of steel structures targeted for reuse remain limited. To address this gap, this paper presents the development of a vision-guided semi-autonomous robotic system for unscrewing operations by integrating an industrial robot, a custom pneumatic nut-runner, a machine vision system, and a Digital Twin. The object-detection models namely, YOLOv5, YOLOv8, YOLOv9, and YOLOv11 were evaluated for reliable bolt detection. YOLOv8 achieved the best overall performance, with an mAP50 of 0.9287 and an mAP50–95 of 0.6916. The Digital Twin was developed for path planning, control, and virtual assessment of the robotic system's adaptability to two additional construction scenarios involving screwing operation. The integrated system was experimentally demonstrated on a prototype steel structure, confirming the feasibility of automated unscrewing with successful unscrewing operation with no observed damage.

Multi-objective optimization and experimental validation of an ultrasonic plate sonotrode for needle-punching applications

Authors: Christian Mirz, Johannes Bolk, Jan-Lukas Archut, Chengrui Wan, Jonas Broening, Bernd A. Markert, Thomas Gries, Burkhard J. Corves

Institution: RWTH Aachen University

Published: 2026-11-01

Journal: Ultrasonics

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

The application of ultrasonic vibrations is a well-established technique in manufacturing technologies for reducing friction and process forces. A novel field for the application of ultrasonic assistance is the manufacturing of nonwoven textiles. While high fiber–fiber friction is desirable in the final product, low friction during processing is advantageous. Reducing fiber–fiber friction by introducing ultrasonic vibrations through a surface radiator (plate sonotrode), for example during needle-punching, may decrease the risk of material damage and wear on machine components. A central challenge in this approach is the design and tuning of the vibrating components, particularly when an existing machine element is proposed to be used as a plate sonotrode. These components must be engineered to introduce ultrasonic oscillations at the desired location, frequency, and amplitude. Against this background, this paper presents a systematic approach for the design of rectangular plate sonotrodes for ultrasonic applications, with a focus on their integration into needle-punching processes for nonwoven textiles. The methodology combines parametric finite element analysis with a multi-objective optimization framework for a stripper plate intended to operate as a plate sonotrode with predefined vibrational characteristics. These include resonance at a predefined target frequency and similarity to a target mode shape, which is measured and verified using the Modal Assurance Criterion (MAC). To avoid mode superposition or modal coupling, the target mode is required to be sufficiently separated in natural frequency from neighboring vibration modes with similar shapes. To this end, a novel optimization index is introduced. The proposed design approach is validated by the design and subsequent manufacturing of an exemplary plate sonotrode. A nominal frequency of 2 0 k H z and a vibration amplitude of 2 5 µ m were selected based on prior studies indicating effective friction reduction during textile processing. Experimental validation was carried out on a dedicated test bench. The measured resonance characteristics and associated mode shapes show strong agreement with the simulation results, thereby confirming the accuracy and effectiveness of the proposed design methodology.

Empirical Temperature-Dependent Failure Criterion: An Application to a Quartz-Rich Hořice-Type Marine Sandstone

Authors: Marek Brabec, Lukáš Krmíček, Daniela Marková, Radomír Sokolář

Institution: Brno University of Technology

Published: 2026-11-24

Journal: Rock Mechanics and Rock Engineering

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Effect of elevated temperature can severely alter the mechanical properties of geomaterials, both in case of manmade structures and natural geological formations. Sandstones are among the most typical Central European building stones, necessitating a thermomechanical study. Hořice-type quartz-rich sandstone from Upper Cretaceous was selected as a representative rock type for this experiment. Uniaxial and triaxial tests were carried out on specimens fired up to 1600 °C, presenting a unique dataset. The unconfined strength continuously decreases to a negligible value at 1400 °C, followed by a recovery to nearly its initial strength at 1600 °C. The trend is much simpler under confinement, with only slight weakening towards the highest temperature. The test results were then used for calibration of an empirical temperature-dependent failure criterion. The Hoek–Brown criterion addresses the confinement-induced strengthening. Contrary to the previous approaches (thermal damage or GSI-softening), this approach assumes that the rock mass after heat exposure undergoes a thermally induced microstructural reorganisation, directly altering the intact rock parameters σ ci and m i. Both parameters are then controlled with temperature-dependent empirical equations to obtain the model. Geological strength index is, therefore, reserved for description of the macroscopic discontinuity formation instead. This approach is also applicable to rocks, which strengthen during the firing (including sandstones), providing an alternative way to capture strengthening behaviour not addressed by previous approaches. Comparison of the obtained data with other similar sandstones also suggests a possible generalisation of the behaviour for the Upper Cretaceous quartz-rich sandstone group.
