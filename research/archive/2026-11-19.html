State and boundary condition estimation in pipeline networks using a two-stage sliding-mode framework

Authors: Morteza Imani, Aaron Carlo Zecchin, Wei Zeng, Martin Francis Lambert

Institution: The University of Adelaide

Published: 2026-11-18

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Reliable monitoring of pressurized pipeline systems requires estimates of hydraulic states and boundary quantities that are not directly measured. This study develops a two-stage observer framework for simultaneous reconstruction of internal hydraulic states and specified unknown boundary inputs in pipe networks. The Elastic Water Column Model (EWCM) is linearized about a steady operating point to obtain a control-oriented state-space model. When the observer matching condition is not satisfied, measured pressure outputs are augmented with estimated derivatives. A High-Order Sliding Mode (HOSM) differentiator reconstructs the required auxiliary outputs, which are supplied to a first-order Sliding Mode Observer (SMO) for state and unknown-input estimation. Numerical evaluation considers an 18-node, 24-pipe, 40-state network with two unknown demands and a 164-pipe, 277-state network with three unknown demands. In both cases, derivatives through fourth order are required, and direct noise-free comparisons with a High-Gain Observer (HGO) are used to isolate the effect of derivative depth. Test Case 1 also compares linear and nonlinear EWCM reference responses and examines simultaneous ±10% variations in wave speed and Darcy–Weisbach friction factor while the observer remains nominal. Laboratory testing on a branched copper-pipe rig provides boundary-head reconstruction from internal pressure measurements using recorded pressure data.

Multi-scale wavelet Kolmogorov–Arnold networks with adaptive feature refinement for cross-speed spur gearbox fault diagnosis

Authors: Rajeev Kumar, Zhigang Tian, Ming Jian Zuo

Institution: University of Alberta, Fuyao University of Science and Technology

Published: 2026-11-18

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Other

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Accurate spur gearbox fault diagnosis across different rotational speeds remains challenging because gear-mesh and sideband components are shifted and the time–frequency distribution of fault-related vibration signatures is modified by rotational-speed variations, producing distribution shifts between training and deployment conditions. A Multi-Scale Wavelet Kolmogorov–Arnold Network (MWKAN) is proposed for direct cross-speed classification of controlled gear-health states. Complementary discrete and continuous wavelet representations are integrated and processed through multi-kernel convolution, progressive hierarchical feature fusion, KAN-based channel refinement, and a B-spline KAN classifier. Physics-guided auxiliary supervision based on vibration-energy and impulsiveness statistics is further incorporated during training so that signal-relevant information is retained within the shared representation. The framework is evaluated on the XJTU spur gearbox dataset under within-speed and bidirectional cross-speed transfer conditions between 15 and 20 Hz, without target-speed data being used for model development, and further validated on an independent RRL gear-crack dataset. Mean within-speed accuracies of 99.56 ± 0.21% and 99.77 ± 0.18% are obtained at 15 and 20 Hz, respectively, while direct cross-speed accuracies of 96.16 ± 0.48% and 98.46 ± 0.31% are achieved for transfer from 15 to 20 Hz and from 20 to 15 Hz, respectively. On the independent RRL dataset, cross-speed accuracies of 92.3 ± 1.28% and 94.2 ± 1.01% were obtained for the corresponding transfer directions. Complementary analysis of the learned representations and model behavior is provided through stage-wise feature separation analysis, gear-mesh-frequency-domain analysis, Grad-CAM localization, component-wise ablation, and controlled-noise evaluation. The independent RRL evaluation further supports methodological consistency across the investigated datasets, while the observed directional transfer asymmetry is associated with operating-speed-dependent signal characteristics.

A multimodal wearable human-robot interaction interface for construction robotics: EMG physiological and IMU motion sensor fusion with a gated transformer-temporal convolution neural network architecture

Authors: Oscar Poudel, Rayan H. Assaad

Institution: New Jersey Institute of Technology

Published: 2026-11-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Existing human–robot interaction (HRI) approaches—largely dependent on vision-based tracking or manual controllers—often struggle with occlusions, setup complexity, and high operator burden, limiting their effectiveness in dynamic and safety–critical construction environments. This paper introduces a multimodal wearable HRI interface that enables seamless, gesture-driven robot control by directly capturing human intent through the fusion of physiological and motion signals. At the core is MYONIC (Myo ‘n’ Inertial Controller), a custom-designed wearable HRI hardware device integrating electromyography (EMG) and inertial measurement unit (IMU) sensing to monitor hand and wrist activity. Building on this multimodal sensing capability, a hybrid gated transformer-temporal convolution neural network deep learning architecture was developed that not only learns modality-specific representations but also fuses them to achieve robust, real-time intent inference that supports both discrete gesture recognition and continuous robot pose control. Results demonstrated strong performance of the developed system, achieving 93% accuracy with high precision of around 94% for gesture classification, outperforming unimodal baselines, while maintaining low positional and orientation errors of around 0.039 m and 8.1°, respectively, and minimal temporal jitter during continuous control tasks. The developed HRI system was tested and validated by carrying out various closed-loop robot-control experiments representative of different construction tasks and workflows, including robot-controlled demolition, welding, pick-and-place activities/operations, and conduit alignment and placement, demonstrating its practical viability in construction-representative simulation and physical-robot scenarios. This research advances the body-of-knowledge in HRI interfaces through multimodal AI-driven EMG-IMU fusion-based human intent recognition, enabling both discrete gesture classification and continuous robot pose control within a unified framework.

Scalarized Pareto workflow for high-fidelity multi-criteria optimization of RC and composite frame buildings

Authors: Iván Negrin, Dirk Roose, Víctor Yepes

Institution: Universitat Politècnica de València, KU Leuven

Published: 2026-11-17

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Numerical Simulation

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This study presents Scalarized Pareto Optimization (SPO), a workflow for computationally expensive multi-criteria design optimization using high-fidelity simulations. SPO integrates established scalarization and single-objective search concepts into structured sequential searches that reuse previously evaluated designs through a cumulative database for initialization, normalization, and Pareto extraction. The framework is applied to conventional reinforced concrete (RC) frames and composite systems combining RC columns with transversely hybrid variable-section (THVS) steel girders, optimizing life-cycle environmental impact and constructability, with THVS girder deflection as a third objective for the composite typology. Performance is assessed against PESA-II and SPEA2 under fixed budgets. Based on three independent high-fidelity optimization runs per method and case study, the adapted SPO strategy reduced the number of structural evaluations by about 50% in the two-objective RC problems while achieving higher hypervolume (HV) values than the best benchmark under the adopted reference points. In the three-objective RC–THVS problems, computational savings were less consistent, but SPO achieved higher HV values, reaching over twice the benchmark values in several cases. These savings apply only to the final high-fidelity optimization stage and exclude the offline surrogate development cost. Structurally, RC–THVS solutions in the environmentally driven Pareto region reduced life-cycle environmental impact by about 20–28% relative to RC solutions for the investigated cases and assumptions, highlighting the composite system’s environmental potential. Minimizing THVS deflection as an objective also strongly penalized environmental and constructability performance. Overall, SPO provides a competitive workflow for targeted high-fidelity structural optimization within the evaluated algorithms, budgets, and case studies.

New Multistep HST Model for the Analysis of Time-Dependent Displacements of Arch Dams. Part 1: Formulation and Validation

Authors: Edoardo Lusini, Alessandro Graziani, Daniela Boldini

Institution: Sapienza University of Rome, Roma Tre University

Published: 2026-11-17

Journal: Rock Mechanics and Rock Engineering

Method: Analytical / Theoretical

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Abstract The paper presents an improved multistep statistical model (mHHSTT) for the analysis of monitoring data of arch dams, with a particular focus on displacement time series. Building upon the classical Hydro-Seasonal-Time (HST) model, the proposed approach progressively introduces nonlinear predictors within a constrained regression framework. This enables the model to capture delayed thermal and visco-elastic effect responses to changes in air temperature and reservoir level, while reducing the risk of overfitting and preserving the physical interpretability of the regression model. The framework is further extended to highly nonlinear time series exhibiting plateau-like responses, such as those associated with joint closure, by combining weighted regression models, each representing a distinct range of homogeneous structural behaviour. The paper also provides a stepwise guideline for the practical application of the model that can be readily implemented using a standard spreadsheet. The proposed methodology is validated using the arch dam owned by Électricité de France (EDF), which served as the benchmark case for the 2022 ICOLD International Benchmark Workshop (Theme A). The analysis of the measures of two pendulums and a short-base extensometer highlights significant improvements in both the explanatory and predictive performance of the basic HST model. Although the study focuses on a single, representative case, the generality of the proposed methodology is investigated in a companion paper through application to additional dams and monitoring instruments. Beyond enhancing predictive accuracy, the proposed strategy offers insights into the constitutive behaviour of dam-foundation systems, thereby supporting the development of more physically informed models in the context of dam safety assessment.
