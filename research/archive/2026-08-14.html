Graph-based database structure and calculation framework for automated dynamic life cycle assessment of buildings

Authors: Chujun Zong, Farzan Banihashemi, Frank Petzold, Werner Lang

Institution: Birn (Germany), Max Planck Computing and Data Facility, Technical University of Munich

Published: 2026-08-12

Journal: Automation in Construction

Method: Numerical Simulation

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Dynamic life cycle assessment (DLCA) captures temporal changes for construction sector decision-making but faces challenges in modeling dynamics affecting embodied emissions, integrating multiple interacting factors, and providing transparent automated calculation methodologies. This paper introduces GraphDLCA, a tool embedding a building-sector DLCA database and, once the input parameters are defined, providing an automated calculation workflow for building DLCA. GraphDLCA supports automated hierarchical impact calculations from material to district. Four background dynamic factors are modeled: dynamic environmental impact calculation, dynamic import ratios, dynamic circular recycling/remanufacture, and dynamic electricity mix at three voltage levels. A proof of concept at different levels reveals that circular recycling/remanufacture lowers embodied impacts, while import ratios exert upward pressure. The dynamic electricity mix has limited influence on embodied emissions but pronounced effects on operational emissions from heating. GraphDLCA’s extensible architecture and transparent calculation logic provide a transferable foundation for future DLCA research and integration with digital construction workflows.

Full-field full-stress-tensor identification of base-excited structures

Authors: Jaša Šonc, Klemen Zaletelj, Janko Slavič

Institution: University of Ljubljana

Published: 2026-08-12

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Identifying the full-stress tensor over the surface of a vibrating structure is essential for design validation and vibration-fatigue-life estimation, yet current experimental methods provide only pointwise or incomplete stress data. Infrared cameras can provide non-contact, full-field surface-stress measurements on vibrating structures via the thermoelastic effect; however, they measure only the first stress invariant ( i.e. , the sum of the normal stresses at the free surface: σ x x + σ y y ). The individual stress components ( σ x x , σ y y , τ x y ) required for a complete characterisation of the surface-stress state remain inaccessible from a single thermoelastic measurement. This article introduces a method that recovers the full-field, in-plane-stress power spectral density (PSD) field from thermoelastic camera data on a base-excited structure, demonstrated for thin, isotropic, metallic, plate-like structures under adiabatic conditions. First, a characterisation measurement identifies the hybrid stress-mode shapes containing all the plane-stress components by merging thermoelastic camera data with a simplified FE model through SEMM. Then, during operation under broadband random base excitation, the camera response is projected onto these mode shapes and expanded to the full-stress-tensor PSD. The method is validated experimentally on a clamped aluminium plate, identifying spatially resolved stress PSDs for all three in-plane components ( σ x x , σ y y , τ x y ). The full-field first invariant σ x x + σ y y is measured experimentally; the individual components σ x x , σ y y and τ x y are reconstructed through an experimentally corrected modal expansion informed by an FE stress basis, which supplies the inter-component ratios.

Live reconstruction in ultra-fast dynamic computed tomography

Authors: Qianwei Qu, Luis Barba, Christian M. Schlepütz, Leonardo Hax Damiani, Benjamín Béjar Haro, M. Janousch, Goran Lovrić

Institution: Paul Scherrer Institute

Published: 2026-08-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Ultra-fast dynamic X-ray computed tomography (CT) enables time-resolved three-dimensional imaging of rapidly evolving processes, but its full potential is often constrained by the lack of real-time reconstruction and analysis capabilities. Modern high-speed detectors generate projection data at rates that far exceed the throughput of conventional three-dimensional reconstruction pipelines, posing significant challenges in data transmission, computation, and memory management. In this work, we present a live reconstruction and processing framework designed for ultra-fast dynamic CT experiments. The proposed framework integrates a high-speed data transfer module implemented using Remote Direct Memory Access, a multi-stage CUDA stream design, and a GPU-accelerated analytical reconstruction algorithm to bridge the gap between high-rate data acquisition and real-time volumetric imaging. By partitioning large volumes into subvolumes and carefully orchestrating host-to-device transfers, kernel execution, and device-to-host transfers, the framework achieves sustained high throughput while avoiding GPU memory exhaustion. Benchmark results demonstrate that the proposed framework can continuously process large volumes at projection consumption rates compatible with modern X-ray detectors exceeding 10 GB/s. These results indicate that the proposed system provides a practical and scalable solution for live three-dimensional reconstruction in ultra-fast dynamic CT experiments.

Experimental study of size effect on shear-controlled hollow clay brick unreinforced masonry walls under in-plane shear-compression loading

Authors: Ernesto Inzunza Araya, Savvas Saloustros, Katrin Beyer

Institution: École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne

Published: 2026-08-12

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Drift capacity of unreinforced masonry walls (URM) is a key parameter for seismic assessment and design. Although previous studies have established its dependence on wall size for flexural and hybrid failure modes, this relationship remains unclear for walls failing in shear. This study investigates how wall size influences the in-plane drift capacity of hollow clay brick URM walls in walls with shear-controlled behaviour. Six quasi-static shear-compression tests were conducted on walls of three heights (1.4 m, 2.0 m and 2.8 m), with the same aspect ratio, material properties and boundary conditions, and with two levels of compression load. Results show that small walls exhibit significantly higher drift capacity than medium and large walls, supporting previous recommendations to adopt a minimum testing height of 1.5 m when deriving drift capacities from experiments. From a mechanical perspective, the ratio of shear-to-flexural displacement was found similar across the wall sizes, indicating it is not responsible for the observed drift differences. Digital Image Correlation measurements revealed that at a given drift, crack widths were smaller in small walls, while at a given limit state, crack widths were similar across all sizes. This suggests that crack width could be used as a size-independent indicator of limit state attainment, despite the challenges associated with quantifying this parameter in certain numerical models. Future research should examine the applicability of these findings to other masonry typologies.

Smoke control in tunnels with various water-based fire suppression systems

Authors: Ying Zhen Li, Haukur Ingason

Institution: RISE Research Institutes of Sweden

Published: 2026-08-12

Journal: Tunnelling and Underground Space Technology

Method: Other

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Medium scale tunnel fire tests (1:3) were carried out to investigate smoke control in tunnels with various water-based fire suppression systems. The critical velocity decreases as the water density and water pressure increase, and it also approximately follows the 1/3 power of the heat release rate for small fires but becomes independent for large fires. The momentum of the water sprays is found to play a key role in the critical velocity. A calculation model for critical velocity is proposed to cover all tunnel scenarios, i.e., with and without water-based fire suppression systems. The smoke backlayering behaves differently within and beyond the water spray region. Within the water spray region, the backlayering length is less sensitive to the longitudinal ventilation velocity, i.e., a large reduction in velocity results in only a slight increase in the backlayering length. Beyond the water spray zone, the variation of the backlayering length with velocity approximately follows the same trend as for the tunnel without water-based fire suppression systems. Calculation models for the backlayering length both within and beyond the water spray region are proposed. Comparisons of the calculation models with data from various large scale tunnel fire tests and medium scale tests show reasonably good agreement for both models.
