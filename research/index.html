A low-cost hybrid RFID-WiFi RTLS for industrial batch tracking and legacy ERP integration: a case study in the wood industry

Authors: José Abreu

Institution: Universidade Politécnica de Viana do Castelo

Published: 2026-11-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

• Hybrid RTLS detected 99.1% of 660 audited movements. • CCTV-referenced error ≤0.5 m: 55.0% hybrid vs 3.8% WiFi-only samples. • Manual-log errors occurred in 4.2% pre- and 0.8% post-deployment records. • Eight project-specific WiFi APs support RFID confirmation at ten loading lines. • Modbus ASCII integration links legacy measurement equipment to the ERP. The digital transformation of small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) is constrained by limited budgets, legacy information systems, and harsh production environments. This paper presents a low-cost hybrid WiFi + RFID real-time location system (RTLS) for generating auditable movement events and integrating them with a legacy ERP. The architecture combines RSSI-based positioning, discrete RFID landmark confirmation, adaptive polling, and transaction-safe synchronization. In a six-month, single-site deployment, 654 of 660 audited movements were correctly detected (99.1% recall). In separate, unpaired sample-level localization datasets, 55.0% of hybrid observations and 3.8% of WiFi-only observations had CCTV-referenced error at or below 0.5 m. Because the estimated operational uncertainty of the visual reference is approximately 0.5 m, this threshold comparison is descriptive and does not establish true-position accuracy, a paired difference, or a causal effect. Manual-entry errors were observed in 4.2% of pre-deployment records and 0.8% of post-deployment records, while no new duplicate cases were observed after the RTLS-assisted validation workflow was introduced. A Modbus ASCII interface also linked legacy raw-material measurements to the ERP. The study reports operational evidence and implementation constraints for SME tracking; it does not establish a causal economic effect or universal localization performance.
