Topology-driven automated generation of geometric digital twins for railway overhead line equipment

Authors: M.R. Mahendrini Fernando Ariyachandra, Ioannis Brilakis

Institution: University College London, University of Cambridge

Published: 2026-11-16

Journal: Automation in Construction

Method: Numerical Simulation

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Generating geometric digital twins (GDTs) of railway overhead line equipment (OLE) from point cloud data (PCD) using manual or semi-automated approaches is labour-intensive. This diminishes their practical benefits, with 74% of modelling effort spent converting PCD into GDTs. Automation reduces cost and effort, yet state-of-the-art methods struggle to deliver kilometre-scale GDTs without precision loss or manual intervention. This paper presents an automated method combining topology-aware segmentation, rule-based classification, and template-driven geometric reconstruction. Leveraging engineering rules and OLE spatial relationships, it enables modelling in cluttered, partially occluded scenes. OLE elements are segmented into labelled clusters, then aligned with parametric assemblies to generate industry foundation classes (IFC) models. Evaluation on 18.5 km of PCD achieved F1 scores of 98.3% for C sections, 89.8% for contact and 84.1% for catenary cables, with ±5.6 cm RMSE. Automation saved 98.6% of time versus manual workflows. This contributes a topology-informed strategy for scalable digital twin generation.

Physiological micro-movement tracking via FMCW radars: An actuator-driven phantom setup with machine learning and image processing

Authors: Nurhan Güneş

Institution: Sivas Bilim ve Teknoloji Üniversitesi

Published: 2026-11-13

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Continuous, contactless vital sign monitoring is constrained by two persistent gaps: evaluation scenarios tied to limited cohorts of live subjects, and signal-processing pipelines that prioritize tissue velocity over the clinically valuable absolute displacement measurement. This study addresses both gaps by building an actuator-driven oil-in-gelatin phantom testbench that mimics chest-wall/skin-surface movements, and using it to test the micro-motion tracking capabilities of two Frequency-Modulated Continuous-Wave (FMCW) radar platforms working at 24 GHz and 77 GHz. A 1 cm-thick oil-in-gelatin phantom, mounted on a servo-actuation mechanism, was driven through 20 synthetic periodic motion profiles spanning 36–272 bpm equivalents while both radars, suspended 60 cm above the target, logged raw range profiles. Regressive modeling yielded promising relative tracking performance. However, direct frequency analysis of raw range bin amplitude fluctuations proved more robust for transient periodicity tracking, resolving 16 of 20 profiles, whereas the regression model, optimized on quasi-static signals, reduced the number of resolved profiles by 5. Furthermore, by conceptualizing the radar data matrix as a two-dimensional digital image, a computationally efficient pixel-wise tracking method was implemented to extract one-dimensional relative time series that faithfully preserve the temporal and morphological characteristics of the underlying signals. These outcomes validate the phantom–actuator framework as a reproducible test platform and demonstrate the feasibility of applying image processing to range-FFT matrices for advancing non-contact, high-precision clinical monitoring.

Accuracy of smartphone LiDAR scanning for dimension measurement and volume estimation of large-diameter oak (Quercus sp.) roundwood

Authors: Karol Tomczak

Institution: Łukasiewicz Research Network - Poznań Institute of Technology, Poznań University of Life Sciences

Published: 2026-11-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Accurate measurement of large-diameter roundwood volume remains a bottleneck in wood management. To date, no LiDAR-based application is in routine operational use for single-log measurement of large-diameter wood. This study evaluates consumer-grade smartphone LiDAR for measuring 50 large-diameter oak ( Quercus sp.) logs against a Newton-from-manual under-bark reference. In total, 9 vol estimation methods were compared across 3 levels of digital independence: linear dimensions, classical dendrometric formulae, and continuous mesh-based integration over 200 cross-sections. Moreover, the potential influence of scan quality, described as the number of frames and the missing underside of logs, was studied. The linear dimensions were comparable to the manual reference, with a mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) of 2.6%. In terms of volume estimation, the Huber formula based on manual measurements ranked first (MAPE 3.39%), Newton’s formula applied to smartphone-derived dimensions ranked second (MAPE 4.99%), and continuous mesh integration (Mesh-Integral) ranked fifth (MAPE 7.28%, bias −6.23%, RMSE 9.36%). Scan quality strongly affected agreement with the reference: high-quality scans (>250 frames) achieved a MAPE of 4.58% (bias −2.07%), whereas low-quality scans (<130 frames) reached a MAPE of 11.41%. The mesh-derived volume converged to within 0.1% from 75 cross-sections onward, so its bias was not a sampling effect. Instead, it was explained by the missing underside of logs scanned on the ground: the convex-hull area deficit (median 5.0%) explained 56% of the between-log variance in volume error, and with completeness in the model, the number of scan frames had no significant independent effect. Consumer-grade smartphone LiDAR therefore agreed with the Newton-manual reference under the tested conditions, with surface completeness, rather than sampling density, as its main limitation.
