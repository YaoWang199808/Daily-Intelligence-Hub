Life cycle assessment of a material-efficient 3D-printed concrete bridge: Comparative sustainability evaluation

Authors: K. Chiranjeevi Reddy, Shreya Mahajan, Likith Koneru, Kolluru V. L. Subramaniam

Institution: Indian Institute of Technology Hyderabad

Published: 2026-10-14

Journal: Automation in Construction

Method: Other

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Sustainable construction requires reducing both material consumption and environmental impacts. This paper presents a comprehensive cradle-to-gate life cycle assessment (LCA) of a structurally optimized 3D-printed concrete bridge incorporating a material-efficient compression-based form. The bridge is compared with a conventional reinforced-concrete slab designed to have an equivalent flexural capacity and meet code-prescribed deflection limits. The optimized 3DCP bridge reduces concrete consumption by 55% while maintaining comparable structural capacity. Using the structure as the functional unit, the LCA was conducted in accordance with ISO 14040 for stages A1–A5. Although the high cement content of printable concrete increases impacts per unit volume, the material-efficient geometry enabled by 3DCP can offset this higher binder content at the structural level, resulting in lower impacts across several categories. Scenario-based analyses further demonstrate that reducing clinker content can lower the global warming potential below that of the conventional bridge, highlighting the importance of mix optimization.

Explainable AI-integrated multi-objective optimisation for circular material routing in modular construction

Authors: Thilina Ganganath Weerakoon, Zenonas Turskis, Jūratė Šliogerienė

Institution: Vilnius Gediminas Technical University

Published: 2026-10-13

Journal: Automation in Construction

Method: Machine Learning

Citations: 0

Abstract:

The rising digitisation of the construction sector has increased the demand for automated, data-driven decision-making in BIM-enabled operations. However, material routing decisions in modular construction are still primarily manual or based on opaque optimisation models, which limits their integration with construction automation systems. To solve this, the Explainable AI-Integrated Multi-Objective Optimisation (XAIOM) framework is presented, which allows for automated material routing straight from BIM-derived data. The system combines machine learning (ML), SHAP and LIME explainability, non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm II (NSGA-II) optimisation, and Monte Carlo uncertainty analysis into a single pipeline. Explainability is built into the optimisation process to aid the development of Pareto-optimal solutions based on feature contributions. Validation of a modular residential construction in Finland shows clear trade-offs between CO₂ reduction and economic value under uncertainty. The findings confirm that the framework enables transparent, reliable, and scalable automated decision-making, advancing AI-driven construction automation by linking BIM-based digital models with intelligent optimisation workflows.

Review of AI agents in engineering design workflows

Authors: Zoie McCreery, M.Z. Naser

Institution: Clemson University

Published: 2026-10-13

Journal: Automation in Construction

Method: Review / Survey

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Technology related to artificial intelligence (AI), especially agentic systems, has attracted significant interest and rapid advancement in recent years. With this growth comes an increasing potential for integrating agents into engineering design workflows. This paper examines the current state of AI agent technology from an engineering design perspective. Descriptive bibliometric analysis characterizes recent research trends, showing rapid growth in engineering-related research despite comparatively limited publication volume. A consistent boundary for agentic behavior is established to distinguish task-specific AI and near-agentic workflow automation from systems demonstrating more direct agentic participation. Recent single- and multi-agent workflows demonstrate meaningful capabilities, although their implementation in engineering design remains limited. Contributing technical, ethical, and human-centered challenges are discussed alongside their implications for broader adoption. The technology's broader impacts on engineering are then considered, with an emphasis on the potential for agents to assume more critical roles in design workflows as these barriers are addressed.

Automated facade solar retrofits using feed-forward 3D reconstruction and generative image editing

Authors: Ayça Duran, Christoph Waibel, Bernd Bickel, Iro Armeni, Arno Schlueter

Institution: ETH Zurich, Flemish Institute for Technological Research, Stanford University

Published: 2026-10-13

Journal: Automation in Construction

Method: Review / Survey

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Building-integrated photovoltaics (BIPV) enable solar energy generation from facades, yet facade retrofit assessment remains largely manual and time-consuming. This paper introduces a pipeline for automated analysis and visualization of BIPV retrofit designs. Unlike prior studies relying on building footprints, 3D models, or camera parameters, the proposed method automatically estimates facade aspect ratios and dimensions from images using feed-forward 3D reconstruction and vision-language models. The pipeline edits facade images using pretrained generative models to visualize BIPV retrofits considering system size and provides preliminary energy-yield estimates. On two datasets covering facades from 10 cities, the best-performing configurations achieved normalized mean absolute errors of 9% and 15% in aspect ratio estimation and an average floor-count prediction error of 0.5 floors. The pipeline enables scalable evaluation of early-stage facade solar retrofit designs, supporting decision-making in retrofit planning by reducing reliance on manual surveying and modeling. The pipeline implementation and dataset are available to support future work.

In situ impact printing with an autonomous legged excavator: System development, integration, and full-scale demonstration

Authors: Kunaljit Chadha, Filippo Spinelli, Lauren Vasey, Grzegorz Malczyk, Ananya Kango, Pok Yin Victor Leung, Marco Hutter, Matthias Köhler

Institution: ETH Zurich

Published: 2026-10-13

Journal: Automation in Construction

Method: Other

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This paper presents an additive manufacturing process, Impact Printing, developed for autonomous on-site construction using in situ resources such as excavated earthen material. The process is enabled by a custom three-subsystem tool that portions and deposits dense, pre-mixed material parts at high velocity, enabling the fabrication of unreinforced architectural structures. To investigate the feasibility of in situ construction, the custom tool is integrated on a mobile autonomous legged excavator. A trajectory-following controller with iterative closest point-based state estimation is implemented to enable accurate motion, while a fabrication-aware software architecture and computational design workflow provides kinematic checking and export of fabrication parameters. The system is validated through the construction of a three-meter-tall wall demonstrator built over multiple days from multiple base positions in a semi-structured outdoor environment. Construction data is post-processed to assess system accuracy, efficiency, and influential parameters, and directions for future development are discussed.

Control-Based Continuation for self-oscillating systems: Application to flow induced vibrations

Authors: Romain Caron, Etienne Gourc, Bruno Cochelin, Fabrice Silva, Christophe Vergez

Published: 2026-10-14

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

An enhanced version of Control-Based Continuation (CBC) is proposed to carry out experimental continuation on self-sustained systems, whose working frequency is not known a priori. Our approach combines three main stages: an online frequency tracking algorithm using an embedded phase-locked loop, an online noninvasive CBC stabilization loop using adaptive filters, and a derivative free arc continuation algorithm. As a proof of concept, it is implemented to study an elementary aero-vibro-acoustical model of buzzing lips that exhibits self-sustained oscillations with a wide range of fundamental frequency. The model relies on interactions between an acoustical resonator (the air column) and an oscillating valve (the lips) through a nonlinear flow. The Control-Based Continuation is implemented without taking advantage of the modelling equations, mimicking a real experiment. This results in a pseudo-experimental bifurcation diagram, showing strong accordance with virtual open-loop testing and numerical continuation, which proves robustness of our method.

Influence of localized failures on the vibration characteristics of gear-rotor-bearing system considering gear lead crown relief

Authors: Xiangming Zhang, Lei Fu, Zhehao Fu, Songda Zhang, Jiawei Xiang

Institution: Zhejiang University of Technology, Wenzhou University

Published: 2026-10-14

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

The Gear-Rotor-Bearing (GRB) system plays a critical role in the transmission chains of high-end equipment systems. However, existing research on failure mechanisms tends to neglect the intrinsic coupling effects of key components, leading to inaccuracies in failure modeling and dynamic response prediction. To address these limitations, this paper proposes a novel Analytical-Finite Element Method (AFEM)-based approach for calculating the Time-Varying Meshing Stiffness (TVMS) of gear pair with spalling faults and lead crown relief. Meanwhile, a 32-Degree-of-Freedom (DOF) closed-loop iterative dynamic model is proposed, incorporating nonlinear factors such as time-varying backlash and rotor misalignment. This aims to capture the bidirectional coupling mechanism between localized bearing failures and gear surface load distribution. The study reveals the internal load transfer and contact stress variation law within the GRB system under localized failure, providing a theoretical foundation for analyzing failure mechanisms and vibration control in complex transmission systems.

Cost-sensitive active learning for rotating machinery fault diagnosis under data imbalance

Authors: Ying Chen, Lubing Wang, Xufeng Zhao

Institution: Nanjing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics, Zhengzhou University

Published: 2026-10-14

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Data-driven fault diagnosis has become an important approach for ensuring the reliability and safety of rotating machinery. However, practical deployment is often hindered by severe data imbalance and label scarcity. Active learning (AL) mitigates label scarcity by selecting the most informative samples for annotation to improve model performance. Nevertheless, conventional AL strategies typically optimize for overall accuracy and treat all misclassifications equally. Consequently, they fail to account for the asymmetric operational costs of diagnostic errors, particularly the greater losses caused by missed detections and severity underestimation. To bridge this gap, this paper proposes a cost-sensitive active learning (CS-AL) framework tailored for highly imbalanced fault distributions. In this framework, raw vibration signals are first transformed into two-dimensional time–frequency representations via continuous wavelet transform (CWT). A Bayesian convolutional neural network, integrated with a convolutional block attention module, is developed to extract fault-sensitive features, while epistemic uncertainty is quantified through Monte Carlo (MC) dropout. To explicitly penalize high-risk errors during sample selection, an asymmetric diagnostic cost matrix is constructed to formulate a cost-sensitive acquisition score based on the expected pairwise misclassification cost. To optimize annotation efficiency, a diversity-aware clustering mechanism selects representative prototypes from this high-risk candidate pool. Experimental validations on the CWRU bearing and MCC5-THU gearbox datasets demonstrate that the CS-AL framework achieves enhanced diagnostic performance and significantly reduces high-cost misclassification risks compared to representative AL baselines. The results confirm the effectiveness of CS-AL in accurately allocating limited labeling resources to rare, safety-critical faults while minimizing total operational risk under imbalanced conditions.

Modeling of AMR magnetic encoder signals for instantaneous angular speed and vibration separation

Authors: Zhengde Chu, François Guillet, Maolin Luo, Yu Guo, Baoping Cai, Hugo André

Institution: Université Jean Monnet, Chongqing University of Posts and Telecommunications, Kunming University of Science and Technology, China University of Petroleum, East China

Published: 2026-10-14

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Magnetic encoder signals are susceptible to distortions caused by small transverse vibrations and dynamic misalignments, particularly in off-shaft configurations. A novel anisotropic magnetoresistive (AMR) encoder coupling model is established, capturing both torsional and transverse displacements to reveal how dynamic vibrations affect signal formation. Based on this model, a dual-sensor reconstruction method is developed to estimate instantaneous angular speed (IAS) and transverse vibration. Simulation and experimental validation under varying encoder resolutions and multi-source vibration conditions confirm the method’s robustness and high accuracy, achieving high correlation coefficients and low normalized root mean square error (NRMSE). The approach enables accurate IAS measurement with fewer sensors and facilitates its integration into practical condition monitoring systems.

Hybrid testing of wind turbine components using Kane’s method

Authors: Frederik Nordtorp, Elif Ecem Baş, Cláudio Gomes, Per Jensen, Jan Jørgensen, Giuseppe Abbiati

Institution: Aarhus University, FORCE Technology (Denmark)

Published: 2026-10-14

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Hybrid testing combines numerical simulations with physical experiments to evaluate large mechanical systems, such as wind turbines, that are impractical to test at full scale. While hybrid testing has been widely applied in structural dynamics, its extension to complex multibody dynamic systems remains challenging due to nonlinear kinematics and strongly coupled degrees of freedom. Although Kane’s method underpins widely used wind turbine simulation tools, hybrid testing frameworks that directly integrate Kane-based multibody formulations and interface force coupling remain largely unexplored. Here, a stepwise hybrid testing framework is presented in which the equations of motion are formulated using Kane’s method, enabling a systematic treatment of multibody dynamics within hybrid testing. Interface forces are identified from free-body diagrams and incorporated into the equations of motion through the principle of virtual power, allowing consistent coupling between numerical and physical substructures. The framework is demonstrated in a pseudo-dynamic hybrid test of a 13-degree-of-freedom simplified wind turbine rotor system, where a pitch bearing is tested as a physical substructure under force-controlled bi-axial bending and displacement-controlled pitch rotation. A virtual hybrid test validates the formulation and coordination algorithm, while the physical hybrid test demonstrates stable mixed-mode coupling under experimental feedback. The results show that the framework can couple a Kane-based multibody model with a physical wind turbine component and capture its influence on the system-level rotor response.

Adaptive and uncertainty-aware surrogate modeling for reliability analysis of nonlinear dynamic systems

Authors: Zhi-wei Wang, Abdoul Aziz Sandotin Coulibaly, Michael D. Todd, Joel P. Conte, Zhen Yu Hu

Institution: University of Michigan, University of Michigan–Dearborn, University of California San Diego

Published: 2026-10-14

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Numerical Simulation

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Reliability analysis of nonlinear inelastic dynamic systems under uncertainty remains a critical challenge, owing to the path-dependent, high-dimensional nature of the problem. To this end, this paper proposes an adaptive and uncertainty-aware surrogate modeling framework comprising three synergistic components. The first component is the aLSTM–SNGP surrogate model, an uncertainty-aware surrogate for nonlinear dynamic systems that integrates an attention-enhanced LSTM (aLSTM) encoder with a Spectral-normalized Neural Gaussian Process (SNGP) predictor, jointly optimized through end-to-end co-training. The aLSTM encoder processes excitation sequences and system parameters together, capturing temporal dependencies while implicitly reducing dimensionality; the SNGP predictor provides scalable uncertainty-aware prediction through random Fourier feature approximation. The second component develops two surrogate-based failure probability estimation methods: Method I (aLSTM-SNGP-MCS) based on direct Monte Carlo simulation, and Method II (aLSTM-SNGP-IS) based on importance sampling with an adaptively constructed proposal density that concentrates sampling effort in failure-prone regions. The third component is an adaptive exploration–exploitation refinement strategy that iteratively refines the surrogate model by combining active learning with an alternating training scheme of full training and local fine-tuning. The framework is validated under stochastic seismic excitations through two examples: a numerical example based on a six-story nonlinear inelastic shear building model with Bouc-Wen hysteresis, and a practical engineering example based on a nine-story SAC steel frame represented by a nonlinear finite element model. Results demonstrate that both methods yield accurate failure probability estimates while significantly reducing the number of evaluations to the simulation model relative to crude Monte Carlo simulation, with Method II outperforming Method I.

Vibration signal AM-FM models and spectral characteristics for rotate vector reducer fault diagnosis: an insight into multiple modulations and fault feature analysis

Authors: Zhipeng Feng, Zi'an Li, Rongzhou Lin, Tongxin Gao, Ying Zhang

Institution: University of Science and Technology Beijing, China North Industries Group Corporation (China)

Published: 2026-10-13

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Rotate vector (RV) reducers need to undertake cyclic heavy load, and therefore are prone to faults. However, they feature highly intricate kinematics, due to their complex structure, simultaneous multiple point contacts and coupled spin–revolution of cycloidal and planetary gear stages. This leads to high vibration complexity, and poses a great challenge to vibration based fault diagnosis. To address the above issue, we derive the fault characteristic frequency equations for the main components in both cycloidal and planetary gear stages, including the sun, planets, cycloids and pinwheel. Considering the multiple modulation effects on meshing vibration (including the amplitude modulation and frequency modulation effects due to localized fault, and the extra amplitude modulation effects caused by time-varying measurement angle and vibration transfer path), we establish analytical vibration signal models for the localized fault case of each main component. These analytical signal models provide an insight into the multiple modulation nature of vibrations, and justify the presence of gear rotating and fault characteristic frequencies in vibrations from kinetics perspective. In order to extract fault symptoms from multiple modulation signals, we further derive the explicit equations of Fourier and envelope spectra, thus revealing the fault vibration characteristics explicitly. Such explicit spectral signatures provide a guidance for vibration based fault diagnosis. The above theoretical derivations are validated experimentally. The localized fault of both cycloidal and planetary gear stages is successfully diagnosed.

A multimodal fusion network guided by environmental semantics for reconstructing missing bridge strain trends

Authors: Jiaqi Liu, Weijie Li, Xuefeng Zhao, Stefano Mariani

Institution: Dalian University of Technology, Politecnico di Milano

Published: 2026-10-13

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Long term bridge strain trends describe the gradual evolution of structural responses under environmental actions and operational loads, providing important information for structural health monitoring and condition assessment. However, communication interruptions, sensor abnormalities, and unstable data transmission can lead to missing strain data, which affects the completeness of monitoring records and the reliability of subsequent analysis. This study proposes an environmental semantics-guided multimodal fusion network for reconstructing missing bridge strain trends. The network mainly consists of an environmental semantic modulation module and a temporal reconstruction module. The environmental semantic modulation module encodes environmental textual descriptions into semantic representations and introduces environmental contextual information through feature-wise semantic modulation. The temporal reconstruction module uses a one-dimensional convolutional neural network (1D CNN) to extract local trend variation features and a modified Transformer encoder to model long-range temporal dependencies, enabling environment-aware strain trend reconstruction. Based on strain data obtained from a real bridge field monitoring experiment, this study constructs several typical missing scenarios and validates the proposed method through comparison with multiple baseline methods. The results show that the proposed model can effectively recover the original strain trends under different missing conditions and outperforms the compared methods in terms of reconstruction accuracy and robustness. Ablation experiments further demonstrate the effectiveness of the environmental semantic modulation and temporal reconstruction modules.

Experimental and numerical investigations on methods for improving impact performance of precast segmental bridge piers

Authors: Yifei Hao, Pengcheng Li, Hong Shun Hao, Delei Zou, Yu Liu, Shuyou Xue

Institution: Curtin University, Guangzhou University, Hebei University of Technology, Dalian Minzu University, Zhejiang Water Conservancy and Hydropower Survey and Design Institute

Published: 2026-10-14

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Precast segmental bridge piers (PSBPs) are increasingly used in accelerated bridge construction. However, segmental joints and bottom segments are more vulnerable to severe damage under impact loads. This study experimentally and numerically investigates three retrofitting methods to improve the impact performance of PSBPs, i.e., wrapping carbon fiber reinforced polymer (CFRP) sheets around segment joints, using steel tubes to connect the bottom two segments, and adopting a cast-in-place bottom segment. The effectiveness of these methods was first verified through missile impact tests. Results indicated that wrapping CFRP sheets around joints effectively reduced concrete spalling damage within the impacted zone but led to more severe bottom shear damage due to improved integrity and joint restraint. In contrast, the use of continuous steel tubes extending through the joint to connect the two bottom segments and the cast-in-place longer bottom segment could enhance the impact performance of PSBPs by effectively activating their energy-dissipation mechanism. Numerical models of the four types of PSBPs subjected to missile impacts were then established and validated against test results using the explicit dynamic analysis software LS-DYNA. An analysis of the PSBPs subjected to off-center missile impacts was then performed. Results confirmed the effectiveness of using steel tubes to join the two bottom segments and the cast-in-place of a longer bottom segment to improve the performance of PSBPs subjected to off-center impacts, while wrapping CFRP sheets around joints weakened the impact performance of PSBPs in off-center impact scenarios. These findings provide experimental and numerical evidence to support the selection of suitable retrofitting strategies to improve the impact performance of PSBPs in complex collision scenarios.

Seismic performance of local prestressed concrete joint with interior anchorage in the core region

Authors: Qing Xu, Dong Chen, Xiaoda Xu, Bin Zeng, Qingfeng Xu

Institution: Shanghai Research Institute of Building Sciences (China), Central Research Institute of Building and Construction (China)

Published: 2026-10-14

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Local prestressing technology is widely used in large public and industrial buildings due to its flexible spatial arrangement and significant improvement of structural serviceability. However, owing to limited experimental research, the effects of asymmetry and anchorage characteristics in the core zone on the seismic performance of joints and the mechanical behavior of the core region remain unclear. This study conducted quasi-static tests on five local prestressed concrete (LPC) joints and one reference RC joint, supplemented by finite element parameter extension analysis. The influences of prestressing degree, beam depth ratio, joint stirrup ratio, and axial compression ratio on the seismic performance of the joints were investigated. The model predictions show good agreement with experimental results and collected data, with errors within ±12.1%. Parameter analysis based on this method reveals the influence patterns of various parameters on the seismic bearing capacity of LPC joints.

Experimental–inverse investigation of frictional heating and interfacial heat partition in friction pendulum bearings

Authors: Jiaxi Li, Kui Yang, Zeyu Zhu, Ping Tan

Institution: Guangzhou University, Fuyang Normal University

Published: 2026-10-14

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Frictional heating significantly influences the performance of friction pendulum bearings (FPBs), yet the mechanisms of heat generation and interfacial partition remain inadequately quantified. This study investigates frictional heating in FPBs using flat-plate sliding experiments under varied velocities, contact pressures, and surface roughness levels. Direct steel surface temperature measurements, enabled by a thin-film thermocouple technique, are integrated with a data-enabled, physics-based inverse framework to predict the transient surface temperature of the UHPF friction pad without assuming a priori fixed heat partition ratio. To our knowledge, this constitutes the first experimentally validated reconstruction of the pad interfacial temperature and transient heat-partition ratio for FPB-relevant contacts. The results indicate that frictional heating is dominated by deformational heat generation within the friction pad, leading to an initial heat partition into the steel plate that is negligible at sliding onset. As sliding proceeds, the heat partition ratio gradually increases toward a steady state, with the transition process primarily governed by interfacial thermal resistance rather than bulk conduction effects. Key thermal parameters are identified along with their credible ranges and evolution trends. The proposed approach offers a physically consistent foundation for refined frictional heating models and improved thermo-mechanical assessment of FPB systems and design-oriented simulation.

Time-variant error-adaptive interval inversion for resilience improvement based on nonlinear redundancy of steel-concrete composite girder bridges

Authors: Xiaoming Wang, Zhenying Zhang, You Dong, Liang Liu, Yongjian Liu, Wenjie Yang, Jianling Zhao, Yizhe Shi, Zeliang Gao

Institution: Chang'an University, Hong Kong Polytechnic University, China Railway Design Corporation (China)

Published: 2026-10-14

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

The system nonlinear redundancy of the multi-girder Steel-Concrete Composite Girder (S-CCG) bridge provides the necessary internal buffering mechanism against asymmetric extreme loads. In the process of developing a bridge resilience improvement strategy, error-adaptive capacity from system nonlinear redundancy against inevitable deviations holds the key to ensuring optimal comprehensive performance including reliability, redundancy, cost, and constructability. However, as a measure of such capacity, the “error-adaptive interval” in the identification of ductility degradation and transverse stiffness is not accounted for. This paper proposes a time-variant Error-Adaptive Interval Inversion (E-AII) framework to exploit the error-adaptive capacity, in which the inversion is formulated as a reliability-constrained, multi-objective interval optimization problem with the aid of the machine-learning method and the Fiber Macro-Element Numerical Simulation Method (FM-ENSM). The proposed method is validated using a real S-CCG bridge. Numerical validation shows that the FE-ENSM accelerates one single nonlinear analysis from 1568 s to 2 s while maintaining simulation accuracy. Furthermore, the deployed surrogate learning strategy for worst-case reliability index predictions achieves a coefficient of determination of R 2 of 0.99103 and a maximum relative error of 2.52%. These indicate that the proposed method is able to significantly mitigate the computational burden while sustaining high predictive precision. The multiple comprehensive performances of the resilience improvement process can be greatly optimized with the guidance of the maximized error-adaptive interval of the ductility factor and transverse stiffness. This method can be extended to the E-AII of other scenarios of bridge resilience improvement.

Constrained Performance-Based Optimization of T-NES: A Stochastic Polynomial Chaos Expansion approach with Far-Field and Near-Field considerations

Authors: Leandro Fleck Fadel Miguel, Felipe Costa Macedo, Andre Teofilo Beck

Institution: Universidade de São Paulo

Published: 2026-10-13

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This paper proposes a reliability-constrained Performance-Based Design Optimization (PBDO) framework for Track Nonlinear Energy Sinks (T-NESs) that integrates Stochastic Polynomial Chaos Expansion (SPCE)-based fragility modeling, dual-hazard seismic representation, and life-cycle cost optimization within a unified probabilistic framework. Existing probabilistic optimization approaches for T-NES devices typically rely on lognormal fragility assumptions combined with first-passage formulations, requiring quasi-stationary excitation models that are unsuitable for representing pulse-like Near-Field (NF) earthquakes. The proposed methodology introduces three main contributions. First, SPCE is embedded directly within the optimization loop to construct fragility functions from peak Engineering Demand Parameters (EDPs), eliminating predefined distributional assumptions while reducing the computational cost of fragility construction by approximately a factor of eight compared with conventional Monte Carlo-based procedures. Second, the peak-response formulation eliminates the quasi-stationary excitation requirement inherent to first-passage approaches, enabling consistent treatment of both Far-Field (FF) and NF ground motions within the same optimization framework. Third, the design problem is formulated as a dual-hazard reliability-constrained optimization, in which expected life-cycle cost is minimized under FF seismic demand while a probabilistic stroke constraint is enforced under NF excitation to ensure device operability during pulse-like events. The framework is demonstrated on a mid-rise steel frame located in Concepción, Chile. Results indicate that unconstrained economic optimization marginally violates the target stroke reliability under NF excitation.
