Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Dynamic analysis and crack observability of combined-material spur gear pairs: a model-based study

Authors: Omar D. Mohammed, Sadok Sassi

Institution: Örebro University, Qatar University

Published: 2026-11-10

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

The use of lightweight materials is increasingly demanded to support energy-saving and weight-reduction trends. In this work, the dynamic behavior and crack detection of a spur gear system are studied for four material combinations of the engaging gears: steel–steel, polyamide–polyamide, steel–polyamide and polyamide–steel. The analytically computed time-varying mesh stiffness (TVMS) drives a six-degree-of-freedom (6DOF) dynamic model, from which the residual response and the corresponding frequency response functions of each configuration are obtained over a range of pinion root-crack depths. The analysis yields a closed-form crack observability coefficient Λ showing that the normalized sensitivity of the equivalent mesh stiffness to a crack-induced loss of tooth stiffness is governed by the ratio of the two elastic moduli alone, independently of crack depth, transmitted load, rotational speed, and damping. Λ = 0.985 when the crack lies in the compliant polyamide member of a dissimilar pair, against 0.0148 when it lies in the steel member, a factor of about seventy. Combined with the crack-induced modal shift, Λ yields a dimensionless detectability index D that spans nearly two orders of magnitude across the four pairings, from 2.50 for steel–steel and 1.85 for polyamide–steel, through a marginal 0.375 for polyamide–polyamide, to 0.028 for steel–polyamide, and that classifies a proposed pairing as monitorable, marginal, or masked before any hardware exists. Material combination therefore carries a cost that is not only dynamic but diagnostic. A crack in the stiffer member of a dissimilar pair is predicted to be effectively invisible to vibration-based monitoring, in both the time and frequency domains. The paper presents different diagnostic results analysis and concludes with several findings, which are model-based and await experimental confirmation on a dedicated mixed-material test rig.

Uncertainty propagation and stochastic modelling in correlated two-phase structural load testing

Authors: Rok Kamnik

Institution: University of Maribor

Published: 2026-11-10

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Short-term structural load tests are often performed from a single, unchanged total-station setup, where observations before and after load application may share instrumental, geometric, or environmental error components. If such inter-epoch dependence is ignored, the propagated uncertainty of displacement and the resulting detectability assessment may be distorted. This study develops an analytical and numerically verified framework for uncertainty propagation in two-phase structural load testing with inter-epoch covariance. A joint two-epoch three-dimensional least-squares model is formulated using a block-structured observation covariance matrix. A reduced closed-form expression for the variance of vertical displacement is then derived for unchanged station geometry and constant instrument and prism heights. The expression is verified using a synthetic 3D adjustment model and 5,000 Monte Carlo realisations. For assumed total-station precision parameters of 1 mm +1 ppm for slope distance and 1″ for angular observations, the closed-form and full 3D least-squares covariance results agree to the displayed numerical precision. Monte Carlo standard deviations differ from the analytical values by less than 0.8%. For inter-epoch correlation scenarios of −0.8, 0, and +0.8, the standard uncertainty of vertical displacement is 0.1702 mm, 0.1268 mm, and 0.0567 mm, respectively. Across the interval −0.9 to +0.9, uncertainty varies from 0.1748 mm to 0.0401 mm. For an assumed 0.20 mm displacement, the standardised detectability ratio changes from 1.18 to 3.53. The correlation coefficients are treated as sensitivity parameters, not empirically estimated instrument characteristics.

Nanoimaging using scanning computed tomography

Authors: Przemyslaw Wachulak, Martyna Wardzińska, Karol Adam Janulewicz, Andrzej S. Bartnik, Henryk Fiedorowicz

Institution: Military University of Technology in Warsaw

Published: 2026-11-09

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

The paper demonstrates the application of the knife-edge scanning technique combined with a revolving object to dimensional computed tomography ( CT ). The presented arguments are equally valid for specific technical variants of the technique, such as a revolving scanner. The irradiation system is modified in comparison to that typically applied in CT, but the object is analysed within the same slice-plane. The proposed Scanning Computed Tomography ( SCT ) method is validated mainly by numerical modelling, and it is shown that it has significant potential in improving the measurement resolution. This results from the procedure of the data registration and the following application of the conventional software, including elaboration of the sinogram subsequently processed with the inverse Radon transform ( IRT ). The modelling is applied for the reconstruction of both an arbitrary-shaped aperture and intensity distribution in a radiation nano-focus. The proof-of-concept experiment was conducted in the visible spectrum range and for the Fresnel (near-field) zone.
