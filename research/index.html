Development and experimental validation of an accelerometer-based method for determining the vertical stiffness of the railway track

Authors: Paulo André Moraes Pereira, Talita de Freitas Alves, Alfredo Gay Neto, Liedi Légi Bariani Bernucci, Rosângela dos Santos Motta, Linda Lee Ho, A. Melo

Institution: Universidade de São Paulo, Universidade Federal da Bahia

Published: 2026-10-31

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Track vertical stiffness is an important parameter for evaluating the structural condition of railway tracks. Accelerometer-based approaches have been investigated for reconstructing railway track deflection, but their accuracy depends strongly on signal-processing choices. Existing studies have evaluated specific filtering configurations under particular operating conditions, while the combined influence of filter type and filter-specific parameters under low-frequency excitation has not been systematically investigated using controlled excitation and independent reference measurements. This issue is relevant at low train speeds, when the characteristic frequencies of the train-induced response approach the low-frequency noise region of the accelerometer. This study develops and experimentally validates an accelerometer-based methodology for estimating railway track deflection and vertical stiffness under low-speed operating conditions. The proposed approach combines controlled laboratory excitation over a broad range of amplitude–frequency conditions with an automated procedure for determining optimal signal-processing parameters. Butterworth, Chebyshev Type I, and Elliptic high-pass filters are evaluated across different cutoff-frequency scenarios and parameter combinations using LVDT measurements as an independent reference. The optimized configurations are subsequently applied to field acceleration measurements acquired during a freight train passage at approximately 35 km/h on a heavy-haul railway section in Brazil. After analytical correction, the reconstructed deflections showed high concordance with LVDT measurements. The Chebyshev and Elliptic filters showed no statistically significant differences from the LVDT reference for deflection and track modulus, whereas the Butterworth filter differed significantly. The results support the proposed methodology as a systematic approach for selecting signal-processing configurations and estimating railway track deflection and vertical stiffness under low-speed operation.
