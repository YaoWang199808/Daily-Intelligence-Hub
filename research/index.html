Evaluating pre-impact fall detection: A User-independent assessment of deep learning models on the KFall dataset

Authors: Vincent Hernandez, Saikat Chakraborty, Gentiane Venture

Institution: The University of Tokyo, Birla Institute of Technology, Mesra

Published: 2026-10-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Falls pose a significant risk to older adults, making early detection crucial for injury prevention. This study evaluates deep learning architectures, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs), Transformers, and hybrid models, for pre-impact fall detection using quantitative human motion measurements acquired from a wearable inertial measurement unit (IMU) in the KFall dataset. A comprehensive measurement framework incorporating a standardized signal conditioning pipeline, strict user-independent cross-validation, data leakage prevention measures, and extensive hyperparameter optimization was implemented to ensure reliable and reproducible comparisons across architectures. All evaluated architectures achieved strong performance for both fall detection and activities of daily living (ADL) classification, reaching up to 91.1% F1-score for fall instances, 99.66% ADL accuracy, and an average lead time of 636 (42) milliseconds (ms), exceeding previously reported requirements for protective interventions such as wearable airbags. Participant-level analyses revealed variability across individuals while maintaining lead times sufficient for practical pre-impact intervention. Statistical analyses further showed that the CNNLSTM architecture achieved significantly lower F1-scores and lead times than the other evaluated models, whereas no significant differences were observed between CNN and Transformer-based architectures. These findings suggest that multiple deep learning architectures can achieve comparable performance for pre-impact fall detection when properly optimized and evaluated under a consistent user-independent protocol. External evaluation on an independent ADL dataset of older adults further demonstrated good generalization while maintaining a low false alarm rate during normal daily activities. This study establishes a reproducible benchmark for future research and supports the development of practical fall prevention systems for older adults.
