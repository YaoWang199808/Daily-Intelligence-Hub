Electrical characterization of FFF-printed conductive PLA/carbon black composites: Effects of printing parameters and conductive-path geometry

Authors: Błażej Woźny, Magdalena Rucka

Institution: Gdańsk University of Technology

Published: 2026-11-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Conductive polymer composites produced by fused filament fabrication (FFF) are increasingly used in functional components, but their electrical resistance depends on both filament properties and the structure generated during printing. This study examines how raster orientation, flow rate, layer height, and conductive-path geometry affect the electrical resistance of FFF-printed PLA/carbon black (PLA/CB) specimens and how the electrical response relates to local cross-sectional structure. Resistance was determined using four-probe DC measurements and least-squares fits to bipolar current–voltage sweeps, with three replicate specimens tested per condition. Cross-sectional microscopy and image-based 2D area-porosity analysis were used to characterize local internal structure. Raster orientation produced the largest resistance differences: the transverse 90° orientation increased resistance by 190.6% relative to the longitudinal 0° orientation, while 0°/90° and 45°/135° patterns produced increases of 39.1% and 50.0%, respectively. Reducing flow rate to 95% increased 2D area porosity to 26.7% and increased resistance in all analysed geometries, whereas increasing flow rate to 105% substantially reduced both. Layer height affected resistance despite similar 2D area porosity, showing that total void area alone was insufficient to describe the electrical response. Conductive-path geometry was also associated with resistance differences despite equal nominal centreline length and cross-sectional dimensions. The investigation was limited to one commercial PLA/CB filament and the selected printing conditions. Overall, the study provides an experimentally based assessment of manufacturing and geometrical factors relevant to the electrical design of FFF-printed PLA/CB components.
