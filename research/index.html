Determining the heights and horizontal positions of Tatra Mountain peaks using GNSS measurements, mapping services, and LiDAR data

Authors: Dariusz Strugarek, Krzysztof Sośnica

Institution: Wrocław University of Environmental and Life Sciences

Published: 2026-11-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Accurate heights and horizontal positions of mountain peaks and passes are important for tourism, research, and public use, yet map-based information often lacks documented accuracy. This study provides precise Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) measurements of 140 peaks and passes in the Tatra Mountains and uses them to validate heights and horizontal positions derived from Polish (PL) and Slovak (SK) LiDAR data, geographic registry (PRNG), OpenStreetMap (OSM), OpenTopoMap (OTM), and Google Maps (GM). We assess the feasibility of determining peak and pass heights using remotely derived data (LiDAR + maps) assuming the case where accurate GNSS measurements are unavailable. GNSS-based validation reveals systematic differences between the LiDAR datasets. The PL LiDAR shows height offsets relative to GNSS of 0.01–0.30 m with variability (standard deviation, stdev) of 0.36–0.56 m, while SK LiDAR has negative biases of − 0.06 to − 0.27 m with stdev of 0.19–0.28 m. Horizontal offsets are approximately 0.26 m for PRNG and 2 m for OSM/OTM, while GM performs the poorest. Combining LiDAR with high-quality horizontal positions (PRNG/OSM) enables peak and pass height derivation without GNSS data. We derive different methods of LiDAR + map-based peak height determination: nearest-point, mean-point, and highest-point approaches. The nearest-point approach yields mean height differences of − 0.09 to − 0.25 m, the highest-point approach yields positive biases of 0.06–0.43 m and increased variability, while the mean-point approach performs poorest with differences of approximately − 1.0 m. LiDAR + map-based peak and pass heights can be estimated with stdev below 0.5 and 0.3 m, respectively, which fulfills the typical accuracy requirements for maps.
