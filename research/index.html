An innovative seismic shelter for vulnerable masonry buildings: Shaking table collapse tests

Authors: Seyedsajjad Hosseini, João Guerreiro, Luis Guerreiro, João Gomes Ferreira

Institution: University of Lisbon

Published: 2026-11-02

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This study presents the analysis of the falling trajectory of a seismic shelter placed on top of a masonry building when its collapse occurs. The test objective was to experimentally simulate the collapse of a vulnerable masonry building accommodating a shelter during an earthquake event. The test was conducted on the 3D seismic table at LNEC as part of the ”Structural Hyper-resisting Element for Life Threatening Earthquake Risk” project, known as the SHELTER project. This study aimed to develop seismic protection to increase survival rates in Unreinforced Masonry Buildings (URMs) in high-risk regions. Given the destructive character of the study, wireless instrumentation—specifically optical and wireless sensors—was preferentially employed. The investigation analysed the shelter behaviour during structural collapse to define requirements for enhancing its design.

Integrated statistical-analytical framework quantifying bond behaviour of FRP‑reinforced concrete structures

Authors: Sándor Sólyom

Institution: Budapest University of Technology and Economics

Published: 2026-11-02

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Air-entrained concrete (AEC) is widely used to enhance the durability of reinforced concrete (RC) structures, particularly under freeze-thaw exposure. This study investigates the combined effects of air entrainment and Fibre Reinforced Polymer (FRP) reinforcement on bond behaviour in concrete. An integrated analytical-statistical approach is proposed that combines analytical bond modelling with multifactor statistical evaluation. The bond response is evaluated through bond stress-slip relationships, forming the basis for structural modelling. The analysis is based on an experimental programme comprising 224 pull-out tests. Analytical calibration was performed using the experimental programme, whereas the multifactor statistical analysis considered a nominal subset of 136 GFRP and BFRP specimens with diameters of 6‐12 mm. The study calibrates the parameters of the CMR and the ascending branch of the modified BPE (mBPE) models. Multifactor ANOVA with bootstrap resampling is employed to quantify the effects of air entrainment, concrete strength (approximately 35 and 75 MPa), bar diameter (6‐12 mm) and surface characteristics. The results indicate that air entrainment has a statistically significant but comparatively small effect on bond strength, while concrete strength and FRP surface characteristics have substantially larger effects within the investigated dataset. A significant interaction between FRP surface configuration and diameter is also observed. The results demonstrate that bond behaviour is governed by interaction effects rather than single variables, providing a basis for improved modelling of FRP‑reinforced concrete systems.
