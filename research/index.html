Pavement crack analysis and detection: A system-oriented survey and unified taxonomy

Authors: Jose Maria Castillo-Secilla, José M. Palomares, Joaquín Olivares

Institution: University of Córdoba

Published: 2026-11-20

Journal: Automation in Construction

Method: Review / Survey

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Pavement crack analysis is a representative benchmark problem for intelligent visual inspection, requiring detection of thin, low-contrast, topologically irregular structures under severe class imbalance. Existing surveys remain fragmented across levels of abstraction, typically addressing algorithmic performance, data acquisition, or management systems in isolation. This survey provides a system-oriented synthesis based on a unified taxonomy with three functional layers: (i) data acquisition and sensing; (ii) crack detection and analysis algorithms, from classical vision to advanced Deep Learning, including foundation models, Large Language Models (LLMs), and Vision-Language Models (VLMs); and (iii) downstream applications, comprising predictive maintenance and Digital Twins. Public datasets and evaluation practices are reviewed, examining how task formulation, annotation granularity, sensing modalities, and acquisition conditions affect evaluation validity and generalization. Computational and deployment considerations (efficiency, interpretability, scalability, and robustness) are then discussed to guide the development of pavement crack analysis solutions that are both scientifically rigorous and practically deployable.

A monoscopic, three-dimensional vibration measurement method and its application to human phonation research

Authors: Christoph Näger, Anja Böttger, Stefan Kniesburges, Stefan Becker

Institution: Friedrich-Alexander-Universität Erlangen-Nürnberg, Universitätsklinikum Erlangen

Published: 2026-11-20

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

In this work, a new methodology is proposed for reconstructing three-dimensional (3D) structural surface vibrations from multiple measurement modalities. It is based on employing a combination of a high-speed camera and a Laser-Scanning-Vibrometer on a common optical axis, i.e. in a monoscopic setup. The advantage of this method in comparison to established methods like 3D Digital Image Correlation or 3D Laser-Scanning-Vibrometry is the fact that optical access to the vibrating surface is only needed from one viewing angle instead of two or three. This facilitates 3D vibration measurements in cases where optical access is limited. The high-speed camera in combination with an optical flow algorithm yields in-plane vibration velocities, while the Laser-Scanning-Vibrometer measures the out-of-plane vibration component. Interpolation on a common grid leads to 3D vibrational velocities. To obtain 3D displacements, a particle advection scheme on the measured velocities is applied. We validate the method on artificial measurement data as well as the case of a synthetic vocal fold vibration by comparing it to a stereo marker tracking approach. Both methods show generally good agreement in oscillation amplitudes and phase of vibration velocity and displacement (on average 7.8% deviation in the trajectory and < 2% in the phase), revealing that the monoscopic method can be used to quantify 3D vibration with good accuracy. We apply the method to another case of synthetic vocal fold vibration with an attached vocal tract, where it enables us to perform 3D vibration reconstruction under strong constraints regarding the optical accessibility of the vibrating surface.

A Semi-Self-Supervised framework for universal Multi-Domain Machinery fault diagnosis via unit normalized Selective Embedding and Cross-Domain contrastive Alignment

Authors: Mert Sehri

Institution: University of Ottawa

Published: 2026-11-20

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Intelligent fault diagnosis of industrial machinery is challenged by heterogeneous sensor modalities, inconsistent physical units, and the scarcity of labeled data across operating domains. Existing semi-self-supervised learning (semi-SSL) methods for fault diagnosis are typically evaluated on single-domain datasets and lack mechanisms to align fault representations across different machinery types and sensor configurations. This paper presents the Universal Machinery Fault Diagnosis Framework (UMFD), a semi-SSL framework that unifies vibration and acoustic signals from seven publicly available datasets spanning bearing, gearbox, and electrical motor machinery into a single diagnostic model. UMFD integrates three components. First, Unit Normalized Selective Embedding (UNSE), which standardizes multi-source signals into a common velocity-domain representation via physics-grounded unit conversion and alternating source interleaving. Second, Cross-Domain Contrastive Alignment (CDCA), which enforces domain-invariant fault representations by aligning the same fault signals across different datasets using fault labels during pre-training. Third, masked signal modeling (MSM), which promotes generalizable temporal feature extraction by reconstructing masked signal segments without labels. The resulting universal dataset comprises approximately 457 million single channel samples and 913 million two channel samples across four classes which consist of healthy, bearing faults, gearbox faults, and induction motor faults. UMFD achieves 97.60 % ± 0.74 accuracy with 100 % of fine-tuning labels, and reaches accuracy comparable to the strongest baseline’s fully labeled performance using only 25 % of fine-tuning labels. This work represents the first tier of a proposed three-stage scaling roadmap toward a Large Signal Model (LSM) for industrial condition monitoring.

Adaptive feature regulation and directional consistency optimization for lightweight ORB-SLAM3 in resource-constrained indoor environments

Authors: Runping Zheng, Zhuhua Hu, Yaochi Zhao, Chunyi Yue, Kunkun Ding, Zican Liu, Guangfeng Liu, Shilong Guo

Institution: Hainan University

Published: 2026-11-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (VSLAM) is a foundational technology widely applied in robotics, autonomous driving, and augmented reality, enabling devices to navigate and construct environmental models using visual sensors. However, on resource-constrained hardware platforms, the real-time performance and robustness of VSLAM systems are severely hindered by limited computational resources, especially in indoor environments where illumination changes and dynamic scenes cause significant fluctuations in feature point quantity, undermining tracking stability and increasing backend optimization overhead. To address these challenges, we propose FCD-VSLAM, a lightweight ORB-SLAM3 algorithm integrated with feature point convergence adaptation and pose-based directional consistency checks. Specifically, we design a Feature-Point Convergence Adaptive Module that combines image enhancement, non-maximum suppression, and multi-objective optimization to dynamically determine the optimal feature point range. A Directional Consistency Check Skip Mechanism based on a motion model is introduced to reduce redundant computations during stable pose changes, and a Descriptor Optimization Method fusing directional information is proposed to improve matching accuracy. Evaluations on the EuRoC public dataset and a private dataset show that FCD-VSLAM outperforms the original ORB-SLAM3 and other lightweight SLAM methods, achieving an average 20.18% improvement in localization accuracy (ATE RMSE) on EuRoC sequences and a 12.45% reduction in per-frame processing time. This work advances the practical application of VSLAM on resource-limited devices, providing a reliable solution for real-time environmental perception in scenarios such as indoor robotics and portable augmented reality systems.The code are available at https://github.com/zcrop/FCD-SLAM.

An innovative strengthening approach for reinforced concrete beams using 3D printed concrete with in-process embedded FRP grid reinforcement

Authors: Chaolan Wu, Houqi Sun, Jie-Kai Zhou, Jun‐Jie Zeng, Yan Zhuge

Institution: Adelaide University, State Key Laboratory Breeding Base of Mountain Bridge and Tunnel Engineering, The University of Adelaide, Chongqing Jiaotong University, Guangdong University of Technology, Queensland University of Technology

Published: 2026-11-20

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Enhancing the crack resistance and flexural performance of deteriorated reinforced concrete (RC) beams is essential for extending their service life and ensuring structural integrity. 3D concrete printing (3DCP) offers a cost-effective, formwork-free, and labour-efficient construction approach; however, its limited tensile capacity remains a key challenge. This study proposes a novel strengthening technique for enhanced crack resistance of reinforced concrete (RC) beams comprising 3D-printed fibre-reinforced concrete (3DPFRC) layers with in-process embedded fibre-reinforced polymer (FRP) grid reinforcement at the soffit. The flexural performance of strengthened beams was experimentally evaluated under three-point bending, with parameters including the number of printed layers, interface treatment types, and U-jacket anchorage. Results demonstrate that the proposed FRP-3DPFRC system significantly enhances crack resistance, increasing the cracking load by 47.6%–151.7%, while yielding only marginal improvements in ultimate flexural capacity. The 3DPFRC and embedded FRP grids effectively restrain crack propagation and reduce crack widths. Nevertheless, premature interfacial debonding limits the full utilisation of FRP tensile strength, particularly in multi-layer configurations, where additional layers exacerbate stress concentration and precipitate earlier failure. U-jacket anchorage improves both anchorage efficiency and material utilisation, with the most pronounced improvement in single-layer strengthening schemes. An elastic equivalent section method is developed to predict cracking load, with test-to-prediction ratios ranging from 0.81–1.14. Overall, the proposed technique demonstrates practical effectiveness; however, its performance is governed by interfacial behaviour, highlighting the need for improved bonding and anchorage strategies.
