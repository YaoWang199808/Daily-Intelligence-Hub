Field validation of an SI‑traceable calibration transfer methodology using portable radon monitors

Authors: Daniel Rábago, Scott D. Chambers, Maksym Gachkivskyi, Roger Curcoll, Marta Fuente, Stefan Röttger, Camille Yver-Kwok, Ileana Rădulescu, Arturo Vargas, Annette Röttger

Institution: Universidad de Cantabria, Australian Nuclear Science and Technology Organisation, Heidelberg University, Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya, Laboratoire des Sciences du Climat et de l'Environnement, Physikalisch-Technische Bundesanstalt, Horia Hulubei National Institute for R and D in Physics and Nuclear Engineering

Published: 2026-11-01

Journal: Measurement

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Accurate atmospheric radon measurements require traceable calibration methods applicable under environmental conditions. This study evaluates two portable SI‑traceable transfer standard radon monitors (ARMON v2 and ANSTO 200 L) through field intercomparisons conducted at PTB (Germany, 1 m a.g.L., 17 days) and the ICOS Saclay station (France, 100 m a.g.L., 4 months). At PTB, both transfer standards exhibited a strong linear relationship under variable environmental conditions, confirming the stability of their SI-traceable calibrations outside the laboratory. At Saclay, consistent scaling was maintained, although temporally variable discrepancies of ± 15–20 % were observed, indicating site‑dependent influences rather than fixed calibration bias. A weighted calibration transfer based on inverse‑variance regression successfully harmonised an operational ANSTO 1500 L monitor with both instruments, producing a calibrated time series consistent with two independent SI-traceable reference scales. Additional comparison with a progeny‑based Heidelberg Radon Monitor demonstrated strong agreement at synoptic timescales despite short‑term variability. These results demonstrate that portable transfer standards enable practical in situ transfer of SI‑traceable calibration to operational atmospheric radon monitors without interrupting continuous measurements. This approach was successfully validated at two contrasting European monitoring sites through dedicated field intercomparisons, providing a robust framework for harmonisation within atmospheric monitoring networks.

Engineering Structures

Semi-analytical static analysis of axially non-uniform piles modeled as Timoshenko beams on non-uniform Winkler foundations

Authors: Juan Camilo Molina‐Villegas, Valentina Valentina Gil-Peláez

Institution: Universidad EAFIT

Published: 2026-11-15

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 1

Abstract:

This paper extends and formulates the Green’s Functions Differential Transform Element Method (GFDTEM) for the static analysis of linearly elastic axially non-uniform beams and piles modeled according to Timoshenko beam theory on spatially varying Winkler foundations. Conventional finite element analyses of such tapered members generally require dense meshes to represent spatial gradients in stiffness and foundation reaction and to capture localized fields induced by concentrated forces and moments. The GFDTEM provides a mesh-reduction alternative within the Finite Element Method (FEM) family by combining Differential Transform Method (DTM)/Taylor-series expansions with a standard element-assembly framework, while treating general external actions analytically through Green’s Functions (GFs), thus representing concentrated loads and piecewise-defined distributed loadings. The formulation is developed in strong form by decomposing the element response into homogeneous and fixed (particular) components. The homogeneous solution yields semi-analytical generalized displacement shape functions and the corresponding element stiffness matrix, whereas the fixed response is recovered directly from the stiffness-matrix coefficients and generalized displacement shape functions through the associated GFs of the doubly fixed element, leading to semi-analytical fixed-end forces and load-dependent internal fields. Three benchmark problems — two axially non-uniform beams on a non-uniform foundation and a plane frame supported by axially non-uniform piles with spatially varying skin friction in heterogeneous soil — are solved using one element per structural member. In all cases, the GFDTEM matches reference solutions from very dense conventional FEM meshes with excellent accuracy while achieving substantial reductions in mesh density and global degrees of freedom with a very competitive computational cost.

Design considerations for elastomeric rolling seismic isolators

Authors: Antonios A. Katsamakas

Institution: Polytechnique Montréal

Published: 2026-11-15

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Numerical Simulation

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Seismic isolation of lightweight structures remains limited due to the high cost of conventional bearings. This paper studies the design of a low-cost, high-performance rolling seismic isolator comprising an elastomeric sphere rolling between two concrete plates, one of which is concave to provide gravitational restoring force. A six-parameter numerical model is used to describe the seismic response of the isolators. The model has been validated against 1170 shake table tests in previous studies and captures all stages of the flag-shaped response, including positive and negative stiffness, gravitational restoring force, and rolling friction coefficient. The influence of key design parameters on structural response is examined: isolation period, rolling friction coefficient, sphere deformability, and external viscous damping. An intensity measure analysis is then performed. The results show that: (i) velocity-independent elastomeric components outperform velocity-dependent ones, (ii) the eigenperiod has limited influence on displacements, (iii) high rolling friction reduces the influence of eigenperiod, (iv) increasing rolling friction is as effective as adding external viscous damping, and (v) isolators with high rolling friction and low eigenperiod tend to approximate the bilinear response. Peak ground acceleration and Arias Intensity are the two best intensity measures, with the former correlating better with isolator’s accelerations and the latter with isolator displacements. The findings provide practical design recommendations for the wider implementation of rolling isolators in lightweight structures.

Machine learning guided minimum X-bracing design for progressive collapse prevention in RC buildings

Authors: Muneeb Jadallah, Muhammed Şahin, Adem Doğangün, Moncef L. Nehdi

Institution: Bursa Uludağ Üni̇versi̇tesi̇, University of Guelph

Published: 2026-11-15

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Progressive collapse of reinforced concrete (RC) buildings after sudden column loss remains a low-probability, but high-consequence hazard, and efficient retrofit design still relies on repeated nonlinear simulation and trial-and-error. This study introduces a machine-learning-guided framework that moves progressive-collapse assessment toward direct retrofit decision support by estimating the minimum X-bracing quantity and layout required to prevent collapse. A numerical database covering RC buildings with 5, 9, 12, and 15 stories; 4, 5, and 6 m spans; three column-removal scenarios, and three removal levels was generated using nonlinear dynamic analyses in Extreme Loading for Structures based on the Applied Element Method. The minimum collapse-preventing configuration for each case was used to train six algorithm families, spanning linear, kernel, neural-network, and tree-ensemble approaches, under a nested cross-validation protocol for safe/failure classification, brace-count regression, and layout prediction. Random forest achieved the best classification performance (F1 = 0.962, ROC-AUC = 0.987), while XGBoost achieved the lowest brace-count error (MAE = 0.965 braces, R² = 0.847). A cross-validated SHAP analysis identified span length as the dominant parameter, accounting for 46.8% of the total attributed effect. Because under-prediction is a safety failure, a calibrated safety margin reduced the under-prediction rate from 25.0% to 4.6%. Layout prediction reproduced the complete bracing arrangement exactly in up to 71.6% of configurations. In a hold-out comparison with ELS, 17 of 20 configurations were reproduced exactly, whereas the three under-predicted schemes collapsed, so high-demand predictions require ELS verification. The framework offers a rapid pathway for screening and preliminary retrofit design, with nonlinear dynamic analysis as final verification.
