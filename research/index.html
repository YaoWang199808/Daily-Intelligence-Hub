Elemental potential of streets: A self-organizing model of the geological conditions of the province of Quebec using investigative and generative AI

Authors: Michael Robert Doyle

Institution: Université Laval

Published: 2026-11-01

Journal: Tunnelling and Underground Space Technology

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Current urban planning treats the subsurface as a passive solid or void, constrained by a “needs to resources” paradigm that marginalizes geological knowledge. Standard mapping methodologies, particularly those using the Analytic Hierarchy Process, rely on manual expert solicitation, which is logistically burdensome and prone to cognitive blind spots. This research proposes a theoretical shift from target-oriented systems to an obstacle-oriented framework. Designating the street segment as the fundamental unit of analysis across the province of Québec, this research presents a prototype that implements a static knowledge-retrieval pipeline where a Large Language Model serves as an informational model for preliminary planning, translating hydrogeological constraints into qualitative assessments. These textual descriptions are then encoded into a continuous partition spectrum using a one-dimensional self-organizing map. The prototype demonstrates high computational robustness in standardizing qualitative data, successfully mapping the geological dichotomy between the stable crystalline basement of the Canadian Shield and the conflict-prone sedimentary basins of the St. Lawrence Lowlands. By converting qualitative data into relational contextual numbers, the model identifies latent synergies without predefined target functions. The article concludes that this pre-specific modeling approach validates the utility of artificial intelligence in mitigating cognitive blind spots, demonstrating how computational models can complement human engineering by repositioning geological insights at the very beginning of the urban design process. By establishing a topos of communication capable of circulating around the inherent opacity of the geological volume, this method offers a scalable instrument for sustainable underground urbanism that treats the earth not as an anecdotal resource, but as an active constituent of the built environment.

Rock Mechanics and Rock Engineering

A Comparison of Tensile Testing Techniques and Predictive Correlations

Authors: Evan J. Lindenbach, Jack R. Foran, Kevin P. Schaeffer, R. G. Bearce

Published: 2026-11-02

Journal: Rock Mechanics and Rock Engineering

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Abstract The engineering properties of a rock foundation are key parameters in the analysis of dams as they inform an understanding of how a structure will perform under a loading condition of interest. One important, but difficult to accurately evaluate, engineering property is the tensile strength of rock. As the direct tensile strength (DTS) testing of intact rock is non-trivial and expensive, the tensile strength is typically inferred from more common indirect methods, such as the Brazilian tensile strength (BTS) test, akin to the splitting tensile strength test performed on concrete, or using correlations to other mechanical properties, such as the crack initiation (CI) strength, m i parameter from the Generalized Hoek-Brown failure criterion, or the Mode I fracture toughness. This research leverages a robust laboratory testing program utilizing a state-of-the-art digital image correlation (DIC) system to understand how the different platen shapes and the use of a soft cushion affect BTS results, improve the understanding about the relationship between DTS and BTS tests (with a specific focus on optimizing the test arrangement), and explores correlations between other laboratory-measured properties (e.g., CI and m i ) and the DTS. The DTS was difficult to measure directly, but was found to be closely related to the BTS when testing was performed using curved platens, with or without a cushion; this testing arrangement also resulted in a failure mechanism that aligns with the underlying assumptions from Hondros (1959); ISRM (1978); and ASTM (2016). When analyzing the BTS data from different platen arrangements, it is important to use a relationship that incorporates the loading arc angle to ensure data are comparable as the BTS is a function of the loading arc angle in the full stress solution. The use of CI or m i to estimate the DTS was found to be unreliable with the BTS providing the closest approximation
