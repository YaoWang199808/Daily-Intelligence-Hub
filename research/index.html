Image-guided robotic workflow for automated mortar joint reconstruction in regular and quasi-regular face brick masonry

Authors: Andrea Vecchi, Alessandro Ricciutelli, Valentino Sangiorgio

Institution: University of Chieti-Pescara

Published: 2026-11-23

Journal: Automation in Construction

Method: Review / Survey

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Mortar joint reconstruction of exposed brick masonry is still performed manually, with limited repeatability. This paper presents an image-guided robotic workflow that converts a rectified photograph of a regular or quasi-regular face brick wall into an executable, extrusion-synchronized robot program. AI-based segmentation provides the mortar joint network, which graph-based path planning converts into an ordered deposition trajectory. The trajectory is verified through virtual commissioning, assigned a robot configuration by a multi-criteria kinematic framework, and translated into synchronized robot code. Validation covered three levels: joint centre localization on 12 walls, with a 1.44 mm mean error, within half the 8 mm minimum joint width; path generation on 30 walls, with an 86.7% direct pass rate and full recovery after band adjustment; and physical execution on a planar laboratory specimen along 6750 mm of trajectory, with computation under 45 s. The workflow links image-based survey to robotic joint repair.

Nonlinear damping for one-to-one internal resonance: derivation and nonlinear vibration experiments

Authors: Marco Amabili

Institution: Westlake University, McGill University

Published: 2026-11-23

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

A new nonlinear coupling-damping model for one-to-one internal resonance is derived for the first time and assessed against large-amplitude vibration experiments. The derivation uses the direct tangent stiffnesses of the elastic coupling potential to prescribe the displacement dependence of the damping interaction. The resulting symmetric coupling terms are governed by a single coefficient and make the damping acting on each mode depend on the squared displacement of the other mode and the velocity of the mode. These terms are combined with linear viscous and cubic damping and introduced into a model of a water-filled aluminium circular cylindrical shell based on nonlinear Novozhilov shell theory and potential flow discretized with 14 degrees of freedom. Numerical continuation and direct time integration are used to obtain the nonlinear responses, which are compared with experiments at five different harmonic force levels. Good agreement is obtained for both driven and companion modes over the full excitation range. The coupling coefficient is identified as negative, representing a reduction of intrinsic damping during modal interaction. The proposed model surpasses the limitation of using a linear viscous damping approximation, which requires readjusting the damping coefficients at each excitation level.

Student’s t degrees of freedom as a novel criterion for informative frequency band selection in rotating machinery fault diagnosis in the presence of non-Gaussian background noise

Authors: Justyna Hebda-Sobkowicz, Radosław Zimroz

Institution: Wrocław University of Science and Technology

Published: 2026-11-23

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

This paper proposes two procedures for informative frequency band (IFB) selection in vibration-based diagnosis of rotating machinery, with primary focus on rolling element bearings operating in the presence of heavy-tailed, non-Gaussian disturbances. The procedures, called nugram and nuhilgram, follow the filterbank-based decomposition scheme of classical kurtogram-type methods: the signal is decomposed into candidate frequency bands, each band is evaluated by a scalar criterion, and the most informative band is selected for envelope-spectrum analysis. The novelty lies in the band-selection criterion. Instead of kurtosis, entropy, an α -stable parameter, etc., the proposed methods use the estimated degrees-of-freedom parameter ν of the Student’s t -location-scale distribution. The two variants differ in signal representation: the nugram estimates ν from the envelope-domain representation. Low fitted values of ν are used as an empirical score for selecting candidate bands; the selected bands are subsequently assessed through their envelope spectra. Performance is assessed on simulated signals with Gaussian and non-Gaussian, impulsive noise, Monte Carlo experiments, and semi-synthetic industrial signals in which a simulated fault signature is superimposed on real crusher vibration data, as well as a record from an industrial gearbox. Results are compared with reference methods that share the same filterbank but represent the main families of band-selection criteria: the kurtogram (moment-based), the infogram and its logarithmic and geometric variants (entropy-based), and the alphagram (heavy-tail-parameter-based, the closest competitor to the proposed criterion). In the tested scenarios, the proposed criterion remained useful when non-cyclic disturbances masked the cyclic component. The nuhilgram provided the most consistent band-localization results among the two variants. Compared with the alphagram, the nuhilgram achieves comparable or superior diagnostic effectiveness at about one eighth of its computation time, in the tested computational setting.
