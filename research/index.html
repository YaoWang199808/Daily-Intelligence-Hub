Multidirectional hybrid pendulum-magnetic quasi-zero-stiffness isolator: modeling, analysis and experimental validation

Authors: Ruiqi Gao, Yang Xiao, Zhiyang Yuan, Zhiwei Huang, Rui Zhou, Jiulin Wu, Wei Jiang, Xuedong Chen

Institution: Huazhong University of Science and Technology, Shanghai Micro Electronics Equipment (China)

Published: 2026-10-20

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Multidirectional quasi-zero-stiffness vibration isolation remains challenging owing to the low dynamic stiffness, static stability, coupled rigid-body motion, and the weak linearity of conventional negative-stiffness mechanisms. To address these limitations, this paper proposes a hybrid pendulum-magnetic quasi-zero-stiffness (HPM-QZS) isolator combining a four-point flexible suspension with a centralized magnetic negative-stiffness (MNS) module. It employs separated positive–negative stiffness combinations in the horizontal and vertical directions, respectively. Horizontally, the pretensioned suspension provides positive pendulum stiffness, while the elevated payload mass center introduces an inverted-pendulum effect that reduces the effective horizontal stiffness through translation-rotation coupling. Vertically, the suspension springs act in parallel with the MNS module, whose force is transmitted to the moving frame through a compliant rod. A closed-form linearized six-degree-of-freedom model is derived from suspension-point kinematics and incremental force and moment relations. The model provides an analytical basis for stability, modal, and transmissibility analyses and guides suspension-parameter selection. In order to improve stiffness linearity within the working stroke, an unequal-size three-stator-two-mover MNS is further developed. In addition, static calibration and passive modal tests are conducted to confirm the accuracy of the analytical model. Finally, supplementary absolute-velocity feedback is introduced to suppress residual low-frequency resonance, and passive/active-assisted transmissibility tests demonstrate effective attenuation in all three translational directions. The experimental results demonstrate that the HPM-QZS isolator combines separate horizontal and vertical stiffness-regulation mechanisms, exhibits excellent stiffness linearity in the vertical direction, and offers superior vibration isolation performance, indicating its potential for low-frequency vibration isolation in fields such as precision manufacturing and metrology.

A single-sided inverse absolute nodal coordinate formulation for large-deformation shape sensing of slender structures

Authors: Ying Wang, Liang Ren, Gang Wang

Institution: CNPC Tubular Goods Research Institute (China), Dalian University of Technology

Published: 2026-10-20

Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing

Method: Hybrid

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Shape sensing reconstructs structural displacement fields from discrete strain measurements and is important for monitoring flexible slender structures. For large-deformation cases, existing inverse strain-based methods often require back-to-back measurements and prescribed sensor locations, limiting their use when only one surface is accessible or installation conditions are constrained. This paper proposes a single-sided strain-to-shape reconstruction framework for slender structures, termed SS-iANCF, based on inverse absolute nodal coordinate kinematics. Unilateral surface strain is converted into curvature information and embedded in a nonlinear least-squares inverse problem. An inextensibility penalty and an inter-element curvature-continuity constraint are introduced to stabilize reconstruction without opposite-side strain data. The influence of intra-element sampling location on accuracy and Hessian conditioning is also examined. Numerical simulations and experiments using a weak-FBG/OFDR sensing system confirm that SS-iANCF achieves high-fidelity reconstruction for both unidirectional large-deformation and reverse-bending configurations. Across the experimental cases, the mean absolute reconstruction error remains below 5 mm, with a maximum mean normalized displacement error of 1.22 × 10 −2 occurring in the reverse-bending case with curvature reversal.

Engineering Structures

Development and experimental verification of self-prestressing strengthening systems for cold-formed steel purlins using Fe-SMA strips

Authors: Hai-Tao Wang, Yu-Xiao Zhu, LI Zong-jing, Zhiqiang Dong, Han-ying Qi, Bitao Wu

Institution: Hohai University, Southeast University, East China Jiaotong University

Published: 2026-10-20

Journal: Engineering Structures

Method: Experimental

Citations: 0

Abstract:

Cold-formed steel purlins have been widely applied in the roof system of industrial buildings. With changes in roof functionality, many purlins require strengthening to improve their load-carrying capacity and stiffness. Prestressing is an effective strategy for enhancing the mechanical behavior of steel structures. However, conventional prestressing techniques typically rely on mechanical anchors and external tensioning devices. To overcome these limitations, practical self-prestressing strengthening systems for cold-formed steel purlins were developed using iron-based shape memory alloy (Fe-SMA) strips. The recovery stress behavior and anchorage methods, including bolted anchorage and adhesively bonded anchorage, were first examined. Subsequently, full-scale purlins consisting of two C-shaped cold-formed steel sections were strengthened and subjected to four-point bending tests under static loading. The effects of activation temperature and anchorage system on the strengthening effectiveness were investigated. Test results demonstrate that the recovery stress was largely insensitive to the initial pretensioning stress, but strongly affected by the activation temperature and the applied pre-strain. A recovery stress of about 380 MPa was generated under an activation temperature of 250 °C and a pre-strain level of 4%. Both the bolted and adhesively bonded anchorage systems proved to be effective, as they enabled the Fe-SMA strips to achieve tensile stresses of 680 MPa and 900 MPa, respectively, significantly exceeding the tensile stress demand required for strengthening cold-formed steel purlins. The flexural strengthening tests further indicated that flame-gun heating was a feasible activation method for practical on-site applications. Compared with non-prestressed strengthening, which resulted in only a 0.6% increase in yield load, prestressed Fe-SMA strengthening achieved enhancements of 9.4%-13.6%. Specimens using bolted and bonded methods exhibited comparable flexural performance, but the bonded anchorage system provided a slight advantage in improving the yield load and elastic stiffness.
